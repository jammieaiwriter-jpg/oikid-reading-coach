A Walk on the Bamboo Trail

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Leo: This bamboo trail is so quiet. I have never walked through a forest like this before.

Mia: Me neither! The bamboo trees are so tall, and the wind makes a nice sound when it moves through them.

Leo: Look, there is a small map at the entrance. It says the trail is two kilometers long and takes about forty minutes.

Mia: Forty minutes sounds perfect. Do you have enough water with you? It might get warm once the sun comes out.

Leo: Yes, I have already packed two bottles. My mom always tells me to bring extra water on a walk like this.

Mia: That's a good habit. Since we started walking together, I have learned so much about how to get ready for trips like this.

Leo: Look at the ground — some old bamboo lies here and makes an easy path for us to walk on.

Mia: It's beautiful. I already took ten photos, and we are only halfway through the trail!

Leo: Let's slow down a little. I want to remember this feeling, not just look at it through a phone screen.

Mia: Good idea. Since we have never visited a forest this green, we should just enjoy it while we are here.

Leo: Agreed. When we get to the end, let's check the map again and decide if we want to walk it once more.

Mia: Sounds like a plan. This might become one of my favorite places since we came to this town.

A Day at Grandpa's Orange Orchard

短文
語速：

Every winter, my grandpa's orange orchard turns bright with fruit, and this year my whole family finally had time to visit and help pick the fruit.

My grandfather has planted oranges on this hillside for many years, and he still knows every row of trees by heart.

When we came, my grandmother gave each of us a small basket and a pair of scissors, and she showed us how to cut the fruit without hurting the branch.

My little cousin had never picked an orange before, and she got so excited that she filled her basket in only ten minutes.

I picked a spot near the top of the hill, where the air was cool and the whole valley looked like a sea of orange dots.

By noon, we had already filled more than twenty baskets, and my grandfather said this was a very good harvest year for the family.

We took a break for lunch under a big tree, and my grandmother gave us fresh orange juice that was sweeter than anything from a store.

In the afternoon, my father showed me how to check each orange carefully before we packed them into boxes for the market.

By the time the sun went down, my arms were tired, but I had learned more about hard work and care than any book could teach me.

As we drove home, I looked back at the orchard, and I was proud that our family still owns this land and this tradition after so many years.

The Rice Harvest in the Taiwanese Countryside

短文
語速：

In many parts of Taiwan, farmers have planted rice for hundreds of years, and the rice harvest is still an important event in the countryside every year.

Rice is usually harvested twice a year in the south of Taiwan, once in early summer and again in autumn, because the warm weather lets the plants grow fast.

Before modern machines were common, whole families and neighbors worked together to cut the rice by hand, and children often helped carry water to the workers.

Today, most farmers use large harvesting machines that can cut and clean the rice grain in a single pass across the field.

Even with machines, the harvest season is still a busy and exciting time. Farmers watch the weather with care, since heavy rain can ruin a field in one day.

After the rice is harvested, it is left in the sun to dry before it goes to markets around the country.

Many small towns hold a harvest festival after the hardest work is done, with food, music, and games to thank the farmers for their hard work.

Visitors who travel to the countryside in autumn can often see golden rice growing all the way to the mountains, and many people enjoy taking photos there.

For local farmers, however, the harvest means much more than a nice picture. It shows months of hard work and hope for a good year ahead.

Why Campsites Sound So Different at Night

短文
語速：

Many families go camping in the mountains during the summer. During the day, a campsite is often full of noise: children play games, adults cook food, and cars move in and out of the parking area.

At night, the campsite becomes a very different place. Most people stop talking and go into their tents, so the loud human noise becomes quiet in about an hour.

Once it is quiet, campers can hear sounds that no one could hear during the day. Insects sing in the grass, an owl may call from a tall tree, and small animals move through the plants to find food.

Sound also carries more at night because the air near the ground is cooler and calmer than during the day. This is one simple reason why every small sound feels louder after dark.

Some new campers feel nervous when they hear a strange sound for the first time. A local guide often explains that most night sounds come from small, harmless animals, not from anything dangerous.

Learning to name a few common night sounds can turn fear into interest. A camper who knows the call of an owl or the song of a cricket can enjoy the night rather than fear it.

By the next morning, the campsite slowly wakes up again. Birds start singing, tent doors open one by one, and the noisy world of the campsite returns once more.

A Letter About the Kite Festival

信件
語速：

Dear Ben,

I want to tell you about the Kite Festival I went to last weekend. It happened on a big, open field outside the city, with a clear blue sky and a strong wind.

When we got there, hundreds of kites of every shape and color were already flying high above the field. Some looked like birds, some looked like fish, and one giant kite looked like a dragon.

My father and I took our own kite, a simple red one, and we worked for almost an hour to get it up in the air. It went down to the ground many times at first, but we did not give up.

Finally, a strong gust of wind lifted our kite high into the sky, and we both cheered so loudly that people near us turned to look. It was such a small moment, but it seemed like a big win.

Later, we walked around the field and watched a kite-flying contest. The judges gave points for height, shape, and how long each kite could stay in the air without falling.

Before we left, we bought a small paper kite from a stand near the entrance so I could try flying one at home. I hope you can join me next year; it is more fun than I can explain in a letter.

Your friend,

Ray

Rowing Across the Quiet Lake

短文
語速：

Last Saturday, Leo's family rented a small boat at a lake close to where their grandmother lived. His father was holding two oars, and Leo could not wait to try rowing by himself.

At first, the boat only turned in a circle because Leo pulled one oar much harder than the other. His little sister laughed so hard that she could hardly stay still in her seat.

Their father showed Leo how to pull both oars at the same time and just as hard. After trying two or three times, the boat finally went forward in a straight line.

The lake was calm and clear that morning, and Leo could see several small fish swim below the boat. A gentle breeze moved across the water, bringing the smell of fresh grass.

Halfway across the lake, they took a break near a small island to eat lunch. Leo's mother had packed sandwiches, fruit, and a bottle of cold tea for everyone to share.

On the way back, dark clouds started to cover the sky, so the family rowed faster than before. They reached the dock just as the first drops of rain started to fall.

That night, Leo was proud that he had learned something new. He said the whole story to his grandmother and said he wanted to row again the next time they visited the lake.

How Tomatoes Are Grown on a Small Balcony

短文
語速：

Many people think a garden needs a big yard, but tomatoes can be grown on a small balcony too. All that is needed is a large pot, some soil, water, and enough sunlight.

First, a pot with holes in the bottom is chosen so extra water can flow out easily. Good soil is added, and a few tomato seeds are placed a little below the top of the soil.

The pot is placed where it gets at least six hours of sunlight a day. Without enough light, the young plant will grow slowly and may never produce any tomatoes.

Water is given every day, but the soil should not become too wet. If the soil feels dry when touched, it is time to add water again.

After about two weeks, small green leaves appear. A thin piece of wood can be placed beside the plant to help its stem stay straight as it grows taller and heavier.

As the plant grows, small yellow flowers appear first. These flowers are slowly turned into tiny green tomatoes, which take more weeks to become big and red.

During this time, the plant should be checked often for tiny bugs. If any bugs are found, they should be removed by hand before they damage the leaves.

In about two or three months, the first ripe tomato can finally be picked and eaten. For a beginner, this simple balcony project is a great way to start growing food.

Leo: Got them. Be careful when you light this one — hold it away from your hair and sleeves.

Mia: Good idea. Should we call the power company to ask when the lights will be turned back on?

Leo: My phone battery is almost dead, so let's wait a little while. They are probably already fixing the problem.

Mia: This candlelight actually feels kind of nice and calm. It reminds me of the time we went camping last summer.

Leo: You're right. Let's just sit here and talk until the lights come back. It's not so bad after all.

Mia: Okay. If it takes too long, we can use the flashlight in the drawer instead of more candles.

Leo: Sounds good. I'm glad we have each other, even when the whole house suddenly goes dark.

My First Fried Egg

短文
語速：

Last Sunday morning, I asked my dad to teach me how to fry an egg. I had never cooked anything alone before, so I felt a little nervous but also excited.

First, a small pan was placed on the stove, and a little oil was poured into it. My dad told me to wait until the oil was hot before adding the egg.

I cracked the egg on the edge of the pan, just like my dad showed me. Most of it landed inside the pan, but a little bit dripped onto the counter.

"Don't worry," my dad said. "Every cook makes a mess the first time." He handed me a cloth, and the counter was cleaned quickly before the oil got too hot.

The egg started to sizzle, and white bubbles formed around the edges. My dad said the egg should not be moved too much, or it would break apart.

After about two minutes, the bottom of the egg turned golden brown. A thin spatula was used to flip it gently, and I held my breath the whole time.

The egg was flipped successfully, and it landed back in the pan without breaking. My dad smiled and said my first try looked better than his very first egg.

We ate the egg together with toast for breakfast. It was not perfect, but it tasted great because I had cooked it myself. I already want to try again next weekend.

Grandpa's Old Toolbox

短文
語速：

Every summer, I stay at my grandpa's house for a week. This Saturday, my bike chain falls off, so I ask him for help. He smiles and pulls a heavy wooden box out from under the stairs.

The box is old and a little dusty, but every tool inside has its own place. Grandpa says his own father gave him this toolbox many years ago, when he was still a young man.

I ask him how he learned to fix so many things by himself. He explains that nobody taught him with a manual. He just watched his father work and tried the same steps again and again.

While he fixes my chain, he asks me whether I want to learn too. I nod, and he shows me which tool to pick up first and how to hold it without hurting my fingers.

Inside the box, I find a small metal key that does not match any lock I know. I wonder what it is for, so I ask Grandpa quietly what he keeps it for all these years.

He laughs and explains that the key once opened his very first bicycle lock, from when he was ten years old. He never sells that bicycle, and he never throws the key away either.

After my chain is fixed, we ride our bikes slowly around the yard together. The evening light turns gold, and I feel proud that I finally know how to fix a bike chain myself.

That night, I ask Grandpa if I can keep one small screwdriver from the box. He says yes, and I understand then that this toolbox holds more than tools. It holds our whole family's story.

Every few weeks, Grandma carries her heavy quilt up to the rooftop to dry it in the sun. She says fresh sun and open air keep the quilt warm and free of a musty smell.

This time, I help her carry the quilt up the narrow stairs. We hang it over a long wooden pole, and Grandma shows me how to touch it with care so warm air can move through.

While we work, I ask her how she picks the day to dry the quilt. She explains that she checks the weather report first and picks a day with strong sun and no rain.

How to Sew a Button by Hand

短文
語速：

In many families, an older person often teaches a younger one how to sew a button back on a shirt. This small skill does not take long to learn, but it is still very useful today.

First, a person needs a needle, some strong thread, and of course a button. Many people ask which color of thread to choose, and the simple answer is to pick one that matches the shirt.

Next, the person pushes the needle up through one hole in the button and pulls the thread all the way through. Then the needle moves down through another hole, back into the cloth under it.

This up-and-down movement repeats about six or seven times for each pair of holes. A four-hole button usually needs a small cross or square pattern so that it does not come loose again.

Many older family members explain that a loose button is often the first sign a piece of clothing needs care. They say it is good to fix a small problem early, not to wait and lose the button later.

Some young people ask why this old skill still matters when new clothes are so cheap and easy to buy. However, sewing a single button takes only a few minutes and saves a shirt from the trash.

In some cultures, learning to sew a button is also a quiet way for family members to spend time together and share stories from the past while their hands stay busy with the needle and thread.

So the next time a button falls off your favorite shirt, why not ask someone at home to teach you? It is a small skill, but it can be useful for your whole life.

The Warmest Seat at the Reunion Dinner

短文
語速：

Every year on the night before Lunar New Year, my whole family gathers at my grandmother's house for a big reunion dinner. The kitchen smells wonderful hours before anyone sits down to eat.

This year, more than twenty people crowd around two long tables. My cousins run between the rooms, and my uncles carry extra chairs in from the yard so everyone has a seat.

My grandmother always saves the seat closest to the stove for whoever arrives first. This year, I arrive early, so she smiles and tells me to sit right beside her.

The dinner has many dishes, but the fish is the most important one. Nobody finishes the whole fish, because leaving a little food means our family will have more than enough next year.

After dinner, the children get red envelopes from the older relatives. My little cousin counts hers three times and then hides it carefully inside her pocket.

Later, we all watch a variety show on television together, and my grandfather tells the same funny story he tells every single year. Everyone laughs anyway.

Close to midnight, my family walks outside to watch fireworks light up the sky above our neighborhood. The cold air feels sharp, but nobody wants to go back inside yet.

When I finally go to bed, the house is quiet, but I still feel the warmth of the crowded table. That warm feeling is what Lunar New Year always means to me.

How to Pack a Moving Box the Smart Way

短文
語速：

Moving to a new home can feel exciting, but packing boxes the right way saves time and trouble. A few simple rules can keep your things safe during the move.

First, choose boxes that are strong and about the same size. A box that is too big becomes too heavy to carry, even if it does not look full yet.

Next, put heavy items like books at the bottom of the box and lighter items like clothes on top. This keeps the box balanced and stops things from breaking inside.

Fragile items such as cups and glasses need extra care. You can wrap a piece of paper around each one before you put it in the box for safety.

People who move boxes as their job also suggest you put the room name on every box, such as kitchen or bedroom. This small step saves confusion later.

Some people number their boxes and keep a short list of what is inside each one. If a box gets lost, the list makes it easy to notice right away.

Tape should go across the top and bottom of every box in an X shape. A box with tape this way is much stronger and less likely to open during the move.

Packing takes patience, but a small plan at the start makes moving day much easier. Most people agree that a box packed with care makes the whole move better.

Why Families Love Movie Night

短文
語速：

In many homes around the world, one night each week is special. Parents and children turn off their phones, sit together on the sofa, and watch a movie as a family. People call this custom "movie night."

Movie night usually starts the same way. Someone picks a film that everyone can enjoy, from an old classic to a new story. The family makes popcorn or another simple snack and turns down the lights.

For many families, the movie itself is not the most important part. The real value is the time together. During a busy week, movie night gives everyone a chance to sit close and share a quiet hour.

Some families use movie night to talk about the story after it ends. A funny scene can make everyone laugh, and a sad scene can open a talk about feelings. Children often remember these talks for years.

Movie night also helps children learn from stories. A film about kindness or hard work can teach a lesson without a lecture. Kids watch how characters solve problems and make choices in different situations.

Doctors and teachers agree that simple family time like this is good for children. It builds trust and makes home feel warm and safe. That is why so many families around the world keep this weekly custom alive.

The Faucet That Would Not Stop

短文
語速：

One Saturday morning, I wake up and hear a strange sound in the kitchen. Drip, drip, drip. There is a leak at the faucet by the sink, and water keeps falling into a small puddle on the counter.

My father wants to fix it first. He turns the handle again and again, but the leak does not stop. He looks under the sink and sees an old part that is worn and needs a new one.

We drive to a small shop downtown that sells tools and parts for the house. The owner is a kind man who listens to my father and gives us the right part that our old faucet needs.

Back home, my father puts an old towel on the counter and turns off the water first. Then, with care, he takes out the old part and puts in the new one. I hold the flashlight so he can see well.

It takes almost an hour, but finally my father turns the water back on. We both watch the faucet with care. No leak. No sound at all. The kitchen is quiet again, and we both feel very happy.

That night, my father says that fixing small problems early can save much trouble later. I learn that it is important to notice a small leak early, and that patience really does help in the end.
