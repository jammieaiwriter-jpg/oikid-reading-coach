Reading Passage Shadowing
How the Plague Reached Europe

The Black Death, which struck Europe from 1347 to 1350, was a plague that had no cure and was highly contagious.

Historians surmised that the Black Death may have originated from Asia, where it started among animal and then spread to humans through fleas and dying rats.

The disease then travelled west to Europe through trading ships that journeyed across the Black Sea.

When the ship first arrived, the people at the dock were shocked to see that nearly all the sailors were dead, and those still living were barely alive.

Symptoms and Spread

Men, women and children died of the disease, and those who were infected suffered from damage in their vital organs.

High fever, vomiting, swelling, diarrhea, and feeling delirious from the pain were just some of the symptoms of the plague.

Mysterious black spots that oozed blood and pus also covered the body of those infected, which gave the disease its name - Black Death.

While the ships were quickly ordered to leave the harbor, the plague had already started to spread.

Impact on Europe

It was estimated that millions of people - about one third of Europe's population - were killed by the Black Death over the next few years.

In Paris, France, roughly 800 people died every day.

The number of deaths was so massive that a burial was impossible.

Dead bodies were dumped into large pits.

In some cases, the population in certain towns or villages declined so rapidly that it seemed to be deserted.
