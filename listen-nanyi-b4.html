The Small Hardware Store on the Corner

短文
語速：

On the corner near my house, there is a small hardware store. It is smaller than the big stores in the mall, but it has almost everything a family needs to fix things at home.

The owner, Mr. Chen, knows every shelf by heart. If you ask for a small screw or a long rope, he finds it faster than any computer can.

Inside the store, there are hundreds of little boxes. Some boxes are full of nails, and some are full of keys. The store looks messy, but Mr. Chen says it is more organized than it looks.

Many people come to the store for simple help, not just to buy things. An old man once asked Mr. Chen to fix his broken umbrella, and Mr. Chen did it for free.

The store also sells paint, rope, tape, and small tools. On weekends, more customers visit because they want to fix things around the house by themselves.

Mr. Chen's store does not have a big sign or bright lights. It is quieter than the shops next to it, but neighbors say it is the most helpful shop on the whole street.

Big chain stores sell tools too, but they cannot give the same friendly advice. That is why this small hardware store on the corner stays busy every single day.

A Quiet Morning at the Soy Milk Shop

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Owner: Good morning! You are here before most customers today.

Nina: I know! I got up early, so I came by myself before school.

Owner: Well, welcome. What would you like this morning, hot soy milk or cold?

Nina: Hot, please. It is colder outside than yesterday, so I want something warm.

Owner: Good choice. Do you want a sandwich too? Today's egg sandwich is fresher than the one from yesterday.

Nina: Yes, please, and can I also get a small bag of youtiao?

Owner: Sure thing. Are you meeting someone here this morning, or are you eating by yourself?

Nina: My brother is meeting me here. He says he is always slower than me, so I always wait.

Owner: Ha, that sounds like my son. He tells himself every night that he will wake early, but he never does!

Nina: Maybe he should ask my brother for advice. Oh, there he is now — right on time today!

Owner: Good morning! Whose bag is this on the counter, yours or hers?

Brother: It's mine. I packed my books by myself last night so I would not forget them.

Owner: Smart! Here is your soy milk, sandwich, and youtiao. Have a great day at school, both of you.

Nina: Thank you! This is the quietest and warmest way to start a school morning.

Picture Day for Our Yearbook

短文
語速：

Today is picture day for our yearbook. Our whole class stands outside near the school garden. The photographer says we must stand still and smile at the same time.

Our teacher tells us where to stand. Taller students stand at the back, and shorter students stand at the front. I am taller than my best friend, so I stand right behind her.

Before the group photo, some students want a photo by themselves. My classmate Leo takes a photo of himself next to the old school gate. He says it is his favorite spot in the whole school.

A girl asks, "Whose bag is this on the ground?" Nobody answers, so she puts it under a bench. We laugh because it looks funny sitting there all alone.

The photographer takes many photos. Some are serious, and some are funny. My friend Sara makes the funniest face of all, and everyone laughs so hard that we take that photo three times.

After the big group photo, we take smaller photos with our clubs. My club friends and I stand close together, and we try to look our best for the camera.

When we finish, the photographer shows us a few photos on his screen. This year's photo looks better than last year's photo. We are all excited to see our yearbook in June.

A Letter About Our Old Photos

信件
語速：

Dear Grandpa,

How are you? Last weekend, Mom and I went to Sunshine Photo Studio near our house. We brought all your old photos in a big box.

The photo studio worker was very kind. She looked at each photo one by one and said some of them were a little old and not clear, but she could still print new copies.

She used a machine to clean the old pictures on a screen first. Then she printed a new copy of each picture on nice paper. We waited about one hour for all of them.

The new photos look clearer than before, and the colors are brighter too! Mom says the new copies look even nicer than the old ones now.

My favorite one is the photo of you and Grandma at the village square. You are smiling so wide in that picture! I want to put it on my desk at school.

Next time, I want to bring the photos from your wedding, too. I really want to see them and hear the story about that day.

Love, Ivy

Grandma's Handmade Soap Shop

短文
語速：

Every summer, I visit my grandma. She has a small soap shop in the old town. The shop is tiny, but it always smells like flowers and fresh plants. I love to help her after breakfast.

My grandma makes soap by hand. She mixes oil, water, and a special herb in a big pot. Then she puts the warm liquid into a small mold and waits for it to become hard.

Today, she makes lavender soap. The purple flowers smell sweet and nice. My grandma says lavender can help people rest and sleep well at night.

I help her cut the finished soap into small pieces. Each piece is the same size, but some are round and some are square. My favorite shape is the round one.

A young man called Ken comes into the shop. He wants a birthday gift for his mother. My grandma shows him three kinds of soap: lavender, lemon, and honey.

Ken smells each piece and thinks for a while. He says the honey soap smells the most delicious, but the lavender soap looks the most beautiful. In the end, he buys both.

After Ken leaves, my grandma smiles at me. "A good soap shop needs someone to help," she says. I smile back and start to wrap more soap for the next person.

When I look around the little shop again, it still smells like flowers and fresh plants. I know I will always love this small, sweet-smelling place.

The Small Tailor Shops in Our Town

短文
語速：

In many small towns, there is often a little tailor shop on a quiet street. People bring clothes that are too long, too big, or a little broken, and the tailor makes them fit again.

One common job for a tailor is fixing pants that are too long. The tailor cuts a little cloth from the bottom and makes the ends clean again.

A boy called Hank once brings his new school pants to a tailor shop. The pants are too long for him, so the tailor uses a tape to check his legs.

The tailor puts a few pins in the extra cloth first. Then she uses a sewing machine to make a straight, strong line. It only takes about fifteen minutes to finish the job.

Tailors also work with a needle and thread by hand for small jobs, like fixing a button or a small hole. This kind of work needs much time and skill.

Many families visit the same tailor shop for years. They trust the tailor because the work is always clean, and the price is usually fair for everyone in town.

Today, big stores sell many clothes, but small tailor shops are still important. They help people save money and use their clothes for a longer time. They do not need to buy new clothes right away.

The next time your pants are too long, think about the small tailor shop near you. What a helpful little shop it is for the whole town!

A Flat Tire on the Way to School

短文
語速：

One morning, Leo's bike tire suddenly loses all its air on his way to school. He is already a little late, so he feels nervous and pushes his bike toward the small bike shop near the corner.

The shop owner, a friendly old man, looks at the tire and soon sees a tiny piece of glass inside it. He says this kind of problem happens a lot near the busy road.

While Leo waits, he watches the man take off the wheel, pull out the tube, and check it for small holes. The man works fast, but every step still looks neat and safe.

After he finds the small hole, the man patches it with a thin piece of rubber and puts everything back together again. He also checks the brakes and adds a little air to both tires for free.

In just fifteen minutes, the bike is ready again. Leo thanks the man and gives him a small fee for the repair. He feels lucky that this shop is so close to his school.

On his way out, Leo says he will bring another bike next week, the one his younger sister rides, because its chain always comes off. The shop owner is happy and says he will gladly help.

What Does Uncle Ben Really Do?

短文
語速：

Many people on our street know Uncle Ben. He runs a small water-and-electric shop on the corner, and he fixes water pipes, lights, and small machines for the families near our street.

A plumber like Uncle Ben checks pipes under the sink or behind the wall. Water sometimes leaks from an old pipe, and if nobody stops it, this can slowly damage the floor or the wall.

Uncle Ben also works with electricity. He checks the power lines and lights in a house, and he always turns off the power first, because electricity can be very dangerous if a person is not careful.

Every morning, Uncle Ben packs a big toolbox with items like tape, small tools, and extra parts. He never knows what kind of problem is waiting for him that day, so he must be ready for anything.

This kind of work is not easy. A good worker like him needs patience, and he needs to understand how water and electricity move through a whole building, from the roof down to the ground floor.

Uncle Ben says his favorite part of the job is helping neighbors fast when something breaks. Without workers like him, many homes would not have clean water or safe electricity every single day.

The Gua Bao Stall by the Night Market Gate

短文
語速：

Leo's grandmother runs a small Gua Bao stall near the night market gate. She opens the stall every evening at five o'clock and closes when there are no buns left.

A Gua Bao is a warm steamed bun. Grandmother fills it with warm pork, sour vegetables, and peanuts. Many customers say the bun smells wonderful from far away.

Leo helps his grandmother after school. He washes the bowls and passes the warm buns to customers. His favorite job is sprinkling sweet peanut powder on top of the pork.

Every day, several regular customers visit the stall. An old man always orders two buns and a cup of hot tea. He sits on a small stool and talks with Grandmother.

Last Friday, it started to rain while Grandmother was cooking. Leo got an umbrella right away and put it over the pot so the small fire would not go out.

Grandmother said with a smile, You are such a great helper today. Leo was proud of himself, and he loves working at the stall, even when the night market gets busy.

On weekends, the line outside the stall grows very long. Grandmother works fast with skillful hands, but she never forgets to thank every single customer with a smile.

Leo hopes to learn Grandmother's recipe one day. He wants to keep the small stall open by the gate for many more years, just the way it is right now.

A Letter About the Old Record Shop

信件
語速：

Dear Nora,

How are you? I want to tell you about a small record shop I found near my house.

Every Saturday afternoon, I walk to the shop and look through boxes of old records. The owner, an old man named Mr. Chen, knows every song in the shop by heart.

Last week, he showed me a record from many years ago. He said his father gave it to him when he was young, and he still plays it every quarter, on the first day of each new season.

The shop is small and a little dark, but it feels warm and friendly. Soft music always plays in the background while customers look through the shelves.

I asked Mr. Chen why he loves old records so much. He said new music is popular now, but old records tell a story that many young people never hear.

Yesterday, I bought my very first record with my own money. I felt so excited! I can't wait to play it at home this weekend.

Do you like music like this? I hope you can visit the record shop with me next time you come to my city.

Your friend,

Leo

Red Bean Cakes: A Popular Street Snack

短文
語速：

If you walk down a street in Taiwan, you may smell something sweet and warm. That smell often comes from a red bean cake stand, a popular street snack that many people enjoy.

A red bean cake is small and round, a little like a thick pancake. Workers pour batter into a special iron machine with many round holes, then add filling in the middle.

The most common filling is sweet red bean paste, but stands today offer several other choices. Some people like taro, some like custard, and children often choose chocolate.

Making a good red bean cake takes a skillful worker. The machine gets very hot, so the worker must watch the time carefully and turn each cake at the right moment.

Many stands open in the afternoon and stay open until the evening. Students often stop by after school, and families visit on their way home from work.

Each cake usually costs between fifteen and twenty-five dollars, so it makes an easy afternoon treat. A hot cake in cold weather can make anyone feel warm and happy.

This snack became popular many years ago and is still loved today. It connects old cooking methods with new flavors that young people enjoy.

That is why this small, simple snack still brings a long line of happy customers to street stands all across the city.

Busy Morning at the Flower Shop

短文
語速：

Last Sunday was Mother's Day, and the small flower shop on Park Street was busier than usual. Some customers wanted roses, but others only wanted a small plant for the kitchen table.

The shop owner, Mr. Lin, worked with his son all morning. If a customer could not choose a color, Mr. Lin always showed them three or four different flowers.

One woman asked for pink roses, and another asked for the same flowers in yellow. Both women left with a smile and a small card to write a message inside.

Although the shop was small, it never felt empty. Each hour, more people walked through the door. Some carried empty boxes, and others carried nothing at all.

Around noon, an old man came in. He said his wife liked simple flowers more than expensive ones. Despite the busy shop, Mr. Lin's son found time to help him pick the perfect flowers.

By evening, almost every flower was gone. Anyone who came after five o'clock could only choose from a few plants near the door. Even though there were not many flowers left, no one left angry.

Mr. Lin's son said he was tired but happy. He said helping people find the right gift for their mother made the whole day feel worth it.

How a Locksmith Opens a Lock

短文
語速：

A locksmith is a person who fixes locks and helps people who are locked out of their homes or cars. Anyone can lose a key, so a locksmith's job is always useful.

First, a locksmith looks at the lock carefully. Some locks are old and simple. Others are new and difficult to open. A locksmith must know both types well.

If the key is lost, the locksmith often uses a thin metal tool. He moves the tool inside the lock until he feels a small click. Although this looks easy on TV, it can take real skill and patience.

Every locksmith carries a bag of different tools. Each tool has its own shape and size, because different locks need different tools. Some jobs take only a minute, but others take much longer.

Despite the hard work, a good locksmith stays calm and careful. If he moves too fast, he might break the lock. So he works slowly and checks his work at each step.

After the lock opens, the locksmith usually gives some advice. He might say, "You should make a copy of your key," or "You should change this old lock soon."

Even though people often forget about locksmiths, their work keeps homes and cars safe every day. Anyone who has ever been locked out knows how helpful this job can be.

Buying Band-Aids at the Pharmacy

Kevin: Excuse me, do you have any band-aids here?

Pharmacist: Yes, we have a few kinds. Some are small for a finger, and others are big for a knee or an arm.

Pharmacist: I'm sorry to hear that. If the cut is deep, you should also clean it with water first.

Kevin: It's not too deep, but it's a little sharp near the edge.

Pharmacist: Then this bigger box should work well. Each band-aid inside can cover a wide cut.

Kevin: Great. Do I need anything else, like medicine?

Pharmacist: Although a small cut usually heals by itself, you can also buy this cream if you want.

Kevin: Does the cream have a strange smell?

Pharmacist: Not really. Most people say it smells like nothing at all.

Kevin: OK, I'll take both the band-aids and the cream, please.

Pharmacist: Sure. Despite the small cut, please still rest today and change the band-aid tomorrow.

Kevin: I will. Thank you for your help!

Pharmacist: You're welcome. Take care of yourself, and come back if it still hurts.

The Bakery's Fresh Bread Time

短文
語速：

Every morning before school, I walk past Mr. Chen's small bakery on the corner of my street. At about seven o'clock, warm bread comes out of the big oven, and the whole street smells wonderful.

Mr. Chen has run this bakery for twenty years. He gets up before dawn to make the dough, and by six thirty, the shop is full of golden loaves and sweet rolls.

Today, I stopped and watched him take a fresh loaf out of the oven. Steam rose from the bread, and he smiled and said, "Careful, it's still hot!"

A line of customers was already waiting outside. An old woman said she buys bread here every single morning because it tastes better than bread from the supermarket.

Mr. Chen's daughter helps him after school. She puts the bread into paper bags and writes the price on each one with a black pen.

I asked Mr. Chen why he never gets tired of making bread. He said that baking makes him happy, and he loves to see people smile when they eat his warm bread.

Before I left, he gave me a small piece of bread for free. It was soft, warm, and a little sweet, and I ate it all the way to school.

Now, every morning, I hope to smell that warm bread again. Next year, I want to learn how to bake my own loaf with Mr. Chen.

How Fruit Shops Peel Pineapples

短文
語速：

Taiwan has many small fruit shops that sell fresh pineapples every summer. At many of these shops, workers use a long, sharp knife to peel a whole pineapple in under one minute.

First, the worker cuts off the top and the bottom of the pineapple. Then he turns the knife around the fruit in a circle to remove the thick yellow skin.

After that, he uses a small tool to take out the little brown eyes that are still stuck in the fruit. This step needs a careful hand and a lot of practice.

Some shops even use a special machine that can peel and cut a pineapple into a spiral shape in just a few seconds. Customers love to watch this quick, clever trick.

Once the pineapple is ready, the worker puts it in a clean bag and gives it to the customer. Many people eat it right there on the street.

A fresh Taiwanese pineapple is sweet, a little sour, and full of juice. On a hot summer day, it is one of the most popular fruits at the market.

Many fruit sellers say that peeling pineapples all day is hard work, but they feel proud that so many customers trust their skill and their fresh fruit.

This little street service shows a fun part of local culture. Next time you visit a night market, try one of these fresh, hand-peeled pineapples yourself.

A Letter About My Grandfather's Grain Shop

信件
語速：

Dear Mia,

How are you? I want to tell you about my weekend job at my grandfather's grain shop.

Every Saturday morning, I help my grandfather measure rice for his customers. His shop is small, but it is full of big bags of rice and other grain.

First, a customer tells us how much rice they want. Then my grandfather puts the rice on an old metal scale to find the exact amount.

After that, I carry the rice into a clean bag and write the weight in kilograms on the front. It sounds simple, but it needs a careful hand.

My grandfather has done this traditional job for almost forty years. He says a good shop owner must always be honest about the amount and the price.

Yesterday, I checked a whole bag by myself for the first time. My grandfather was so proud, and so was I.

The shop work can be hard, but I really enjoy learning from my grandfather. I hope you can visit his grain shop with me one day.

Your friend,

Zoe
