A Morning at the Bakery

短文
語速：

Nick is twelve years old, and every Saturday morning, he helps at his uncle's small bakery. He wakes up at five o'clock, before the sun is up. The streets are quiet, but the bakery is already warm and busy.

First, Nick washes his hands and puts on a white apron. His uncle mixes flour, water, and yeast in a big bowl. "Watch carefully," his uncle says. "A good baker needs patient hands."

Nick learns to shape the dough into small, round balls. It feels a little sticky. Some balls are not perfect, but his uncle smiles. "Your bread will get good every week," he says.

At six o'clock, the bread goes into the hot oven. Nick waits and watches through the small glass window. Slowly, the dough rises and turns a golden color. The whole shop smells sweet and warm.

When the timer rings, Nick's uncle takes out trays of fresh bread. Nick carries them to the front table. Customers are already waiting outside the door, ready to buy warm bread for breakfast.

An old woman buys three loaves for her family. A little girl points at a chocolate roll and smiles. Nick likes seeing happy customers. It makes him feel good about the early morning.

By nine o'clock, most of the bread is sold. Nick is tired, but he is proud, too. He made real bread today, not just watched. "Can I come again next Saturday?" he asks his uncle.

His uncle laughs and pats his shoulder. "Of course. Next time, you can try making the rolls yourself." Nick smiles all the way home. Being an apprentice at the bakery is hard, but he loves it.

A Day in the Life of a Honeybee

短文
語速：

Honeybees are small insects, but they are very busy. A worker bee's day is short and full of work. Let's look at how one bee spends her day.

Early in the morning, the bee leaves the hive. She flies from flower to flower, looking for nectar and pollen. Her body is covered with tiny hairs that catch the yellow pollen.

The bee has a long tongue. She uses it to drink sweet nectar from inside each flower. She stores the nectar in a special part of her body.

After visiting many flowers, the bee flies back to the hive. Inside, other bees take the nectar from her. They turn it into honey and store it in small wax rooms.

Bees also "talk" to each other with a special dance. When a bee finds good flowers, she does a dance to show the other bees the direction and the distance.

A worker bee visits about a hundred flowers on just one trip. In her whole life, she may visit thousands of flowers, but she makes only a small spoon of honey.

Bees are important for nature. When they move from flower to flower, they carry pollen, too. This helps plants grow fruit and seeds. Without bees, farms would have much less food.

A worker bee's life is short, only about six weeks. But every day, she works hard for her hive. The next time you see a bee, remember how busy her small life is.

Same Family, Different Days

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Rosa: Vera, you get up so early every morning! I don't understand it.

Vera: I like to run before school. I run every day at six o'clock. It makes me feel awake.

Rosa: Six o'clock? That's too early for me! I get up at seven thirty.

Vera: What do you do after you wake up?

Rosa: I read a picture book for twenty minutes. Then I eat a big breakfast.

Vera: I only eat bread and eggs. I don't have much time before school.

Rosa: After school, what do you do?

Vera: I go to swim practice with my team. I swim for one hour, three days a week.

Rosa: I don't like the water much. I paint pictures in the art room instead.

Vera: That sounds nice and calm! Do you paint every day?

Rosa: Not every day. Only on Mondays and Thursdays. On other days, I play the piano.

Vera: We are twins, but we are so different!

Rosa: I know! You love sports, and I love art. But we both love pizza on Fridays!

Vera: That's true! Let's go eat pizza together tonight.

Rosa: Sounds perfect. Let's go!

Why Junior High Students Need More Sleep

短文
語速：

Doctors say junior high students need about nine hours of sleep every night. But a new report says most students in Taiwan usually sleep less than seven hours.

Why do students sleep so little? Many students go to cram school after dinner, and they often do not get home until nine or ten at night.

After that, they must finish their homework. Some students say they are so busy that they seldom go to bed before midnight, even on school nights.

A tired body and a tired mind make school harder, not easier. Doctors say students who do not sleep enough often feel sleepy during class the next day.

They also have more trouble with memory. It is hard to remember new words or math steps when the brain is too tired to work well.

Some schools now try to help. A few schools always start class a little later in the morning, so students can sleep a bit more.

Doctors give simple advice: turn off computers and phones early, and never study right before bed. A short walk or a warm shower can help students relax.

Sleep is not a waste of time. It is part of a healthy student life, and it always helps students learn better the next day.

A Letter to My Pen Pal About My Daily Habits

信件
語速：

Dear Ryan,

Thank you for your last letter! It is always fun to have a pal in another country. You asked about my daily habits, so let me tell you about a school day.

I always get up at six thirty and eat breakfast with my family. After that, I walk to school with my neighbor.

During the day, I have six classes and a short lunch break. I usually eat lunch with my classmates in our classroom.

After school, I go to cram school twice a week, on Tuesday and Thursday. On the other days, I often do my homework at the library.

I never skip exercise. I play basketball once a week with my brother, and we always feel great after a game.

I seldom stay up late, because I get tired easily. I usually take a shower and go to bed before ten thirty.

What about you? Do you have a busy school life, too? I hope you can visit Taiwan and see my daily life for yourself one day.

Your friend,

Kate

A Riverside Morning Run

短文
語速：

Leo lives near a long path by the river. Every morning before school, he puts on his shoes and goes for a run along the water.

He usually starts at six o'clock, when the sky is still a little dark. The morning air feels fresh and cool on his face.

Leo always does a short stretch first. Then he runs past the old bridge, a small park, and a row of tall trees by the river.

Some mornings, he sees other people out for a run or walking their dogs on the same path. They often smile and say hello.

Leo seldom skips his run, even on cold days. He says a short run makes him feel awake and ready for a busy school day.

After about thirty minutes, he walks home slowly to cool down. He never forgets to drink water and eat a good breakfast before school.

Twice a month, his father joins him on the weekend. They run side by side and talk about school, friends, and small daily things.

For Leo, the morning run by the river is not just exercise. It is a quiet hour that belongs only to him before a noisy day begins.

Buying a Reference Book

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Kate: Excuse me, sir. I want a math reference book for seventh grade. Which one is good for a student like me?

Clerk: We have three kinds here. This bright green one is easy. That dark blue one is harder, with more practice pages.

Kate: How many pages does the green one have?

Clerk: About two hundred pages. It also comes with an answer key at the back.

Kate: Great. How much is it?

Clerk: It is three hundred and fifty dollars. If you buy two books together, the second one is cheaper.

Kate: I also need an English reference book. Do you have any for seventh grade, too?

Clerk: Yes, we have a few. Which topic do you want, grammar or reading?

Kate: I want one that has both grammar and reading practice.

Clerk: Then this one is perfect. It has grammar in the first part and reading in the second part.

Kate: How much are the two books together?

Clerk: Let me check the balance… six hundred dollars for both. I can also give you a free notebook.

Kate: That sounds like a good deal! I will take both books, please.

Clerk: Thank you! Do you want a bag, or do you want to carry them together in your arms?

Kate: A bag, please. My arms are already full of snacks from the shop next door!

Clerk: No problem. Here you are. Good luck with your studies, and come back anytime.

How Our Class Money Works

短文
語速：

Many classes in Taiwan have a small amount of money called "class money." Every student in the class gives a little money at the start of the school year. A class leader collects it and keeps a careful record.

How much money does each student give? It is usually not a big amount, maybe one hundred or two hundred dollars. Some classes collect it one time. Other classes collect a smaller amount every month instead.

Who takes care of the class money? Most classes choose one student to be the money helper. This student writes down every dollar that comes in and every dollar that goes out in a small book.

How does a class use this money? Many classes use it to buy things for the classroom, like flowers, art paper, or a new clock. Some classes save it for a class party near the end of the year.

Which things do students like to buy the most? A survey of many classes shows that snacks and drinks are the most popular choice. Cake, juice, and sandwiches often show up at class parties.

Sometimes a class does not spend all of its money. The money helper gives the leftover dollars back to every student, or the class saves it for next year's activities and trips.

At the end of the year, the money helper shows the whole class the full record. Everyone can see how much came in and how each dollar was spent, so it feels fair to all the students.

Class money teaches students an important skill: working together to plan and spend money wisely. Does your class have a money helper, too? Maybe your class can try this simple system next term.

The Ticket Booth at the School Fair

短文
語速：

Our school has a big fair every October. This year, I work at the ticket booth with my friend Nora. We sit at a small table near the school gate and sell colorful paper tickets to everyone who comes.

Each ticket costs ten dollars. Some students want just one ticket. Other students buy five or six tickets for their whole family. Nora writes each number down in a small notebook so we do not make a mistake.

A boy comes to our table. He asks, "Which color ticket can I use for the game booth?" I show him the yellow tickets and say, "These ones are for games. The blue ones are for food."

How many tickets does he want? He wants four yellow ones and two blue ones. He gives us sixty dollars. Nora counts the money twice to make sure the number is correct.

By eleven o'clock, a long line forms in front of our booth. Some kids are thirsty and tired after standing in the sun, so we give out free cups of water. Everyone waits with a smile.

Near lunchtime, our teacher brings us hamburgers and cold juice. We eat quickly and go back to work. Selling tickets is more fun than I thought it would be at the start of the day.

By the end of the day, we sell almost all of our tickets. Only a few yellow ones are left in the box. Nora and I count the money one more time before we close the booth.

I feel proud of our little booth. Next year, I want to work at the ticket booth again, and maybe sell even more tickets for our school fair.

Clerk: Great choice! I will place it in a bag with water for you.

Mia: Thank you so much! What a great afternoon at the aquarium shop!

A Day at the Animal Shelter

短文
語速：

An animal shelter is a place for dogs and cats that do not have a home. Every day, many people work there. They are called volunteers, and they help the animals in many ways.

In the morning, volunteers usually clean the cages first. They also give food and water to every animal. Some dogs need a walk outside, so a volunteer takes them for a short walk.

How many animals live in a shelter at one time? It is different at every shelter, but some places have more than fifty dogs and cats. New animals come in, and some go to new homes.

Why do people bring animals to a shelter? Sometimes a family cannot keep a pet anymore, so they bring it here. Sometimes people find a stray animal on the street and bring it to the shelter, too.

In the afternoon, visitors can come and see the animals. If a family wants to adopt a pet, a volunteer helps them choose the right one. This is often the happiest part of the day.

At night, the shelter is quiet again. The volunteers go home, but they know they help many animals every day. What a busy but happy place an animal shelter is!

Grandpa's Old Radio

短文
語速：

Grandpa keeps an old radio on a small shelf in his room. He bought it a long time ago, and it still works well today.

Every evening, Grandpa turns on the radio and listens to music or the news. He likes old songs the most, and he often sings along quietly.

The radio does not look like a modern radio. It has a big brown box, two round buttons, and a long silver antenna. Many young people do not know a radio like this.

Grandpa says the radio brings back good memories of his childhood. He remembers his own father listening to the news on a radio like this one.

Sometimes, the radio makes a strange sound, but Grandpa never wants a new one. He believes the old radio still has its own special voice.

My cousins and I usually visit Grandpa on the weekend. We sit near him and listen to the old radio together. At first, the sound seems slow and simple to us.

But after a while, we start to enjoy it too. Grandpa smiles and tells us short stories about each song. Those quiet evenings feel warm and special to all of us.

For Grandpa, the old radio is more than just a machine. It is a small piece of his past, and he plans to keep it for a long time.

The Morning After the Typhoon

短文
語速：

Last Saturday, a big typhoon came to our town. It rained hard, and the storm lasted all night. My little sister was afraid, so we stayed together in the front room.

On Sunday morning, the sky turned blue again. My father and I walked outside to look at our yard. We saw a big tree branch on the ground next to the gate.

We saw wet leaves and small branches all over the street. A bike from next door was on the ground near our house, so my father picked it up and put it by the wall.

My mother cleaned the water off the porch with a broom. I helped her collect the wet leaves and put them in a big bag. It was hard work, but we finished before lunch.

Some shops near our house opened late that morning. The shop owners cleaned their windows and floors first. Everyone on our street worked together and helped each other.

By noon, our street looked clean and bright again. My father said the typhoon was strong, but our family and neighbors were even stronger. What a busy and special morning it was!

School Life in Early Taiwan

短文
語速：

Many years ago, school life in Taiwan was very different from today. Students walked or rode bikes to school because there were not many cars. Some students walked for almost an hour every morning.

Classrooms did not have air conditioners. In summer, students used paper fans to stay cool. In winter, some schools did not have heaters, so students wore many warm clothes to class.

Students did not have computers or the Internet. They wrote with pencils and used real books to study. Teachers wrote on a blackboard, and students copied the notes into their notebooks.

Lunch was also different. Many students brought a metal lunch box from home. A helper heated all the lunch boxes together in a big steamer before lunchtime.

After school, students did not have as much homework as today. Many children played outside with friends, climbed trees, or helped their parents at a small family shop or on a farm.

Sports days were a big event at school. The whole school ran races and played games together. Families came to watch and cheer for their children.

Even without modern things, students still learned a lot and had fun with their friends. Life was simpler, but school was still full of happy memories for many people.

Today, when a grandmother or a grandfather tells these stories, young people are often surprised. It is hard to imagine a school day without computers, but it was normal for students back then.

Strange Noises at Midnight

短文
語速：

Last night, I woke up at midnight. I heard a strange noise outside my window. It sounded like someone was walking on the roof. My heart beat fast, and I did not move.

I did not want to wake my parents, so I got out of bed alone. I walked to the window very slowly. The noise stopped, but then it started again. It was louder this time.

I picked up my flashlight and opened my door a little. The hallway was dark and quiet. I took a deep breath and told myself, "Don't be afraid. It's probably nothing."

I walked to the kitchen because the noise seemed to come from there. When I turned on the light, I saw a small cat near the trash can! It jumped when it saw me.

The cat ran to the back door and pushed it open with its paw. Someone did not close the door well that night. The cat had come inside to find food.

I laughed and felt silly. The "monster" was just a hungry cat from next door! I gave it some milk, and it drank all of it happily.

I opened the back door again, and the cat walked outside. It looked back at me once before it ran into the dark garden.

The next morning, I told my family the whole story at breakfast. My little brother did not believe me at first, but then he saw the empty milk bowl. We all laughed together.

Now, every night before I sleep, I check the back door twice. I don't want another cat to visit at midnight and give me such a big scare again!

Leo: Did you buy anything for yourself?

Leo: Why did you not answer my messages, then?

Mia: My phone battery died in the afternoon! I did not notice until we got home.

Leo: I see! Well, I am glad you had a good trip. Can I see your photos tomorrow?

Mia: Of course! I will bring my phone to school and show you all of them.

My First Bus Ride Alone

短文
語速：

Today I take the bus alone for the first time. My mom always drives me to school, but this morning she is busy. I feel a little nervous, but I also feel excited.

I walk to the bus stop near my house. I check the time before I leave. The bus comes at 7:40, so I get there at 7:30. I do not want to be late.

When the bus arrives, I ask the driver, "Does this bus go to Central School?" He says yes and tells me where to get off. I pay for my ticket and find a seat by the window.

The ride takes about twenty minutes. I watch the streets go by and count the stops. At each stop, more students get on the bus. Some of them wear the same uniform as me.

Finally, the driver calls out my stop. I say thank you and get off quickly. I look at my watch — it is 7:55. I still have five minutes before class starts!

I walk into school with a big smile. Taking the bus alone is not so hard after all. Tomorrow, I will take the same bus again, and I will not feel nervous at all.
