Taking Care of the Garden Corner

短文
語速：

Behind the school library, there is a small garden corner. A group of students takes care of it every week. They call themselves the Green Team. The garden has tomatoes, beans, and many flowers.

Every Tuesday, the Green Team meets after school. First, they check the soil. If the soil is dry, they water the plants.

If it rains a lot, they do not water the plants that day. Plants need enough water, but too much water is not good for them.

Next, the students pull out the weeds. Weeds grow fast and take food from the other plants. Ling always brings a small tool to help her pull the weeds out. She says the work is not hard, but it takes time.

The team also watches for bugs. Some bugs eat the leaves and hurt the plants. The students do not use strong chemicals. Instead, they pick the bugs off the leaves by hand. This way is safe for the garden and for the students.

In spring, the tomatoes grow very fast. The students put sticks next to the tomato plants. The sticks help the plants stand up straight. Without the sticks, the plants often fall down on the ground.

The Green Team keeps a small notebook. They write down what they do each week. They also draw pictures of the plants. The notebook helps them see how much the garden changes over time.

At the end of the school year, the garden gives a lot of fresh vegetables. The students share the vegetables with the school cafeteria. Everyone enjoys the fresh food, and the Green Team feels proud of their hard work.

A Morning at Grandpa's Breakfast Shop

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Grandpa: Good morning! Welcome to my shop. What do you want for breakfast today?

Mia: Good morning, Grandpa! I want a sandwich and a cup of milk, please.

Grandpa: One sandwich and one milk, coming right up. Do you have school today?

Mia: Yes, I do. I have an English class first, then math.

Grandpa: That sounds busy! Here is your sandwich. It is hot, so be careful.

Mia: Thank you! It smells really good. Do you make the bread yourself?

Grandpa: Yes, I do. I get up at four every morning and make fresh bread.

Mia: Wow, that is so early! Are you tired every day?

Grandpa: A little, but I love this shop. I meet many kind people every morning.

Mia: I always come here before school. Your shop makes my morning happy.

Grandpa: That makes me happy, too. Say hello to your parents for me.

Mia: I will! Oh, I need to go now, or I will be late for school.

Grandpa: Have a good day at school! Study hard and eat a good lunch.

Mia: I will, Grandpa. See you tomorrow morning!

Grandpa: See you tomorrow! Take an umbrella — it looks like rain this afternoon.

A Quiet Morning at School

短文
語速：

Every morning, Leo comes to school early. He likes the quiet classroom before class starts. The room is calm, and only a few students are there. Leo sits at his desk and opens his math book.

At seven twenty, the class monitor turns on the lights. She writes the day's plan on the board. Some students just read. Other students finish their homework from last night. Leo checks his bag for his water bottle and his pencil case.

Today is Monday, so the class has an English quiz first. Leo studies his word list one more time. He reads the words to himself in a low voice. His friend Ken sits next to him and helps him check the spelling.

At seven thirty-five, the teacher walks in with a big smile. "Good morning, everyone!" she says. "Are you ready for the quiz?" Some students say yes, but a few students look afraid. The teacher tells them not to worry too much.

The room becomes very quiet. Everyone puts away their books and takes out a pencil. Leo takes a deep breath. He is a little nervous, but he feels ready for the quiz.

The quiz starts at seven forty. The classroom is silent. Leo writes his name and starts the first question. He thinks the quiz is not easy, but he tries hard on every question.

After twenty more minutes, the teacher says, "Time is up. Please put down your pencils." Leo feels tired but happy. He believes he studies hard every morning, and hard work always helps a little.

Our Library's Favorite-Book List

短文
語速：

Every month, our school library makes a special list. It shows the five books that students borrow the most. The librarian, Mr. Lin, always checks the numbers and prints a new list on the wall.

This month, a funny animal novel is at the top. Many students say they choose it because the story always makes them laugh. Mr. Lin says students usually borrow funny stories more than sad ones.

Sports stories are also popular. Boys often borrow books about basketball and soccer, but girls sometimes pick them too. Mr. Lin thinks a good sports story can teach young students about teamwork.

Some students always return their books late. Mr. Lin does not get angry, but he often sends a short note to remind them. He says a library card is a big responsibility.

Younger students usually borrow picture books with many colors. Older students often want longer stories with more words. Mr. Lin always buys new books for every age group each month.

Every Friday, Mr. Lin shares that week's borrowing habits with the class. He says the number of visitors is usually higher before a school break and lower after a big test.

Mr. Lin believes reading habits change from month to month, but one thing never changes: he always wants more students to visit the library. He says a good book can make a rainy day feel short.

At the end of the year, the library gives a small prize to the class that borrows the most books. Last year, our class got the prize, and everyone was very proud of that result.

A Busy Morning at the MRT Station

短文
語速：

Every school day, Wendy takes the MRT to school with her neighbor Ben. She always leaves home at seven and walks to the station near her building. The station is usually busy, but Wendy does not mind the noise.

On the platform, Wendy always stands behind the yellow line. She never stands too close to the edge. A worker often reminds people to wait behind the line, and most people follow the rule.

The train usually comes every three minutes. Sometimes it is crowded, and Wendy does not have a seat. She often stands and holds the handle. Ben always wants to save a seat for her, but it does not always work.

Wendy usually reads a short story on her phone during the ride. She seldom talks much in the morning because she still feels sleepy. Ben often tells her funny stories about his dog, and she smiles.

At the next station, many students get on the train. They often carry heavy bags and talk about their homework. Wendy sometimes helps a younger student find a seat, and the student always says thank you.

The train stops at five stations before school. Wendy always checks the map above the door so she does not miss her stop. She never gets off at the wrong station.

When the train reaches her stop, Wendy and Ben always walk fast to the gate. They seldom have extra time in the morning, so they usually choose the escalator, not the stairs. It saves a few minutes every morning.

After they leave the station, they still have a ten-minute walk to school. Wendy usually arrives just before the bell rings. She feels a little tired, but she is always ready for a new school day.

A Letter About My Piano Lessons

信件
語速：

Dear Grandma,

How are you? I want to tell you about my piano lessons. I always visit my piano teacher on Saturday afternoon. Her name is Miss Chen, and she is very patient with me.

At first, I often made mistakes. My fingers were slow, and I sometimes played the wrong key. Miss Chen never got angry. She always said, "That's fine. Let's try that again."

Now I practice at home every day for thirty minutes. I usually practice after dinner, but sometimes I practice before breakfast on the weekend. My mom says I improve a little every week.

Last Saturday, Miss Chen taught me a new song. It was difficult at first, but I listened to it many times and worked hard. By the end of the lesson, I could play it without a mistake!

Miss Chen was so happy that she said I was ready for a small concert next month. I feel a little nervous, but I am also proud of my hard work.

I still remember how hard piano was at the start. Now it feels easy, and I love the sound of the music. I hope you can come to my concert and listen to me play.

I miss you very much. I wish you could visit us soon. Please write back and tell me about your garden.

Love,

Ivy

Countdown in the City Plaza

短文
語速：

Every December thirty-first, my family goes to the city plaza for New Year's Eve. Thousands of people come together there. There are colorful lights, loud music, and a big clock on a tall building. Everyone is excited for the new year to start.

This year, my cousin Leo comes with us. It is his first time at the plaza. He looks up at the big clock and asks, When does the countdown start? My dad smiles and says, At eleven fifty-nine!

We find a good spot near the front of the stage. A singer sings happy songs for the people. Between songs, she talks to everyone. She says this event happens every year, in rain or in sunshine.

At last, the big screen shows the time: 11:58. Everyone stands up together. My little sister holds my hand. She is a little nervous, but she is also very excited for the countdown.

Ten! Nine! Eight! The whole plaza counts down together, very loud. Seven! Six! Five! My cousin Leo counts the loudest of all of us. Four! Three! Two! One! Happy New Year!

Fireworks light up the dark sky above the tall building. People smile and say happy new year to strangers nearby. Leo says this is the best night of his life. We promise to come back next year, too.

Meteor Shower on the Clubroom Roof

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Wei and Nina are on the school roof after class. They are both in the astronomy club.

Wei: Look up, Nina! Tonight there is a meteor shower. Our teacher says it starts around eight o'clock.

Nina: Really? I've never seen one before. When does it end?

Wei: It usually lasts a few hours. We should still be able to see it now.

Nina: I see a small light! Was that a shooting star?

Wei: Yes! Make a wish quickly, before it disappears!

Nina: I wish my whole family could be here to see this.

Wei: That's a nice wish. Look, there's another one, over there!

Nina: Wow, I see it too! This is amazing. I want to come back every month.

Wei: Me too. Let's ask the teacher when the next meteor shower is.

Nina: Good idea. I'll write it down so we don't miss it.

Wei: We should also bring warm coats next time. It's a little cold up here.

Nina: You're right. But it's still worth it for a view like this.

Taiwan Festivals Through the Months

短文
語速：

Taiwan has many special festivals during the year. Each festival happens in a certain month, and each one has its own foods, colors, and activities. Let's look at a few important festivals and find out when they happen.

In January or February, families celebrate Chinese New Year. This is the biggest festival of the year. People clean their houses, wear new clothes, and give a red envelope to children. Streets are full of red lanterns and loud music.

About two weeks after Chinese New Year, there is the Lantern Festival. People walk outside at night and look at beautiful paper lanterns. Some towns also have a lantern flying event, where people write wishes on paper lanterns.

In April, many families visit family graves for Tomb Sweeping Day. They clean the graves and bring flowers and food. It is a quiet festival, but it is also an important time for a family to be together.

In June, people celebrate the Dragon Boat Festival. Teams race long, colorful boats on the water. Families also eat rice dumplings made of rice and leaves. The sound of drums fills the air near the river.

In September, the Mid-Autumn Festival comes with a full moon. Families sit outside together, eat mooncakes, and look up at the bright moon. Across the whole year, these festivals show how much people in Taiwan love spending time with their family.

Getting Ready for the Camping Trip

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Mom: We are going camping this weekend. Let's make a shopping list before we go to the store.

Dad: Good idea. First, how many tents do we need for our family?

Mom: We have five people, so I think we need two tents.

Son: How much water should we bring? It might be hot in the mountains.

Dad: Let's bring a lot of water. Maybe six large bottles.

Daughter: What about food? How many bags of rice do we need for three days?

Mom: One bag of rice is enough, but we also need some noodles and vegetables.

Son: How much bread should we buy? I get hungry between meals.

Dad: Let's get two loaves of bread and some fruit, too.

Daughter: How many flashlights do we have at home? It gets dark early at the campsite.

Mom: We only have one flashlight. We should buy two more.

Dad: Good point. Let's also check how much money we have for this trip.

Son: I have some money saved. I can help pay for the extra flashlights.

Mom: Great teamwork! Let's write down everything on this list before we leave for the store.

How Much Rice Do You Want?

短文
語速：

In Taiwan, many people buy lunch at a small lunchbox shop. These shops sell rice with meat and vegetables. Before you pay, the worker often asks two important questions.

The first question is, "How much rice do you want?" Some shops offer a large size and a small size. A big eater may choose more rice, but a small eater may want less.

The second question is, "How many side dishes do you want?" A lunchbox usually comes with one main dish, like chicken or fish, and a few vegetable side dishes.

Some customers say, "A little rice is enough for me." Other customers say, "I want a lot of rice today, I am very hungry." The worker listens and makes the lunchbox by hand.

This custom is a small part of Taiwanese food culture. It shows that food should match each person's needs, not just one fixed size for everybody.

Many students also buy lunchboxes at school. A classmate might ask a friend, "How much rice did you get?" or "How many pieces of chicken are in your box?"

Because portions can change, two lunchboxes with the same price can look very different. One box may have more rice and fewer vegetables, and another may have less rice and more vegetables.

Next time you visit a lunchbox shop, try answering the two questions yourself. Think about how hungry you are, and choose the right amount of rice and side dishes for you.

The Secondhand Book Sale

短文
語速：

Last Saturday, our class had a secondhand book sale in the school gym. Everyone brought old books from home to sell. I brought ten storybooks and a few comic books.

Before the sale, our teacher asked, "How many books does each student have?" We counted our books together. Some students had five books, and some had almost twenty.

My friend Leo set up a small table near the door. He put a sign on the table that said, "Only ten dollars a book!" A lot of students came to look at his books.

I asked Leo, "How much money do you want for this dictionary?" He said, "Only fifteen dollars, because it is a little old." I thought that was cheap, so I bought it.

By noon, many books were already sold. Our teacher asked us, "How many books are still on the tables?" We looked around and answered, "Not many, maybe twenty books."

In the afternoon, more classmates came with their parents. They wanted to buy cheap books for their little brothers and sisters. Everyone was busy and happy at the sale.

At the end of the day, our teacher counted the money. "We got two thousand dollars today!" she said. "This money will go to the school library." Everyone began to clap.

I sold eight of my ten books and bought three new ones from other students. I think a secondhand book sale is a great idea. It helps students save money, and it also helps our library.

The Night the Power Went Out

短文
語速：

Last Friday night, the lights suddenly went out in our town. My sister and I sat in the dark kitchen and listened to the wind outside. My mom said a big storm broke a power line near the market.

At first, we felt a little scared. My dad found a flashlight and some old candles in a kitchen drawer. He lit two candles and put them on the table. The small light made the room feel warm.

We usually watch TV after dinner, but that night we could not. My mom told us an old story instead. My little sister listened with wide eyes and asked questions about every part of it.

After the story, we played a card game by the light of the candles. I often win, but that night my sister won twice! We laughed a lot and almost forgot about the dark street outside our house.

My dad checked his phone once for news about the power. He said workers were fixing the line, but it might take a few more hours. Nobody knew exactly when the lights would come back on.

Around ten o'clock, the lights suddenly came back on! We cheered and clapped our hands. The refrigerator started to work again, and the TV screen lit up.

My mom said we should keep a flashlight in every room from now on. My dad agreed and made it a new family habit. We never want to search for candles in the dark again.

That night taught me something. Even without the TV or the internet, my family found a way to have fun together. I will always remember the night the power went out.

A Letter From Grandma

信件
語速：

Dear Tom,

You asked me what the town where I grew up looked like when I was young. Let me tell you a story about my town almost sixty years ago.

Our street did not have a name, only a number. The road was dirty, and there was no trash truck at that time. We carried our trash to a big hole outside town.

We did not have a clean toilet at home. Our family used a small outhouse behind the kitchen. It was often cold and a little scary at night!

My mother always checked our hands before dinner. She believed dirty fingers could carry germs, and germs could make us sick. We washed our hands in a bucket of water.

Every Sunday, my family walked to the market in town. It took almost an hour to walk there and back. We seldom bought more food than we could carry home.

Life was harder then, but we were happy. We played outside every day and never worried about phones or screens.

I hope you visit the town where I grew up with me someday. The town looks very different now, but some good habits from my childhood are still with me.

With love,

Grandma

An Old Photo, A New Street

短文
語速：

Last month, Leo found an old photo in his grandfather's desk. It showed their street sixty years ago. Leo could not believe how different everything looked in the black-and-white picture.

In the old photo, the street was very narrow and quiet. There were no cars, only a few bicycles and one small shop. People walked slowly and talked with their neighbors on the street.

Today, the same street looks very different. Wide roads now carry many cars and buses every day. Tall buildings stand where small houses once stood, and shops open on every corner.

Leo's grandfather said the old street had a small market on Saturdays. Farmers brought vegetables and fruit from nearby towns. Everyone who lived on the street knew each other's names back then.

Now the market is gone, but a big supermarket stands in its place. People still buy food there, but they seldom talk to their neighbors. Leo's grandfather said he sometimes misses those quiet days.

Leo asked his grandfather what changed the street the most. His grandfather answered that new roads and more cars changed everything after he grew up and started his own family.

Leo decided to take a new photo of the street today. He wants to show it to his own children someday, just like his grandfather showed the old photo to him.

The old photo and the new photo tell two different stories about the same place. Leo believes both stories are important, and he keeps the old photo safe in his room.

Talking About Yesterday's Baseball Game

Owen: It was great, Coach! My dad and I got to the park before the game and got good seats.

Coach Lin: Nice! Who did our team play?

Owen: We played the team from the fishing town near the river. Many people came, so the park was really full.

Coach Lin: Was the game close? What happened?

Owen: At the start of the game, our team did not hit the ball well. The other team got three runs early, and I was a little nervous.

Coach Lin: But your team came back, right?

Owen: Yes! Later in the game, Ryan hit the ball very far, and three runs came in at one time. The fans got up and cheered so loud!

Coach Lin: Wow, that sounds exciting! Did your team have more runs at the end?

Owen: Yes, we did! The final score was five to four. We stayed until the very last minute of the game.

Coach Lin: That's a great story, Owen. I am happy you had a fun time with your dad.

Owen: Me too. We want to go to another game before winter comes. Maybe you can come with us next time!

A New Dinosaur Exhibit

短文
語速：

A new exhibit opens at the city museum this month. The exhibit shows many dinosaur models from around the world. Some models are small, but a few are as tall as a building.

One famous model in the exhibit is a giant plant eater. It has a very long neck and a very long tail. It lived on the land many years ago, before people or fish came to this world.

The museum also has a room with real fossils. A fossil is a very old bone, and scientists use it to learn about ancient animals. Visitors can touch a fossil in this room.

There is also a video room. In the video, kind guides talk about how these animals lived and what they ate. Some animals ate plants, and other animals ate other animals.

The exhibit is open every day this fall and winter. It is open from nine in the morning until five in the evening. Tickets are not very expensive, and children under six do not need a ticket.

Many families and school classes visit the exhibit every week. A worker at the museum says almost a thousand people come every weekend. If you like animals from long ago, this exhibit is a great place to visit.

A Field Trip to the Natural History Museum

短文
語速：

Last Friday, our class went on a field trip to the natural history museum in the city. We got on the bus early in the morning and sang songs along the way.

My classmate Zoe and I talked about the animals we wanted to see. When we got to the museum, our teacher Mr. Wu gave us a map of the building.

The museum was very big, with three floors and many rooms. Our first stop was the space room, where we saw pictures of the stars and the moon.

After that, we went to the animal room. There were big models of a lion, a tiger, and even an elephant. Zoe's favorite was the birds, but my favorite was the bones from a giant ancient animal.

Our guide said the animal walked the land a long time ago, before people came to this world. She said some scientists got the bones near a river many years ago. I took some notes about it.

Before we left, we went to the museum shop. Many visitors bought small toys and books there. I bought a postcard for my mom, and Zoe bought a book about ancient animals.

We got back to school before lunch. Mr. Wu asked us to write about our trip. I said it was one of my favorite trips of the year, and I want to visit the museum again soon.
