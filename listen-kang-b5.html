My First Badminton Match

短文
語速：

Every Saturday morning, I go to the badminton court near my house for a real match. My hands feel cold, even though the room is warm.

My coach tells me to relax and watch the shuttle with care. She says good players do not look at the score; they look at the next hit.

In the first game, I do not win a single point. My opponent is fast, and her serves are hard to return.

During the short break, my coach sits beside me and says, "Forget the score. Just hit one good ball at a time."

I try to stay calm and walk back to the court. This time, I watch the shuttle instead of the scoreboard.

Little by little, I start to win a few points. My arms are tired, but I move my feet the way my coach teaches me.

In the end, I do not win the match, but I win more points in the second game than in the first.

After the match, my opponent shakes my hand and says my footwork is much better than before.

My coach smiles and tells me that a close match can teach more than an easy win.

On the way home, I already feel excited to practice more and play on this court again next weekend.

What's on Our Community Bulletin Board?

短文
語速：

Many neighborhoods have a large board near the entrance called a community bulletin board. People use it to share news with their neighbors.

One common thing on the board is lost-and-found notices. If someone loses a cat or finds a set of keys, they can put up a short note with their phone number.

The board is also used for local events. A neighbor might put up information about a weekend clean-up day or a small holiday party in the park.

Some people use the board to sell or give away things they no longer need, such as bicycles, books, or old furniture.

A few boards even have a section for classes. A neighbor who plays the piano well might offer free lessons for children after school.

Because the board is free for everyone, people are asked to keep their notes short and polite, and to take old notes down after the date has passed.

Volunteers usually check the board once a week. They take away notes that are too old and make space for new ones.

A good community bulletin board can make a neighborhood feel more like a big family, because neighbors learn what is happening close to home.

A DIY Cookie Day at the Factory

短文
語速：

Last Saturday, my class took a bus to a cookie factory outside the city. We were all excited because we could make our own cookies there.

At the door, a worker gave each student a small chart. It showed several steps, from mixing to baking, so we would not forget any step.

First, we had to choose a shape for our cookies. There were many choices on the table, like stars, hearts, and animals. I felt a little bored with the plain round shape, so I picked a star instead.

My friend Zoe seemed nervous at first. She said she was scared she would make a mistake, but the worker was helpful and showed her exactly what to do.

While we waited for our cookies to bake, we watched a short video about the history of the factory. Some parts were surprising, and nobody in my group felt tired or bored during the video.

When our cookies came out of the oven, everyone was surprised at how good they smelled. My mind was already thinking about how yummy they would taste.

Before we left, the worker gave us a small discount card for the gift shop. My classmates and I were interested in the chocolate cookies there, so we bought a few boxes to share.

On the bus ride home, I felt tired but happy. It was one of the most interesting school trips I have ever had, and I hope we can visit another factory soon.

Why Creative Markets Are So Popular

短文
語速：

In many cities, a creative market opens on weekends near a park or an old street. People walk among rows of small tents and tables full of handmade things.

At a creative market, artists sell things they made themselves, such as bags, cards, and small toys. Unlike a big store, every item on the table is a little different.

Some people like these markets because they enjoy talking with the artists. A shopper can ask about the reason behind a design, and the artist is often happy to explain the idea.

Other people are interested in the low prices. Because there is no big shop building to pay for, sellers can sometimes give a small discount.

Local news reports say more young people now use an app to find creative markets near their location. With a few taps, they can check the opening hours and see photos of the goods.

A market can also feel exciting because visitors do not know what they will find. One visitor might feel surprised to discover a new favorite artist among the many small tables.

However, a rainy day can be tiring for both sellers and shoppers, since most tents are outside. Several markets now have a few indoor spots, just in case.

For many families, spending an afternoon at a creative market is a fun choice. Whether they buy something or just look around, it always feels like more than shopping.

Dear Uncle Wei,

I want to tell you about something interesting I saw last weekend. I went with Grandpa to the old temple near his house, and several older men were playing chess in front of it.

Grandpa said he comes here almost every afternoon. He sits on a small stool, and his friends bring their own chairs too. Nobody seems to mind sitting outside, even when it is a little hot.

At first, I felt bored because I did not understand the game. But Grandpa was patient, and he explained each move like a helpful teacher.

One old man made a surprising move, and everyone around the table got excited. A small crowd gathered to watch, and some people even offered their own ideas about what to do next.

I was surprised that Grandpa is actually quite good at chess. His friends say he has a good chance of winning almost every match at the temple.

After the games, we bought some tea from a small cart nearby. Grandpa gave the driver a small tip and thanked him with a big smile.

I think that playing chess at the temple is more than a game for Grandpa. It is also a chance to see his old friends. I hope I can visit again soon and learn to play myself.

Why Do New TVs Look So Thin?

短文
語速：

If you walk into an electronics store today, you will see rows of thin, modern televisions on the wall. Have you ever asked yourself why new TVs are so thin?

Old televisions had a big tube inside to make a picture. That tube needed much space, so old TVs were thick and heavy to carry.

Modern TVs use a flat panel instead. A pixel is a tiny point of light, and a modern screen uses millions of them, in many colors, to make the picture you see.

Some modern TVs put a backlight behind the panel to make each pixel glow. Newer TVs let each pixel make its own light, so the whole screen can be very thin.

Because there is no heavy tube inside, this flat panel can be put on a wall, just like a painting.

A worker at the electronics store once said that a modern TV uses less power than an old one, even though the picture looks much brighter.

The next time you watch a screen at home or in a store, remember that thousands of tiny lights work together every second to make one clear picture.

Practice Makes a Team, Not Just a Player

短文
語速：

Every Saturday morning, Leo and I meet at the basketball court beside the river. The morning air feels cool, and the water moves next to the court.

Last week, my brother said to Kate that basketball was a game for boys, and she should just watch from the sideline. Kate did not answer him. She just picked up the ball.

She started to dribble past two players, jumped, and the ball went straight into the basket without even touching the backboard. Everyone on the court went quiet for a second, and then everyone cheered.

After that, my brother never said basketball was only for boys again. Instead, he asked Kate to be his teammate for the next game.

We play three games every Saturday. The team that does not win has to buy drinks for everyone else. We do not like to lose, but we always end the morning and laugh together.

Today, a new student joined us. He looked a little nervous and stayed near the sideline instead of joining right away.

Kate walked over, and said, "Come on, we need one more player for a fair game." The new student was happy and ran to the court.

By the time we finished, the sun was high above the river, and we were tired but happy. Next time, I want every Saturday morning to feel just like this one.

Why Cities Build Pedestrian Bridges

短文
語速：

In many busy cities, you can see a tall bridge that crosses above a big road. This kind of bridge is called a pedestrian bridge, and it is only for people walking, not for cars.

The main reason cities build these bridges is safety. A busy road with fast cars can be dangerous for people who want to cross it. A bridge lets people walk above the traffic in a safe way.

Some pedestrian bridges also give people a great view of the city. From the top, you can see cars, buildings, and sometimes even mountains far away.

The city government chooses where to build a bridge with care. They often put a bridge near a school, a hospital, or a busy market street, because many people cross the road in these places.

Building a pedestrian bridge takes many months of work. Engineers first study the traffic and the number of people who use the road every day. Then workers build the bridge, step by step.

Some newer bridges are even built with a small elevator, so older people and people in a wheelchair can go up without trouble.

Next time you walk across a pedestrian bridge, take a moment to enjoy the view from the top. What a useful piece of city design it is!

The New Choir Leader

短文
語速：

Every Wednesday afternoon, the music room fills with voices. Our choir teacher, Ms. Tan, picks a new student leader for the spring show this year, and to my surprise, she chooses me.

At first, I feel nervous. Being a leader means I must help everyone sing the right notes and keep the group together. I am not a great singer, but Ms. Tan says I am patient and careful.

During the first practice, some students sing too fast, and some forget the words. I ask them to repeat the first line again and again, until every voice sounds natural together.

One boy called Ryan keeps singing off pitch. Instead of feeling sad, I stand next to him and sing in a low voice in his ear. After trying a few times, he finally finds the right note, and I feel proud.

Ms. Tan watches us from the back of the room. She says a good leader does not need to be the loudest voice. A good leader listens, helps others improve, and never gives up on the group.

By the end of the month, our choir sounds very good. Even the shy students sing with confidence now. We practice a difficult song again and again, and it starts to feel simple.

On the day of the show, our voices rise together as one sound. When we finish, the whole hall claps loudly, and Ms. Tan smiles at me from the side of the room.

After the show, Ryan tells me thank you for helping him find his voice. I realize that being a leader is not about singing the loudest note. It is about helping everyone do good work together.

Why Our School Grows a Garden

短文
語速：

Behind our classroom building, there is a small garden with rows of vegetables. Every Tuesday afternoon, our science teacher, Mr. Ho, takes our class outside to work in the soil.

At the start of the term, we plant tiny seeds in small pots. Mr. Ho explains that seeds need water, sunlight, and good soil to grow. Without these three things, a seed cannot sprout.

After about two weeks, small green sprouts appear above the soil. We move the strongest plants into the garden outside, where they have more room to grow bigger and stronger.

Some students add natural fertilizer made from old fruit and vegetable pieces. Mr. Ho says this fertilizer gives the plants extra nutrients, and it also keeps our school less wasteful.

Growing vegetables is not always simple. Some plants get sick, and insects sometimes eat the leaves. When this happens, we learn how to check the soil and fix the problem together.

By early summer, our garden is full of tomatoes, corn, and green beans. It takes weeks of careful work, but watching a tiny seed grow into real food feels amazing.

On harvest day, every student picks vegetables to bring home. Mr. Ho tells us that growing food teaches patience and hard work, two skills that help us in every part of life.

Our school garden also gives extra vegetables to a nearby charity kitchen. Mr. Ho says helping others is another important lesson that a small garden can teach a whole class.

Why Students Clean Their Own Classrooms

短文
語速：

In many schools in Taiwan, students clean their own classrooms every day. This is different from schools in some other countries, where a cleaning staff does most of the work.

Cleaning time usually happens after class, and it lasts about fifteen to twenty minutes. Each student in the class has a small cleaning job to finish.

Some students sweep the floor, while others wipe the windows or clean the board. A few students empty the trash cans and collect the recycling.

Teachers believe that cleaning the classroom teaches students to take care of their own space. It also teaches teamwork, since the whole class must finish together.

Before summer or winter break, schools often have a bigger cleaning day. On this day, students clean areas outside the classroom too, such as the stairs and the yard.

During a big cleaning day, some students move desks and chairs so the floor can be cleaned well. Others wash windows until they shine.

Many students say cleaning together with classmates is actually fun, even though it can feel tiring. They talk and laugh while they work.

After cleaning, the teacher usually checks each area and thanks the class for their hard work.

A clean, comfortable classroom is a reward the students share as a group, and some schools even give a small prize to the cleanest class.

This custom helps students understand that a classroom belongs to everyone, not just the teacher. It builds a sense of responsibility that many students remember for years.

In the afternoon, each class picked a class leader and worked together to write class rules on a big piece of paper.

Before we went home, Mr. Chen reminded us to bring our textbooks and a pencil case on the first real day of class.

I walked out of the school gate feeling excited instead of nervous. I could not wait to start learning with my new classmates.
