全文（點任一字查中文意思）

Ken looked at his phone screen for hours every single day. His parents worried he had a small addiction to funny memes, and his attention span for homework had become very short.

His aunt lived in a quiet village in the country, far from the busy city. She invited Ken to stay for one week and promised there was no phone signal in her yard.

On the first morning, Ken felt bored without his phone. His aunt gave him an old letter from his grandfather and asked him to read it slowly and think about each sentence.

His aunt helped him set a simple study schedule: two hours to focus on schoolwork, one hour to tidy the yard, and one hour to walk beside the sea and watch the waves.

At first, Ken's brain kept wandering, but he learned to repeat each idea to himself until it stayed in his memory. His notes became clearer with every single day that passed.

A package arrived from his mother with a new notebook and a small blanket for cold evenings. A wooden sign near the gate read, "Slow down and store today in your heart."

By the end of the week, Ken could review a whole chapter without stopping once. He realized memes and games were not stupid by themselves, but they had quietly stolen his time.

When Ken's father came to park the car outside the yard, he found his son studying calmly at the table. Ken smiled and said his IQ had not changed, but his focus had.

區塊 6 / 8　⑥ 文法 B-2：被動語態易錯點（＝知洞 GP-52 下半）講義 原創題（對應課本文法點）
易錯點：be動詞不能省略（不能只用過去分詞當謂語）；過去分詞要用正確的不規則變化（broken 不是 breaked、found 不是 founded、spread 不是 spreaded）；不及物動詞（如 sleep）沒有受詞、不能改被動；by之後的執行者若不重要或不知道可以省略，不是每句都要加。

Last spring, Ben found a green notebook under his seat on the morning train to school. He picked it up and looked inside for a name, but there was only a small drawing of a cat.

Ben thought about leaving the notebook on the seat, but he worried the owner would never find it there. Instead, he decided to bring it home and think about what to do next.

That night, Ben searched through the notebook page by page. Near the back, he found a bus ticket with a store address printed on it in small, careful letters.

The next morning, Ben carried the notebook to the small store on the ticket. The owner said a girl often stopped there after school to buy juice before catching her train home.

Ben waited outside the store for almost an hour. Finally, a girl with a blue backpack walked in, looking worried. Ben asked if she had lost anything on the train that day.

The girl's face lit up with surprise. She said the notebook held every drawing she had made that year, and she had been afraid it was gone forever.

She thanked Ben many times and offered him a reward, but he said no. He told her that finding her smile again felt like reward enough for a simple, honest act.
