全文（點任一字查中文意思）

Every February, my class does a special project for Valentine's Day. This year, Ms. Chen had us choose a topic about food and share it with the whole school. My partner and I picked chocolate.

At first, we thought chocolate was simple, but it was harder than we thought. We read a web page about cocoa beans and learned that most of the world's cocoa comes from West Africa, like the Ivory Coast.

The information surprised us. Workers there produce millions of pounds of cocoa beans every year, but many of them earn very little money for such hard labor.

A new law now says companies must protect workers and pay them fairly. This kind of fair trade service also helps small farmers share more of the profit for their hard work.

We also learned that raw cocoa beans taste bitter, not sweet. Sugar and milk are added later to make the chocolate taste good. Without sugar, nobody would want to eat it!

On the day of the fair, we set up a small table alone in the corner of the gym. We were a little nervous, but we spoke clearly and loudly so people could hear us.

An American student named Zoe stopped by our table. She asked good questions, and we had a chance to explain how a small chocolate treat can also mean a fair chance for a farmer.

That weekend, my family visited my uncle's small farm, where he raises animals and grows vegetables. My uncle, the owner, walked us slowly over the fields so we would not get dirty.

We saw a goat, a goose, a hen, a duck, a sheep, and even a mouse running quickly under the fence. My uncle warned us not to break any eggs while we collected them near the hens.

We picked ripe strawberries, grapes, peaches, pears, guavas, papayas, tomatoes, and a huge watermelon. My uncle said good soil and hard work make his produce grow well, not luck.

That night, my uncle made warm cocoa drinks for everyone and let us choose a strong cup or a weak one. It was a wise way to end a busy but sweet weekend.

Between the chocolate project and the farm trip, I learned that even one cent of chocolate can carry a big story. I improved my English and my thinking at the same time, and I felt very proud.

區塊 2 / 7　② 單字驗收（全課 55 字）講義 本課單字（例句原創）
全課單字集中在這一區：先聽 → 唸對才過，然後每個字都要「聽音拼寫」（三個詞組改聽音選字），拼錯不算過。
全文（點任一字查中文意思）

I sometimes eat lunch alone, and my American friend often joins me. Yesterday she brought some cocoa beans that tasted very bitter, and she warned me not to break my cup while I ate.

The old machine only costs one more cent, so I decided to try my chance and choose a cup of hot cocoa. The teacher spoke clearly about how it was made.

My shoes got dirty when a duck and a goat ran past me on the farm path. A goose walked by too, and I picked a sweet grape to eat while I watched them.

The guava tasted fresh, but it was hard to find a ripe one. The farmer said, "Have some water first," and then had us help her feed the hens, and she said the work would improve our patience.

We found new information about the Ivory Coast, where much of the world's cocoa is grown. A new law protects workers from unfair labor, and our teacher explained it loudly to the class.

It takes many steps to make good chocolate. I did not know what one word could mean until I read that millions of beans are picked by hand. A small mouse ran quickly over the barn floor.

The farm owner let us pick a papaya, a peach, and a pear from his trees. Each fruit tasted a little different, but all of them were sweet.

The farm can produce many pounds of fruit in one week. A fair trade service helps protect the farmers, and workers move quickly to pack the fresh fruit for market.

We watched a sheep eat grass slowly near the fence while we picked a big strawberry and some tomatoes. My cousin was happy to share the basket with everyone.

As a Valentine's Day treat, we brought home a turtle-shaped cake, fresh vegetables, and a giant watermelon from the farm stand.

I read a web page that said even a weak cup of cocoa can taste good without much sugar. My uncle said it was a wise way to end the day well.

先把整篇文章讀完（用眼睛讀，還不用唸）

Last Friday, I took the MRT home after school. When I got off the train, I could not find my backpack. My heart began to beat fast, and I felt a little sick.

I remembered that my backpack had my house key, my wallet, and my favorite notebook inside. Without it, I did not know how to get into my apartment.

A worker at the station told me to go to the Lost and Found Center on the first floor. I walked there quickly, feeling nervous the whole way.

At the center, a kind man asked me questions about my backpack. What color was it? What was inside? I answered as clearly as I could.

He checked a long list on his computer. After a few minutes, he smiled and said, "I think we found it." He brought out a gray backpack just like mine.

I opened it and saw my key, my wallet, and my notebook, all still inside. I was so happy that I almost cried in front of everyone there.

The man explained that another passenger had found my bag on the train and brought it straight to the center. I could not believe how kind that stranger was.

Now I always check my seat before I get off the train. I learned that a small mistake can cause a big problem, but a kind stranger can also fix it.
