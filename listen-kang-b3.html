Growing Tomatoes on the Balcony

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Leo: Mom, can we grow tomatoes on our balcony this spring? My teacher says tomatoes need much sun and warm weather.

Mom: That sounds fun! Our balcony gets sun most of the morning, so it should be a good spot for tomato plants.

Leo: What kind of weather do tomatoes like most? Do they grow well in the rainy season?

Mom: They like warm, sunny days. Too much rain can hurt young plants, so we will need to move the pots under the roof on very rainy days.

Leo: Should we water them every day, even when it is cloudy?

Mom: Not every day. We should check the soil first. If it still feels wet from the last watering, we can wait one more day.

Leo: What happens if it gets really hot and dry for a whole week?

Mom: In hot, dry weather, the plants need more water and something to cover them from the sun in the afternoon.

Leo: This sounds like a fun project! When can we get the seeds and start planting?

Mom: Let's go to the garden shop this weekend and pick out some strong young tomato plants together.

Weather and the Spring Kite Festival

短文
語速：

Every spring, many towns near the coast hold a kite festival on a big open meadow. People of all ages bring colorful kites of different shapes, from simple diamonds to long dragons with many parts.

Kite flying really depends on the weather. A festival needs gentle wind, not too weak and not too strong. If the day is calm with almost no wind, kites are not able to rise into the air at all.

On the other hand, a very windy day can also cause trouble. Strong wind can break a kite string or pull a kite away from a person holding it. Because of this, festival workers check the wind speed with care before the event starts.

Most kite festivals choose a date in spring because the weather in this season is often warm, with gentle wind and few storms. Cold winter wind is usually too strong and hard to plan for.

A large meadow is the perfect place for this activity. The open space keeps kites away from trees, buildings, and power lines. It also gives hundreds of people enough room to run and fly their kites at the same time.

Besides the fun, kite festivals also teach people about air and wind. Visitors learn that a kite can fly because wind pushes against it, lifting it higher into the sky.

Many families now plan a trip to the meadow every year for the festival. They check the weather report the week before, hoping for a sunny sky and a gentle wind for their favorite kite-flying day.

Sunrise at Cloud Rock Platform

短文
語速：

Last weekend, Mia and her grandfather got up before dawn to watch the sunrise from Cloud Rock Platform. The night before, they checked the weather report. It said the sky would be clear, but early morning is often foggy in the mountains.

They walked up a small path with a flashlight. The air was cold, and thick fog covered the trees around them. Mia felt a little nervous because she could not see far ahead, but her grandfather said the fog usually clears before sunrise.

When they reached the platform, other visitors were already there, standing quiet and still. Everyone looked toward the east and talked in soft voices about the weather. Some people felt afraid that the clouds were too heavy and the sun might not appear at all.

Slowly, the sky above the mountains turned from gray to pink, then to a bright orange color. The fog slowly went away, and warm light spread across the valley. A visitor next to Mia said the early start was a good idea.

After the sun came up, the sky turned clear and blue, and the morning felt much warmer. Grandfather explained that mountain weather can change very fast, so visitors should always bring extra clothes, even on a sunny day.

Before they left, Mia took several photos of the golden mountains and the small village far below. She said the change from a dark, foggy sky to a bright, clear one was the most exciting part of the whole trip.

Puzzle Night in the Living Room

短文
語速：

Every Friday night, my family plays a game together in the living room. This week, my dad gets a new puzzle with five hundred small pieces. My little brother feels very excited.

We sit around the table and start to put the pieces into small groups. My mom finds all the corner pieces first. My brother tries to find the edge pieces, but his hands are too fast, and some pieces drop on the floor.

After one hour, my brother's fingers feel tired from picking up so many small pieces. He touches his sore arm and says, "My arm feels sore!" My mom laughs and tells him to take a short rest.

We turn on the lamp next to the sofa because the living room feels dark. My dad plays some soft music, and we still work hard together. Slowly, the picture on the puzzle looks clear: a big mountain with a blue lake.

Near the end, only one small piece is missing. We look under the sofa and see it under a pillow. My brother puts in the last piece with a big smile.

"We do it!" we all say together. My mom takes a photo of the finished puzzle. My brother feels proud, and his sore arm does not matter now.

We want to buy a new puzzle next month too. My dad says a puzzle night is a great way for a family to rest and have fun together.

Learning to Sew a Button

信件
語速：

Dear Grandma,

Thank you for teaching me to sew this weekend! I want to tell you how it goes.

On Friday, I look at my winter coat and see a button is missing. Mom says the coat looks a little torn near the pocket too. I do not know what to do, so I call you.

You tell me to find a needle and some thread that matches the color of my coat. At first, my hands feel a little strange, and the needle almost hurts my finger! But I try again slowly, just like you show me.

It takes me a long time to sew the button back on. My work is not perfect, but the button feels strong and safe now. I also fix the small torn part near the pocket.

Now my coat looks good again, and I feel proud of my new skill. Dad says I am a patient person because I do not give up.

I hope I can visit you again this weekend and show you my coat. Maybe you can teach me to fix my other clothes too!

With love,

Zoe

Why the Power Goes Out

短文
語速：

Sometimes, all the lights in a house or a whole town turn off at the same time. People call this a power outage. It can happen for many reasons, and it usually does not last too long.

Bad weather is one common reason. A big storm with strong wind can break an electric wire, or a tree branch can fall on it. Then the electricity cannot travel to your house anymore.

Another reason is a problem at the power company. Workers there control the electricity for a whole city. A machine can break, so they must fix it fast. Sometimes this takes a few hours.

During a power outage, families need to stay safe. It is important to use a flashlight, not fire, for light. A flashlight is much safer than an open fire in a dark room.

Some families like to use candles too, but a candle can be dangerous. A candle has a small fire, and a careless candle can start a bigger fire. It is best to put a candle on a plate, far away from paper or clothes.

Many families keep a small bag ready at home. The bag has a flashlight, some batteries, and a bottle of water. This way, they do not need to look for these things in the dark.

A power outage can make people feel a little afraid at first, but it does not need to be a bad experience. Some families play games or tell stories together. It can even become a fun family memory.

Drying Quilts on the Rooftop

短文
語速：

In many Taiwanese homes, families like to dry their quilt on the rooftop. This is a simple way to keep the quilt clean, warm, and free from bad smells.

On a sunny day, people carry the heavy quilt up to the rooftop early in the morning. They hang the quilt over a rope or lay it on a mat.

Sunlight is important for this job. Strong sunlight can kill germs and make the quilt feel warmer at night. That is why people usually choose a clear day, not a rainy one.

Wind also helps. When wind blows through the quilt, it carries away moisture and germs. This makes the quilt lighter than before.

Most families check the weather before they carry the quilt outside. If rain might come in the afternoon, they wait for another sunny day.

After a few hours, someone pats the quilt with a soft hand. A dry quilt makes a soft sound, while a wet quilt feels heavy and cool.

Finally, the family brings the warm quilt back inside before dinner. That night, everyone enjoys sleeping under a quilt that smells like fresh sunlight.

This simple habit does not cost any money, but it makes a small difference in daily life. Many grandmas say sunlight is a great help.

The Day the Washing Machine Broke

短文
語速：

Last Sunday morning, my mom washes the laundry when the washing machine stops working. She tries the button again and again, but nothing happens.

My dad comes into the kitchen while my mom checks the machine. He looks inside and says, "I think a part inside is bad. We need to call a repairman."

We have a big pile of dirty clothes, and it is already Sunday. Most repair shops do not open on Sunday, so we have to wait until Monday morning.

My little brother starts to worry because his favorite shirt is in the pile. He asks, "What if the shirt smells bad by tomorrow?" We all laugh a little at his question.

My mom chooses to wash a few important clothes by hand in the sink. My brother and I help her carry water and hang the wet clothes on the balcony.

It takes almost two hours to finish the small pile. Our arms feel tired, but we feel proud when we see the clean clothes hanging in the sun.

On Monday, a repairman comes and fixes the washing machine in twenty minutes. He says an old part is bad, and now everything works again.

That weekend teaches me something important. Even a small problem, like a washing machine that will not work, can bring a family together when everyone helps.

Why Backyard Swings Are So Much Fun

短文
語速：

Many families have a swing in their backyard. A swing is simple: a seat, two ropes or chains, and a strong frame or a tree branch above it.

When you sit on a swing and someone pushes you, you move forward, and then you swing back. This happens again and again, and it feels exciting every time.

When you go up, you are slow for a moment. When you come down, you are very fast. This change from slow to fast is what makes swinging so much fun.

Children often like swings because they feel like flying, even though their feet never leave the ground for long. Adults enjoy swings too, because moving slow and fast feels calm and nice.

Many backyard swings are made of wood, but some use metal parts instead. Parents usually check the ropes and the frame often, so the swing stays safe for kids to use.

On a warm afternoon, a swing is a popular spot in the yard. Brothers and sisters often take turns, and friends count how many times someone can swing before it stops.

Some families even hang two swings side by side, so two kids can swing together and talk while they move through the air.

A backyard swing does not need a battery or a screen. It only needs some open space, a little push, and some imagination to make an afternoon fun.

I hope you can visit us for a movie night soon. We can make popcorn together and watch your favorite movie too.

Love, Ken

A Garage Sale on Saturday

短文
語速：

Last Saturday, Ken and his family had a garage sale in front of their house. They put old toys, books, and clothes on three long tables in the driveway.

Ken's mom made colorful price tags for every item. She wrote the prices with a black pen and put a tag on each toy and book before the sale began.

At nine in the morning, a few neighbors walked over and looked at the tables. An old man picked up a wooden chess set and asked, "How much is this chess set?"

Ken's dad smiled and said, "It's only eighty dollars, and it still works well." The old man was happy, and he paid for it right away.

A young woman with two kids stopped by the tables. Her children saw a box of picture books and asked their mom for three of them.

While Ken helped the customers, his sister counted the money in a small box. "We already have two hundred dollars," she said with a big smile.

Before lunch, a boy from Ken's class came by on his bike. He looked at an old bicycle helmet and a soccer ball, and he bought both of them.

By four in the afternoon, most of the tables were empty. Ken's family was tired, but they were glad to give their old things a new home.

Ben's First Loose Tooth

短文
語速：

My little brother Ben is only six years old. Last night at dinner, he touches his front tooth and says it feels a little loose.

Mom looks at it and smiles. She says a loose tooth is normal at his age, and a new tooth will grow in its place soon.

Ben feels a bit worried, so Dad tells him an old family story. Grandpa always says a new tooth grows straight and strong if you take care of the old one first.

Every day this week, Ben touches his tooth after each meal. He does not want to pull it too soon, but he also does not want to wait too long.

On Friday night, Ben bites a piece of bread, and the tooth suddenly comes out. He is surprised, but he is also very excited.

Our family has a small tradition for a lost tooth. We put the tooth in a small box under Ben's pillow, and Ben makes a quiet wish before he sleeps.

The next morning, Ben finds a coin under his pillow. He runs to tell everyone, and he can not stop smiling all day.

Mom takes a photo of Ben's big smile with the missing tooth. She says we will remember this small, wonderful moment for many years.

Why Taiwanese Families Barbecue on Mid-Autumn Festival

短文
語速：

In Taiwan, many families cook meat and vegetables outside on Mid-Autumn Festival night. This custom is now so common that people often call it "barbecue night."

But this tradition is not very old. Fifty years ago, most families only ate moon cakes and fruit together under the moon on this night.

In the 1980s, a sauce company starts strong TV advertisements about cooking meat at home on a small grill. The advertisements use a simple line: one person cooks, and the whole family comes to eat.

The advertisements work very well. More and more families begin to buy a small grill for this one special night every year.

Soon, stores also sell easy barbecue sets with meat, vegetables, and sauce all together. This makes the custom easy for busy families, too.

Today, on Mid-Autumn Festival night, the smell of meat on the grill fills many streets and parks across Taiwan. Family members and neighbors often cook together and share food.

Some people still eat moon cakes and fruit, too, but for many young people, a barbecue is now the first thing they think of on this festival.

This shows how a clever advertisement, more than an old custom, can create a brand new tradition that a whole country enjoys together.

Sincerely,

The Grade 8 Charity Fair Team

How a Classroom Gift Exchange Game Works

短文
語速：

Many schools in Taiwan now have a gift exchange activity before winter break. Students bring a small, cheap gift, and the class trades the gifts in a fun and exciting way.

There are many rules for a gift exchange game. One common rule is that every gift must cost about the same amount of money, so nobody feels bad about their gift.

Before the game starts, each student puts a gift in a bag or a box. A teacher or a student then mixes all the gifts together so nobody knows whose gift is whose.

During the game, players often sit in a circle. Someone rolls dice or picks a number, and that player can choose a new gift or take a gift from another player.

This kind of game is popular because it feels like a surprise every time. A player's favorite gift can change hands many times before the game ends.

Teachers say the activity is convenient because it does not take much time or space, and it can happen right in the classroom during a short class period.

Some classes also add a fun rule: if a player picks the same gift twice, that player must sing a song or tell a joke in front of the whole class.

By the end of the activity, most students agree that the fun is not really about the gift. It is about laughing together before the winter holiday begins.

The Monster Costume That Almost Wasn't

短文
語速：

Last October, my class planned a Halloween party at school. Everyone had to wear a costume, so I decided to be a green monster with a funny face and big paper claws.

My mom helped me make the costume at home. We used old cloth from my closet, and my little brother drew a big smile on the monster mask with a black pen.

On the morning of the party, I put on my costume and rode my bike to school because it was faster than walking. The wind made my paper claws fly around a little.

When I got to the corner near school, I made a small mistake and turned the wrong way. I had to go back two blocks and turn left again to find the right street.

A kind clerk at a corner store saw my funny costume and laughed. She said, "Sir Monster, you are going to be late!" I laughed too and rode away as fast as I could.

At school, everyone loved my costume. My classmates said the mask looked scary but also a little cute. We took many photos together in the classroom before the party started.

During the party, we played games and ate snacks. One rule was that nobody could take off their mask until the games finished, so my face got a little hot under the mask.

By the end of the day, my monster costume was my favorite thing about the whole month. I told my family that next year, I want to make an even better costume.
