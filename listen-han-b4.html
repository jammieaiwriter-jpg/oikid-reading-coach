A Letter About Waiting at the Scooter Shop

信件
語速：

Dear Ryan,

Yesterday, Dad's scooter would not start, so we pushed it to the small repair shop near our apartment. The shop owner is an old man with kind eyes and strong hands.

He looked at the scooter for a minute and said the battery was too old. He said a new battery would make the scooter start faster and run more smoothly than before.

While we waited, I stayed on a chair outside the shop and watched him work. He worked fast, but he was also very careful with every small part.

A woman came in with a flat tire on her scooter. The shop owner said he could fix her tire first because it was a quick job, and Dad did not mind waiting a little longer.

After about forty minutes, the scooter was ready. Dad gave the shop owner the money for the new battery, and the shop owner was happy and said the scooter should run well for a few more years now.

On the way home, the scooter sounded much quieter than before. Dad said a good repair shop always makes the wait feel okay.

Next time, I want to ask the shop owner more questions about how a battery works. Write back soon and tell me about your week!

Your friend,

Mia

A Morning at the Shoe Repair Stall

短文
語速：

Every Sunday morning, I go with my grandpa to his small shoe repair shop near the old market. He sets up his tools before seven o'clock, and I help him carry the box of nails and glue.

A man came in with a sandal that would not stay on his foot. He said the strap came off while he was walking to work. Grandpa looked at it and said he could fix it in about fifteen minutes.

While Grandpa worked, a woman came with two pairs of leather shoes. One pair had a hole in the bottom, and the other just needed a new stitch near the toe. She waited on a small chair without talking.

Grandpa uses a special needle and strong string for leather shoes. He said leather is much harder to fix than other shoe parts, so it needs a slow, careful stitch. I watched him work with steady hands.

At noon, a boy about my age came fast to the shop with his school shoes. One shoe had a loose sole. Grandpa laughed and said school shoes always break faster than shoes people wear at home.

By the end of the morning, Grandpa fixed eight pairs of shoes. He looked tired, but he was happy to see every customer say thank you. I asked him why he still enjoys this simple work.

He said fixing shoes lets people use them for many more years, so they do not need to buy new shoes right away, and that makes him happy. I thought about that all the way home. What a nice morning it was!

Waiting on the Train Platform

短文
語速：

Every train station has a platform where people wait for their train. In Taiwan, most platforms have a yellow line near the edge, and everyone learns to stand behind it for safety.

A big screen above the platform shows the train number, the time, and which city the train is going to. People check the screen often so they do not get on the wrong train.

Some platforms have two sides. One side is for trains going north, and the other side is for trains going south. A worker often stands near the stairs to help people find the right side.

When a fast train comes, the platform can feel a little windy. Many students hold their bags close and take a small step back. This simple habit keeps everyone safe and calm.

During busy hours, more people wait on the platform than during quiet hours. Workers ask people to form two lines near each door so passengers can get off the train easily before others get on.

Some platforms also have soft music or a friendly voice that tells people when the next train will arrive. This helps people relax instead of feeling worried about missing their train.

A few platforms have small shops that sell drinks, snacks, and simple gifts. Travelers can buy something quick before a long trip, which makes waiting feel less boring.

So the next time you wait on a platform, remember the yellow line, check the big screen, and listen for the friendly voice. A little care makes every trip safer for everyone.

A Chat with the Bus Driver

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Sean: Good morning! Does this bus go to the sports center?

Mr. Ho: Good morning! Yes, it does. It is a stop close to here, about three kilometers away.

Sean: Great. Is this bus usually more crowded than the other buses in the morning?

Mr. Ho: A little, yes. This route is very busy, so please hold the handrail carefully.

Sean: I will. By the way, whose seat is that empty one near the front?

Mr. Ho: Nobody's. That seat is always empty for elderly passengers, so younger riders should not sit there if possible.

Sean: I understand. I will sit myself in the back, then.

Mr. Ho: Good idea. We will reach the sports center in about ten minutes if the road is not too busy.

Sean: Thank you! One more question, how much is the fare for this trip?

Mr. Ho: It is fifteen dollars if you pay with an EasyCard, and it is the same price at every stop in town.

Sean: Perfect, I have my card ready. Thanks for your help today!

Mr. Ho: You are welcome! Enjoy your day, and hold the handrail well, the bus goes now.

The Shuttle Boat at the Fishing Harbor

短文
語速：

Many small fishing harbors in Taiwan have a shuttle boat that carries visitors from the harbor to a nearby island. This kind of boat is smaller than a ferry, but it is faster and cheaper.

The shuttle boat usually holds about twenty people. It travels only a few miles each trip, but the water near the harbor can be deep, so the safety rules are strict and important.

Before the boat leaves, every visitor must wear a life jacket. The captain checks each jacket himself and asks everyone to stay seated during the ride.

The trip to the island takes about fifteen minutes, which is much shorter than the drive around the coast. Visitors say the boat ride is more exciting than a bus ride because of the waves.

On sunny days, more people want to ride the shuttle boat, so the wait can be longer. On windy days, the boat sometimes stops for the day because the sea becomes too wild.

Local fishermen use the same way to the island every morning. They know the harbor much more than any visitor, and they can tell when the weather changes just by watching the waves.

If you visit a fishing harbor with a shuttle boat, remember to check the weather first. A calm sea keeps the trip safe and quick, while a wild sea makes the ride slower and harder.

Many visitors say a ride on the shuttle boat is one of the most memorable parts of a trip to a fishing village, so you should try it at least once.

My First Ride on the High-Speed Rail

短文
語速：

Last month, my family took the high-speed rail from Taipei to Kaohsiung for the first time. My little brother asked again and again how fast the train could go, and I was more excited than he was.

At the station, my dad checked our tickets and showed us where to stand. He said the platform had more space than the platform on a regular train, so we did not feel worried about the crowd.

When the train came, it looked longer and cleaner than any train in our city. We took our own seats, and I took a seat by the window so I could watch outside.

The train got faster and faster until the grass outside turned into a green blur. My brother said it was like flying, and I said it was the most comfortable ride we ever had.

In the middle of the trip, my mom checked her small diary and said the number of kilometers we had already traveled. She said the whole trip was almost three hundred kilometers.

I asked the man next to us whose bag was on the empty seat. He picked it up himself and said sorry for the extra space.

We got to Kaohsiung in less than two hours, which was much shorter than the five-hour drive we usually took. Everyone said the high-speed rail was the most comfortable way to travel.

On the way home, I talked to my classmates about the trip. I said the high-speed rail was safe and quicker than a car, and I hope we can ride it again soon.

Love,

Nora

Why Are Bike Paths So Busy on Holidays?

短文
語速：

On sunny holidays, the bike path along the river gets very crowded. Hundreds of families come out to ride bikes, walk their dogs, or just enjoy the fresh air.

Many people choose the bike path because it is flat, safe, and free to use. Riders do not need a car or a ticket; they only need a bike and some free time.

Local shops near the path also produce more business on holidays. Small stands sell drinks, snacks, and simple repair service for riders whose bike has a problem.

However, when so many people share the same path, riders must be careful. Going too quickly around a corner can be dangerous for both bikers and walkers.

City workers put up signs that remind riders to slow down and to ring their bell before passing someone. These small rules help protect everyone on the path.

Some families bring their own bikes, while others choose to rent one for a few hours. Renting is a wise choice for people who do not own a bike or live far away.

By evening, the path becomes quiet again, just like it was early that morning. Most riders go home tired but happy, already planning their next holiday ride.

A Missing Backpack at the MRT Station

短文
語速：

Last Friday, I took the MRT home after school. When I got off the train, I could not find my backpack. My heart began to beat fast, and I felt a little sick.

I remembered that my backpack had my house key, my wallet, and my favorite notebook inside. Without it, I did not know how to get into my apartment.

A worker at the station told me to go to the Lost and Found Center on the first floor. I walked there quickly, feeling nervous the whole way.

At the center, a kind man asked me questions about my backpack. What color was it? What was inside? I answered as clearly as I could.

He checked a long list on his computer. After a few minutes, he smiled and said, "I think we found it." He brought out a gray backpack just like mine.

I opened it and saw my key, my wallet, and my notebook, all still inside. I was so happy that I almost cried in front of everyone there.

The man explained that another passenger had found my bag on the train and brought it straight to the center. I could not believe how kind that stranger was.

Now I always check my seat before I get off the train. I learned that a small mistake can cause a big problem, but a kind stranger can also fix it.

A Busy Day at the Park Entrance Booth

短文
語速：

Last Saturday, I visited my aunt at her job. She works at the entrance booth of a big mountain park, and she sells tickets to visitors every day.

When I got there, a long line of cars was already waiting outside the gate. My aunt gave each driver a ticket and a small paper map of the park.

Around ten o'clock, a tourist parked his car and asked for directions to the waterfall. My aunt pointed at the map and explained the shortest path with a smile.

At noon, it started to rain hard. Many visitors ran back to their cars, but a few families stayed and put on their raincoats to keep walking.

My aunt said that on rainy days, fewer people buy tickets, so the line moves faster. She still has to stay happy and answer every question with a smile.

In the afternoon, an old couple could not find their parking space. My aunt called another worker on the radio, and he helped the couple find their car quickly.

Before the day ended, my aunt counted all the tickets and the money from the day. She said the number of visitors was about the same as last week.

On the way home, my aunt said her job is hard but interesting because she meets so many different people. I said I would like to visit her again soon.

How Roadside Parking Colors Work

短文
語速：

In many cities, parking spaces along the road are painted with different colors. Each color has a different rule, and drivers must learn what every color means.

A red line means no parking at any time. If a driver parks next to a red line, workers may move the car away, and the driver must pay a fine.

A yellow line usually means a car can stop for a short time to pick up or drop off things, but it should not stay there for long.

A white line often means free parking, but some white spaces still have a time limit, so drivers should check the sign nearby before they leave the car.

Blue spaces usually need money. Drivers park the car first, and then they pay through a machine or an app on their phone in a short time.

Many cities now use an app, not paper tickets. A driver can type in the space number and pay with one touch on the phone.

Although the color system looks simple, many new drivers still make mistakes at first. Reading the small signs near each space is very important.

Despite these small rules, the color system helps a city stay organized. It also helps more people find a parking space more quickly and in a fair way.

A Trail for Watching Birds

短文
語速：

Near our town, there is a quiet trail through the forest. Many people call it the bird-watching trail because so many kinds of birds live along this path all year.

Early morning is the best time to visit the trail. The birds are usually more active before the sun gets too hot, and the forest is calm and quiet at that time.

Visitors do not need much equipment for this trail. A pair of binoculars is helpful, but some people just use their eyes and ears to find the birds among the trees.

Along the trail, there are several signs with pictures of common birds. These signs help visitors learn the names of the birds and remember a few facts about each one.

In spring, many small birds build nests near the trail. Visitors should stay on the path and speak quietly, so they do not disturb the birds or their eggs.

Some visitors bring a notebook to write down every bird they see. Other visitors just enjoy walking slowly and listening to the different songs in the trees.

The trail is free for everyone, and it stays open most days of the year. Local guides sometimes lead a group tour on weekends for people who want more information.

Many visitors say a walk on this trail makes them feel calm and happy. It is a simple way to enjoy nature without going far from the town.

A Morning by the Creek

短文
語速：

Every month, my grandfather takes me to a small creek near his village. He says the creek is famous for its smooth, colorful stones, and many visitors come there every weekend to look for special ones.

When we get there, several people are already walking along the water with baskets. My grandfather often picks up a wide gray stone and tells me that stones like this one can skip far across the water.

I look for stones with interesting colors. Most of the stones are gray or brown, but a few are white or pink. I always find one small stone with a red line across it, and I like it right away.

My grandfather says that not every stone in the creek is special. Some stones are too rough, and others are too weak, and they break right away. He teaches me to choose stones that feel smooth and heavy in my hand.

After an hour, I usually have a small bag of stones, but my grandfather almost has none because he gives most of his stones to other children at the creek. He says it is more fun to give than to keep everything.

At noon, we always sit by the water and eat rice balls. My grandfather often tells me an old story about a boy who finds a special stone in this creek. I ask if the story is true, and he just smiles.

Before we leave, I put my favorite stone in my pocket. My grandfather says I can come back next month, and maybe I will find another special one. I cannot wait for our next trip.

A Letter About the Mangrove Forest

Dear Grandma,

I visit the mangrove forest near the river with my science class every spring. Our teacher says a good time to visit is at high tide, so we always go there in the afternoon.

When we get there, the water almost covers the roots of the trees. Our teacher explains that mangrove trees have special roots that can live in salt water, and few other trees can do that.

I also see a few birds standing on the branches. Each bird waits without moving and then catches a small fish with its long beak. It is wonderful to watch them work so patiently.

Our teacher tells us that mangrove forests are important for the whole town. They keep the ground from washing away, and they also give many animals a safe place to live.

I take some pictures for you, and I will bring them when I visit next month. I hope you are well, and I really want to tell you more about this special place.

Love,

Mia

Why Cherry Blossoms Do Not Stay Long

短文
語速：

Every spring, many people walk along the cherry blossom path near the old temple to see the pink flowers. The path is most beautiful for only about ten days each year.

A cherry tree grows small flowers called blossoms before it grows any leaves. Each blossom has five soft pink petals, and a full tree can have thousands of them.

Visitors often ask why the flowers do not stay long. The answer is simple: the petals are thin and light, so wind and rain can make them fall very easily.

Warm weather also makes the blossoms open and fall faster. If the days become hot too early, the flowers may only stay for about a week.

When the wind blows, hundreds of petals can fall from the trees at the same time. Some people say it looks like pink snow falling in the air.

After the flowers fall, small green leaves begin to grow instead. The path looks very different in summer, with green trees instead of pink ones.

Many visitors take pictures under the trees before the flowers fall. Local workers also plant new cherry trees along the path almost every year.

Even though the cherry blossoms do not stay long, people return to the same path every spring. For many visitors, this short and beautiful time is worth the wait.

Rain at the Trailhead Pavilion

短文
語速：

Every weekend, Leo and his father like to walk up a mountain trail near their town. They usually plan to reach the top before noon and eat lunch there.

Halfway up the trail, there is a small pavilion where hikers can rest. It has a roof made of wood and a few benches for people to sit on.

This Saturday, just as Leo and his father sit down, dark clouds come in quickly, and rain starts to fall. Leo feels a little worried because they still have a long way to go.

"Let's wait here for a while," his father says. "The pavilion keeps us dry, and the rain will probably stop soon." Leo agrees and opens his bag.

While they wait, they eat some bread together and watch the rain fall on the leaves. An old man with a wooden cane joins them under the roof.

The old man says he walks this trail every week. He talks about a deer that he sometimes sees near the top of the mountain in the morning.

After about twenty minutes, the rain gets lighter, and soon it stops completely. The sky slowly turns bright again, and birds start to sing.

Leo and his father thank the old man and continue up the trail. The path is a little wet, but the air feels fresh and clean.

They finally reach the top just before one o'clock. The scene from the top is beautiful, and Leo feels proud that they do not give up in the rain.

Why Towns Sweep Autumn Leaves

短文
語速：

Every autumn, the leaves on many trees turn yellow, orange, and red before they fall to the ground. This natural change happens because the weather becomes cool and the days grow short.

When leaves fall, they often cover sidewalks, roads, and gardens. If nobody sweeps them away, the leaves can become wet and slippery, and this is dangerous on rainy days.

In many towns, families work together every weekend in October and November to sweep the leaves on the ground. They use a rake and large bags to collect the leaves quickly.

Sweeping leaves is not only about safety. It also keeps the town looking clean and tidy. Some people say a clean street makes everyone feel happy and calm.

After the leaves are collected, many towns do not throw them away. Instead, they turn the leaves into compost, which helps the garden grow healthy plants in the spring.

Some schools also join this effort. Students bring gloves and small bags to help teachers clean the school. It teaches them to care for their environment together.

Leaf sweeping can be hard work, but many families enjoy the fresh autumn air while they work. They often talk and laugh together, which makes the job feel much shorter.

By the end of the season, the streets and schools look clean again, ready for winter. This simple autumn tradition brings neighbors close together and keeps the town safe and clean.

Making Tangyuan for Winter Solstice

短文
語速：

Every winter, Ethan's family gathers at the house of his grandmother to celebrate the Winter Solstice. It is one of the busy, warm days that the whole family enjoys together.

In the morning, Ethan's grandmother mixes rice flour with warm water to make sticky dough. She teaches Ethan and his cousins how to roll the dough into small, round balls called tangyuan.

Some tangyuan are plain and white, but others are colored pink with a few drops of food coloring. Ethan likes to make the pink ones because they look pretty and happy on the plate.

His little cousin often makes the balls too big or too small, so grandmother shows her again how to roll them the same size. Everyone laughs when a ball rolls off the table.

By noon, hundreds of tangyuan sit on big plates, waiting to be cooked. Grandmother boils a big pot of water and drops the balls in slowly, a few at a time.

The tangyuan float to the top when they are ready. Grandmother puts them into bowls of warm, sweet ginger soup, and steam rises from each bowl like a small cloud.

The family sits together at the table and eats the warm tangyuan with joy. Grandmother says that eating tangyuan on this special day means the family will stay close all year.

After dinner, Ethan helps his grandmother wash the pots and bowls. He feels thankful for this simple tradition and hopes his own children will make tangyuan with him one day.
