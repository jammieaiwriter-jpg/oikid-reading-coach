One Dollar at the Fish Pond

短文
語速：

My classmate Mia loves animals. Every Sunday, she goes to the park with her little brother, Paul. They like to feed the fish in the pond near the swings.

Mia never buys new bread for the fish. She keeps the bread that her family does not eat during the week. She says, It is better to feed the fish than to waste good food.

Last month, Mia's family joined a school event called Fight Hunger Week. Every day, they thought about people in Taiwan who do not have enough food. Mia decided to help in her own way.

Now, Mia puts one dollar into a small box every time she feeds the fish. After a month, she has almost fifty dollars. She wants to give the money to a charity that helps hungry families.

Paul asks her, Why do you only give a little money each time? Mia says, A little money every week can become a big gift. It is the same as one fish becoming many fish in the pond!

Next time we go to the park, I want to bring my own box, too. Maybe our whole class can help the charity together, one dollar at a time.

One Small Package, One Small Gift

短文
語速：

Last week, my classmate Ella went to the post office to mail a small package. Inside the box were old toys and books for a charity that helps children far away. While she waited in line, a worker said something interesting to her.

Every year, people around the world waste almost one billion tons of food. That means a family throws away food almost every week without knowing it.

At the same time, millions of people do not have enough food to eat. Many charity groups try to fight this problem. They collect extra food from stores and give it to hungry families.

In Taiwan, some schools also help. They have events like Fight Hunger Week, meat-free lunch days, and food drives. Students learn that even a small can of food can help someone.

The worker at the post office says, Every package we send can be a small gift for someone else. Ella thinks about this on her way home.

What an interesting day! Ella learns that helping does not need to be a big or expensive thing. You can send one box, give one dollar, or eat one meat-free lunch to help fight hunger.

A Letter from the Bus Stop

信件
語速：

Dear Ryan,

I am writing this letter at the bus stop. It is raining hard today, so I wait here for almost twenty minutes. I have some time, so I want to tell you about my week.

My school starts a new event called Fight Hunger Week. Every day this week, we learn about hunger in Taiwan and around the world. On Wednesday, we even have a meat-free lunch!

On Friday, my class works for two hours after school. We make small bags of rice for a charity. The charity gives the rice to hungry families every weekend.

Do you know a charity near your house? I think it is a good idea to help, even a little. My family gives some money every month, and it makes us feel happy.

The rain stops now, and my bus is here. I will write again next week and tell you how much money our school gives to the charity. Take care, and say hi to your family!

Your friend,

Max

Why Fruit Prices Change with the Weather

短文
語速：

My name is Ella. Every weekend, I go to the small grocery store near my home to help my mom buy vegetables and fruit. One day, I ask the store owner why fruit prices change so often.

The owner explains an interesting fact about Taiwan's weather. Different seasons bring different weather, and this changes what fruit farmers can grow. In spring, the weather is warm and a little rainy, which is good for strawberries.

In summer, the weather becomes hot, and sometimes a big storm comes. Farmers grow watermelons and mangoes because these fruits like hot weather. Prices for cold-season fruit go up because it is harder to grow them now.

In winter, the weather turns cold, and some mountain areas even get a little snow. This is a good season for oranges and other cold-weather fruit. Prices for summer fruit go up instead, because it is not their season.

The owner says people who understand the weather can guess which fruit will be cheap in each season. She always tells customers, Buy fruit that matches today's weather, and you will save money at the store.

Now, every time I count my change at the small grocery store, I remember her lesson. Weather is not only about rain or sunshine; it also changes what is on the shelves and in our wallets.

New in Town, New Winter Coat

短文
語速：

I am Ted. I am new at this school. My family moves here in September, and I do not know many people yet. My new classmate Zoe takes me to a sporting goods store near the school after class.

The store is big, and it sells clothes for every season. There are shelves of summer T-shirts, spring jackets, and thick winter coats. Zoe says, You need a warm coat, Ted. Winter here is very cold and windy.

A worker helps us find a nice coat. It is not too heavy, and the color is dark blue. Zoe says the style looks great on me. I try it on, and it feels warm right away.

Zoe also shows me some winter shoes. She says her old shoes get wet when it is snowy, so good shoes are important in winter. We look at a few pairs and pick a strong, comfortable one.

We also look at a light jacket for spring. Zoe tells me spring here is often rainy, so a jacket like this will keep me dry when the sky turns cloudy and gray in the afternoon.

Before we leave, Zoe tells me about summer, too. She says the sun is very strong, and everything feels hot and a little dry near the mountains. I write down everything so I remember it later.

I am happy to have a new friend who helps me learn about the weather here. Every season is different, but now I feel ready for all of them. Do you have a friend who helps you in a new place, too?

A Letter from the Convenience Store

信件
語速：

Dear Sam, I am writing to you from the small convenience store near my house. It is almost ten at night, and my dad asks me to buy some milk before the store closes.

Outside, the wind is very strong, and rain starts to fall fast. The store worker says a big storm is coming, and everyone should stay inside tonight.

There is a robot in the store! It can scan drinks and tell you the price. The robot also speaks two languages, English and Chinese, which I think is very special.

While I wait for the rain to stop a little, I look at the different snacks. I want to try a salty one, but Dad says a sweet one tastes better after a cold, windy walk home.

The store worker gives me a free hot drink because the weather is so bad tonight. She says, Everyone needs something warm on a night like this!

I run home in the rain with my milk and my free drink. My hair is a little wet, but I feel warm inside. Write back soon and tell me about your weather, too! Your friend, Iris

Small Bugs, Big Teamwork

短文
語速：

Ants are small bugs, but they do big things. Most ants live together in a large group. Some people call this group a family, and it can have thousands of ants.

Every ant has a job. Worker ants look for food every day. When a worker finds food, she leaves a smell trail on the ground. Other ants follow the trail back to the food.

Some ants carry food that is many times bigger than their own body. They are not strong alone, but they work together, so the job feels easy for the whole group.

Under the ground, ants build many tunnels. The tunnels are like small streets and rooms. Ants use the tunnels to keep food, sleep, and take care of baby ants.

One special ant lives in the tunnels, too. People call her the queen. She does not look for food like the other ants. Her job is different: she helps the family grow bigger.

Ants do not talk with words like people do. They use smell and touch to share information. When one ant finds danger, she can tell other ants very fast.

The next time you see a small ant on the street, watch it for a minute. That one small bug is part of a busy team, and every ant has an important job to do.

A Letter from My Cousin

信件

Dear Iris,

The party starts at two o'clock in the afternoon. We will have games, music, and a big cake. My mom asks, How many guests do you want? I say, About twenty, please!

This year, I turn thirteen years old. My dad wants to make a colorful cake with my name on it. My mom will decorate the yard with balloons and lights.

Can you come to my party? Please tell me before the first Friday of April so I know how many guests to plan for. I really wish you can come!

I want to see you and have a fun day together. Next time, maybe we can plan a party for your birthday, too!

Love, Nora

The Fourth Act in the Talent Show

短文
語速：

My name is Owen. My class has a talent show on the first Friday of March. I am the fourth student to go on stage, right after my friend Kate.

Before the show, I feel nervous. I hold my guitar and practice one more song. My hands feel a little cold, but I want to do my best for my class.

Kate asks me, What is your act? I say, I can sing and play the guitar. She says, That is great! I want to hear it. Good luck, Owen!

Kate goes on stage first. She dances to a fast song, and everyone in the audience claps for her. She smiles a big smile when she comes back.

Then it is my turn. I walk on stage and start to sing. My hands are still a little cold, but I do not forget any of the words to my song.

When I finish, the whole room claps for me, too! My teacher says, Great job, Owen! I feel very proud, and my nervous feeling is finally gone.

After the show, the teacher tells us one hundred people come to watch this year. What a fun day for our class! Next year, I want to write my own song for the show.

Counting Bowls at the Alley Noodle Stand

短文
語速：

Every Friday night, I help my family at our small noodle stand at the end of the alley. The smell of hot soup always fills the street before we even open.

My job is easy but important. How many bowls do we need tonight? I count the clean bowls on the shelf. Tonight, we have forty bowls ready to use.

My friend Ryan comes to help, too. He asks, How much noodle soup do we make every night? My dad says we make a big pot, enough for about sixty bowls.

At seven o'clock, customers start to come. An old man asks, How many dumplings come with one bowl of noodles? I tell him five dumplings come with each bowl.

A woman named Ella orders two bowls and a small plate of vegetables. She asks how much it is. My mom checks the price list and tells her the total.

By nine o'clock, we only have ten bowls left. My dad says, How much soup is still in the pot? Not much, so we know we will close early tonight.

I like counting bowls and talking to customers. When the last bowl is sold, the smell of hot soup is still in the air, just like when we first opened tonight.

My First Ferry Ride

短文
語速：

Last weekend, my family and I take a ferry across the river for the first time. My friend Hank tells me a ferry is like a big boat bus for people and cars.

Before we get on, my mom asks the worker, How much is a ticket for a child? He says a child ticket is thirty dollars, and an adult ticket is sixty dollars.

My sister Gina asks, How many people can this ferry carry at one time? The worker says the ferry can carry about two hundred people and twenty cars.

We stand near the front and watch the workers work. How many minutes does the trip take? Hank says it usually takes about fifteen minutes to cross the river.

On the ferry, I see a small shop. It sells coffee, juice, and bread. How much does a cup of coffee cost on the ferry? A worker says it is only twenty-five dollars.

Many people use the ferry every day, not just for fun. Some workers use it to get to their jobs. It saves them a lot of time compared to a long walk.

I also learn something new. A ferry uses less gas than many small boats because it carries so many people and cars together at once.

When we get off the ferry, I feel excited. What a fun day this is! I believe I want to ride the ferry again very soon.

Winning at the Ring Toss Stall

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Toby: Look, Ivy, a ring toss stall! Let's try it before we go home.

Ivy: Sure! How much does one turn cost?

Toby: The man says one turn is fifty dollars. How many rings do we get for that?

Ivy: He says we get five rings for fifty dollars. That is not too much.

Toby: Okay, let's try. How many bottles do we need to hit to win a prize?

Ivy: The sign says we need to ring three bottles. Let's see how many you can get!

Toby: I only ring one bottle. How many rings do I have left?

Ivy: You have two rings left. Try again, slowly this time.

Toby: I got it! I ring two more bottles. Now I have three. Do I win a prize?

Ivy: Yes! The man says you can choose a small toy. How many toys can you pick?

Toby: He says I can only pick one. I choose the little green frog.

Ivy: That is so cute. Next time, I want to try the ring toss, too!

Toby: Next time, we can both play. Maybe we will win two prizes together!

The Quiet Fish in the Class Tank

短文
語速：

Every classroom at our school has a job list. Ken's class has a special job: taking care of the class fish tank. Hank is this week's fish helper.

Every morning, Hank comes to school early to feed the fish. He always checks the water first, because fish need clean water to live.

Sometimes the water looks a little dirty, and Hank asks the teacher for help. The teacher shows him how to clean it the right way.

One day, the teacher notices something interesting. One small fish never eats with the others. It usually swims alone near the plants.

Ken sits close to the tank, so he often watches this fish during class. "Why does that fish swim alone?" Ken asks.

The teacher says some fish are just quiet, like some students. Everyone laughs, because Ken is also very quiet in class.

Hank works hard every day. He never forgets to feed the fish, even on busy homework days. Twice a week, he also cleans the small rocks at the bottom of the tank.

By Friday, the quiet fish finally swims with the other fish. Hank feels proud, and Ken claps his hands. "Look! It has some new friends now," Ken says.

Next time, Ken wants to be the fish helper, too. He thinks it takes real effort, but it also looks like a lot of fun.

How Often Do Students Visit the Health Room?

短文
語速：

Our school has a small health room next to the office. The school nurse works there every day, from Monday to Friday.

Many students visit the health room during the year. Some come because they feel a little sick. Others come after a small accident, like falling down during sports.

The nurse always asks the same first question: What is wrong today? Then she checks the student's temperature and asks more questions.

Paul often visits the health room in winter, because he easily gets a cold. He usually rests for ten minutes and drinks some warm water. Then he feels better and goes back to class.

Nora seldom gets sick, but she visits once after a small bike accident at school. The nurse cleans her hand carefully and gives her a bandage.

The nurse says students should not worry too much about a small visit. She notices that most students feel much better after a short rest.

She also gives some simple advice: drink enough water every day, do not skip breakfast, and tell a teacher early if something feels wrong.

What a helpful room our school has! It helps many students feel better and go back to learning.

Dear Ella,

How are you? I am writing to tell you about my busy week.

Every Tuesday after school, I go to the stationery store near my house. I usually buy a new pen there, because I lose my pens so often!

I am also on the school swim team now. We train twice a week, and I seldom miss a practice, even when I am tired.

Your pen-loving friend,

Ivy

The Little Blue Bikes in the City

短文
語速：

I am Kate, a new student in this city. Last week, I go to a stationery store to buy pens for school. My classmate Ken goes with me.

Outside the store, I see a row of little blue bikes. Ken says, "Those are city bikes. Anyone can ride one!"

Ken tells me how it works. You use your phone or a card at the station. Then you can take a bike and ride it to another station.

Many people use these bikes to go to work or school. Some ride to the metro station, and then they take the train the rest of the way.

The bike stations are all over the city, often at a corner or next to a park. You do not need your own bike at home.

This helps the city in a few ways. Fewer cars on the road means the air is cleaner. It also means people get some exercise on the way to work.

Ken says the bikes are cheap, too. The first thirty minutes are almost free. After that, it costs a little more money for each hour.

On rainy days, fewer people use the bikes because it can be dangerous to ride in the rain. Most riders wait for a clear day.

After we buy our pens, Ken and I decide to try the little blue bikes together. We ride past the bakery and the library, feeling the wind on our faces.

Now, every time I see a little blue bike outside a store, I think of my first ride with Ken.

Sunset Talk on the Rooftop

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Ivy: Look at the sky, Zoe! It turns orange and pink every evening up here.

Zoe: It's so pretty. This rooftop is my favorite place after art club.

Ivy: How do you usually go home after we watch the sunset?

Zoe: I take the metro. The station is only two blocks from school.

Ivy: I ride my bike home. It takes about fifteen minutes if I go straight along the river road.

Zoe: Isn't it dangerous to ride a bike when the sky gets dark so fast?

Ivy: A little, but I always wear a light on my bike, so cars can see me.

Zoe: That's a good idea. My mom says I should never walk home alone after dark.

Ivy: Right, and on rainy days, I don't ride at all. I wait for my dad and go home by car.

Zoe: Sounds smart. Hey, do you want to come to my house for dinner tonight?

Ivy: Sure! Should I take the metro with you, or do you want to walk?

Zoe: Let's walk. It's not far, and the weather is clear tonight.

Ivy: Great. Do you want to watch the sunset from the rooftop again tomorrow, too?

Zoe: Yes! Let's meet here right after class.
