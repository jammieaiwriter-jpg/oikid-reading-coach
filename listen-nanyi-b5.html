An Afternoon at the Ice Shop

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Ken: Wow, it's so hot today. Let's stop at the ice shop.

Ling: Good idea! I have never had their mango ice before.

Ken: You should try it. It's the most popular type here.

Ling: What toppings do they have?

Ken: They have red beans, taro balls, peanuts, and sweet milk.

Ling: I'll get mango ice with red beans and taro balls, please.

Ken: I'll have the same, but with peanuts, not red beans.

Ling: This shop has been open since I was a little kid.

Ken: Really? My mom says it's even older than that.

Ling: The old lady behind the counter has worked here for over thirty years!

Ken: She always remembers what my family orders.

Ling: That's so nice. Oh look, our ice is ready!

Ken: Let's find a table before it melts.

Ling: Good idea. This will be so refreshing.

How the Balloon-Shooting Game Works

短文
語速：

Balloon shooting is a popular game at Taiwanese night markets. Players use a small toy gun that shoots small darts at rows of colorful balloons on a wood board.

This stall has its own rules, but most games work the same way. A player pays a little money for eight or ten shots. Every balloon a player pops earns points or a small prize.

The balloons are not all the same size. Small balloons are hard to pop, so they earn more points. Big balloons are easy to pop, so they earn fewer points.

This stall also has special balloons with a number inside. If a player pops one, the player can trade the number for a big prize, like a stuffed toy or a snack.

Many players say the game looks easy, but it is not. The toy gun is often old, and the sight is not always straight. Good players learn to aim a little to one side.

Night market workers say children and adults both enjoy this game. It does not take much time to play, but it takes practice to become good at it. That is why many people come back to try again.

Scooping Goldfish at the Night Market

短文
語速：

Every Friday night, my cousin and I walk to the night market near our house. My favorite game there is catching goldfish with a paper scoop. A worker gives me a thin paper scoop and a small bowl of water.

The paper scoop is weak, and it can tear fast if it stays in the water too long. I have to move fast and stay gentle at the same time. Some nights, I catch three goldfish before my scoop tears.

My cousin is not very good at this game. He always pushes the scoop too hard, and it tears right away. He has tried many times, but he has never caught even one goldfish.

Tonight, a young boy next to us catches a big orange goldfish. He looks so happy that he jumps and shakes, looking so excited. The worker gives him a small plastic bag with water for his new pet.

I try again. I move my scoop with care under a small goldfish. My hand shakes a little, but I do not give up. Finally, I lift the scoop, and there is a tiny goldfish inside!

My cousin claps for me and smiles. We put the goldfish in a bag and walk around the market together. I feel proud, and I already want to come back next Friday to try again.

How a Breakfast Griddle Works

短文
語速：

Many breakfast shops in Taiwan use a big flat metal griddle to cook food. It is hot and round or square in shape, and it can cook many things at the same time.

The shop owner turns on the griddle early in the morning. It takes only a few minutes to get very hot, and then the cook can start to cook eggs, meat, and toast.

The heat under the griddle comes from gas. A worker controls a small knob to turn the fire up or down, so the food does not burn on one side.

First, a thin layer of oil is put on the griddle. This stops the food from sticking to the metal, and it also gives the food a nice golden color.

Some shops cook eggs, sausages, and bread on the same griddle at the same time. The cook must move fast and turn each item at the right moment.

A good griddle can stay hot for many hours without much extra gas. That is why so many small breakfast shops use one every single day.

After the shop closes, someone cleans the griddle with care. A clean griddle helps the food taste good the next morning and keeps the metal in good shape for years.

A Busy Night at the Roast Meat Shop

短文
語速：

My uncle owns a roast meat shop, and it gets very busy every evening. Workers walk in with their lunch boxes, and the delicious smell of roast pork fills the street outside.

My uncle can chop the meat very quickly with a big knife. He always puts extra sauce on top because he knows most customers love a strong, sweet taste with their rice.

Last night, a young woman came in during the busy hour. She was in a hurry because her train would leave soon, so my uncle packed her lunch box first.

An old man waited patiently near the counter. He has visited this shop for twenty years, and he always orders the same thing: roast duck with rice and vegetables.

My little cousin helps too. She puts each lunch box into a paper bag and hands it to the customer with a smile. Everyone says she is very polite.

By nine o'clock, very little meat was left. My uncle cleaned his hands and finally took a short rest after a long, tiring, but happy day.

I asked my uncle why he never gets tired of this work. He said he loves seeing hungry customers leave with a full lunch box and a happy face.

A Letter from the Train Platform

信件
語速：

Dear Leo,

I am writing to you from the train platform right now! My train is late, so I have some time to write a short letter.

I got here thirty minutes early because I did not want to miss my train. But the sign says my train is now twenty minutes late because of a small problem on the tracks.

While I wait, I am watching so many people. An old woman is reading a newspaper on a bench, and two small children are running along the platform while their mother calls them back.

A large group of students just arrived with big bags. They are going on a school trip, and they all look very excited about it.

I bought a cold drink from a small shop near the platform because I felt thirsty. It is quite warm today, even though it is almost autumn.

My train should arrive soon, so I need to stop writing now. I will call you when I get home tonight.

Your friend,

Ryan

Changing Buses at the Transfer Station

短文
語速：

Every month, I visit my aunt in another town by bus, and I always change buses at a big transfer station on the way. The station is busy, and at first it feels hard to understand.

Inside, there are many gates with numbers, and a big screen shows the bus times. My bus usually leaves from gate twelve, but the gates are easy to mix up.

Once, I could not find gate twelve, so I asked a worker in a blue uniform for help. She points down a long hall and says, Walk straight, then turn right.

I hurry down the hall, past a small shop that sells drinks and snacks. My heart beats fast because the bus leaves in only five minutes.

When I finally reach gate twelve, the driver almost closes the door. I wave my hand and call out, and he waits for me in a kind way.

I sit down, very tired, and watch the station grow smaller through the window. I am happy, and I almost forget to thank the driver.

Now I always leave home early when I have to change buses. That trip teaches me that a big station can feel hard to understand, but a friendly worker can help so much.

The Shuttle Boat to the Island

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Leo: Look, Nora! The shuttle boat is finally here. This is my first time on a boat to the island.

Nora: Mine too! I hear the ride only takes about fifteen minutes.

Captain: Welcome! Please sit down and hold on well. The sea can feel a little wild today.

Leo: Wow, the wind is strong! Is it safe to stand near the front?

Captain: It is safe to stay seated until we leave the harbor. After that, you can walk around a little.

Nora: Captain, how many times does this boat go to the island and come back each day?

Captain: About eight times. Fishers use it early in the morning, and visitors like you usually ride in the afternoon.

Leo: I can see fishing boats near the rocks! Are they catching fish right now?

Captain: Yes, this area has many fish in the morning. By afternoon, most fishers already sell their catch at the market.

Nora: That is so interesting. I do not know a small harbor could be this busy.

Captain: This kind of small harbor is the heart of the whole town. Everything starts here.

Leo: Thanks for the ride, Captain! I hope we can come back again next year.

Why Small Repair Shops Matter

短文
語速：

In Taiwan, many people ride a scooter to work, to school, or to the market. Because so many people ride every day, small repair shops appear on almost every street corner.

A mechanic at a repair shop checks the engine, the wheels, and the oil. If a scooter makes a strange sound or feels weak, people bring it to the shop right away.

Most people visit a repair shop every few months, even if nothing seems wrong. This regular check helps the mechanic find small problems before they become large, expensive ones.

While customers wait, they usually sit on a chair near the shop and read a magazine, use their phone, or talk with other customers about their day.

A simple oil change takes only about twenty minutes, but large repairs can take an hour or more. The mechanic always explains the problem before he starts the work.

Good mechanics also give people free advice, such as how to keep a scooter clean or how to make the engine last longer without any large repair.

Because scooters are so important for daily life in Taiwan, these small repair shops play a big part in every area, and many people trust the same shop for years.

A Letter About the New Bike Path

信件
語速：

Dear Mia,

How are you? I want to tell you about the new bike path near my house. It opened just last weekend, right before the long holiday.

On Saturday morning, my brother and I rode our bikes along the whole path. It goes past a small pond, under a row of tall trees, and along the edge of a quiet park.

So many families were out riding together. Some parents pulled small carts with young children inside, and a few groups even stopped to have a picnic near the water.

Halfway through the ride, it started to rain a little, so we waited under a bridge with a group of other riders. Everyone talked and laughed until the rain stopped.

By the afternoon, my legs were tired, but I felt happy. My brother said we should return every holiday, and I think that is a wonderful idea.

Do you have a bike path like this near your home? I hope you can visit and ride with us the next time you come to my city.

Your friend,

Ren

Why Bus Drivers Say Hello

短文
語速：

In many cities, bus drivers greet each rider with a short hello or a small nod. This simple habit seems tiny, but it can make a bus ride feel more friendly.

Some cities even train new drivers to greet riders in a warm and friendly way. Bus companies believe a friendly greeting can lower stress for both the driver and the riders during a busy trip.

A short greeting can also help safety. When a driver looks at each rider for a second, the driver stays more alert and less tired during a long day.

Different riders act in different ways when a driver says hello. Some smile and say hello back, while others are shy and only nod. Either way, most riders say a greeting makes the bus feel less like a machine.

Older riders often enjoy greetings the most. For some, a short hello from the driver might be the first friendly word they hear all morning, especially if they live alone.

A few cities choose a favorite bus driver every year and give a small prize. Riders send in short stories about drivers who remembered their names or waited a few extra seconds for them.

Not every driver finds greeting easy. Long hours and heavy traffic can make a smile hard to keep. Still, many drivers say a quick hello makes them feel good too.

In the end, a simple greeting costs nothing, but it can turn a short bus ride into a small, friendly moment between two strangers who might never meet again.

Mr. Wu, the Friendly Taxi Driver

短文
語速：

In this city, there is a taxi driver everyone calls Mr. Wu. He has been a taxi driver here for more than fifteen years and always starts work early, before the sun comes up.

Every morning, Mr. Wu picks up his first customer near the train station. Many passengers are students on their way to school or workers heading to early meetings downtown.

Mr. Wu likes to talk with his customers. He asks about their day, their families, and sometimes even their favorite food. Most passengers say his taxi feels warm and friendly.

One rainy afternoon, an old woman got into his taxi with a heavy bag of food. Mr. Wu took the bag from her and helped her walk with care to her front door.

Mr. Wu also remembers small facts about his regular passengers. He knows which street a certain teacher lives on, and he always keeps a bottle of water ready for a tired nurse.

Sometimes traffic gets very slow, and passengers worry that they will be late. Mr. Wu stays calm and looks for a shorter road, so most passengers still arrive close to on time.

Last month, a young man left his wallet inside the taxi by mistake. Mr. Wu went back to the same street corner and waited almost an hour until the man returned.

The young man was surprised and said thank you again and again. He offered Mr. Wu some money, but Mr. Wu said returning someone's wallet was just the right thing to do.

After many years on the road, Mr. Wu still enjoys his job. He says every ride tells a small story, and he feels proud to be part of so many lives every single day.

The Bridge That Wouldn't Move

短文
語速：

Last Friday, our school bus got stuck on the long bridge that crosses the river near my house. It was almost six o'clock, and the cars on the bridge could not move at all.

At first, everyone on the bus stayed quiet. Then a boy near the window said he could see a fruit vendor between the cars, selling cold drinks and bags of fruit to the drivers.

Some drivers rolled down their windows and bought a drink from the vendor. Other drivers just stayed in their seats and waited.

A man on a motorcycle wanted to weave between the rows of cars, but there was no space at all. He finally turned off his engine and waited like everyone else.

My friend Leo was beside me, and he explained that his father once said this bridge gets busy every evening because two highways meet in the middle of it.

After waiting for a while, I could hear a loud honk far ahead. Someone said there was a small accident, and that was the reason the whole bridge slowed down.

Little by little, the truck went to the side, and the cars in front of us started to move again. The bus driver was happy and said, "Finally, we can go home."

When we crossed the rest of the bridge, the sky was already turning orange. I looked back at the bridge and could not stop thinking about the other people still waiting there.

Now, every time our bus crosses that bridge, I always look out the window first, just to check whether the road ahead looks clear or not.

What Really Happens at a Gas Station

短文
語速：

A gas station is a busy place where drivers stop their cars to buy fuel. Most stations have several pumps, and each pump has a long hose with a nozzle at the end.

At many stations, an attendant helps each driver. The attendant asks what type of gasoline the car needs, opens the fuel tank, and puts the nozzle inside with care.

While the tank fills, the attendant often checks the oil and the tires for free. Drivers usually stay in their cars and wait, and they pay by cash or card when the job is done.

Some newer stations use self-service pumps instead. Drivers choose the fuel type on a small screen, hold the nozzle themselves, and press a button to start the pump.

Every gas station follows clear safety rules. Drivers must turn off the engine before the tank opens, and nobody can smoke near the pumps, because fuel catches fire very fast.

Workers also wear special gloves and clothes to stay safe. One small mistake near the pumps can cause a big accident, so everyone follows the rules with care.

At night, bright lights stay on above the pumps so that drivers can see well. Many stations also stay open twenty-four hours a day, so drivers can stop at any time.

Next time you pass a gas station, take a look at the pumps, the signs, and the workers there. There is more going on than most people ever stop to see.

Inside an Airport Waiting Lounge

短文
語速：

An airport waiting lounge is a large room near the gates where passengers wait before their flight. It usually has rows of chairs, big windows, and screens that show flight information.

Each lounge has a screen that lists the flight number, the gate, and whether the flight is on time or late. Passengers check this screen often so they do not miss their boarding call.

Every lounge also has small shops that sell snacks, drinks, and magazines. A traveler who feels hungry before a long flight can buy food there before the flight.

A newer lounge often has a place to charge a phone. Anyone whose phone battery is low can find a plug near the seats and use it while waiting.

During busy hours, the lounge can become crowded, and almost no seat is free. Passengers with young children often look for a quiet corner away from the noise.

Staff members speak more than one language so that every traveler can understand when it is time to board. They walk around to answer questions and help passengers who feel lost.

Even though waiting can feel boring, many people use the time to read a book, rest, or talk with family. A good lounge makes the whole waiting time feel shorter.

A Letter About the Birdwatching Trail

信件
語速：

Dear Mia,

I hope you are doing well! Last weekend, my family went to a nature trail near Blue Hill Park, and I want to tell you about it.

The trail is a quiet path in the forest. My father, whose hobby is bird photos, took his big camera and a pair of binoculars with him.

We saw more birds than we expected. One bright green bird had a call that sounded like a small bell. A guide near the start of the trail explained the habits of each bird.

There is a small rest area in the middle of the trail, where people can sit and eat a snack. We stayed there and ate for about thirty minutes.

The whole walk took almost two hours, but I did not feel bored at all. Every part of the trail showed us something new, except one long part with no birds at all.

I think you would enjoy this trail too, especially in the morning, when the birds sing the most. Maybe we can go together next month?

Write back soon and tell me about your trip to the bookstore!

Your friend, Wei-Lin

The Crossing Guard Every Morning

短文
語速：

Every morning before school, an old man named Mr. Ho stands beside the crosswalk near Sunshine Elementary. He wears a bright yellow vest and holds up a small flag whenever students need to cross the busy street.

Mr. Ho works as a volunteer because he misses talking with young people. He says that helping students cross the street in a safe way gives him something important to do each day.

Some drivers do not slow down right away, so Mr. Ho always steps out first and lifts his flag high. Students trust him because he never lets anyone cross until it is really safe.

One rainy morning, a boy with a big umbrella almost walks into the street without looking. Mr. Ho catches his arm fast and pulls him back.

After that morning, the boy always waves to Mr. Ho and says thank you before he crosses the street. Other students begin doing the same thing, and soon Mr. Ho knows the name of almost every child.

Even on cold days, Mr. Ho comes early and waits with a smile. He says a few minutes outside are nothing next to keeping every student safe.

Each year, the school gives Mr. Ho a small thank-you card from the whole class. He says it is the nicest gift he gets.

Now, whenever new students join the school, older classmates tell them about the kind man at the crosswalk whose flag means it is finally safe to cross.
