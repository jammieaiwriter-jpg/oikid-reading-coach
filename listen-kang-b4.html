Ray's First Singing Contest

短文
語速：

Every summer, our community holds a singing contest in the park near my house. This year, my neighbor Ray decides to join for the first time.

Ray practices every evening after dinner. He picks a slow song about friendship, and he sings it in front of his bedroom mirror again and again.

On the night of the contest, a big stage stands in the middle of the park. Many families bring chairs and sit close together to watch.

When Ray's name is called, he walks up with a microphone in his hand. His hands shake a little, but he takes a deep breath first.

Ray sings clearly, and his voice sounds warmer than it does at home. The crowd claps loudly, and some people even stand up and cheer for him.

After all ten singers finish, three judges talk quietly and then choose the winner. Ray does not win first place, but he wins a small trophy for "Most Improved Singer."

Ray smiles and says the trophy means more to him than winning first place would. Next year, he plans to practice even harder and try again.

What Happens at a Temple Parade

短文
語速：

In Taiwan, many towns hold a special event called a temple parade. During a temple parade, people carry a god through the streets to bring a lucky year to the town.

A temple parade often includes a special group called a parade team. The parade team wears colorful costumes and walks with the parade from beginning to end.

Some team members in the parade team play loud drums, and others hold flags or wear big masks. Their movements and music tell an old story about brave gods and spirits.

Children in the town often stand near the road to watch. The sound of the drums and the bright costumes make the parade exciting to see.

A temple parade usually happens during a festival, such as a god's birthday or a New Year party. Families believe it brings peace and safety for the whole year.

Learning to be part of the parade team takes a long time. Young team members practice their steps and their drum beat for many months before the real parade.

Today, some schools even teach students about parade team culture, so this tradition can continue for many more years to come.

How Schools Choose a Bus for a Graduation Trip

短文
語速：

Before a graduation trip, a school must pick a good bus company. This job sounds simple, but it is actually more complex than most students think.

First, a teacher must check that the bus company has a safe record. A company with a poor safety record is never a good pick, no matter how cheap its price is.

Second, the school checks how many seats the bus has. A small bus with too few seats is not useful for a big class, so schools often need a big bus with many seats.

Third, the price is important too. A company with a high price is not always the safest, and a company with a low price is not always the most dangerous. Schools compare a few companies first.

The bus driver's driving style is very important too. A careful driver who drives at a slow, steady speed is more valuable than a driver who drives too fast to save time.

Some buses have useful equipment, such as a small radio or a screen for movies. These things are nice, but they are not as important as safety and comfort.

Teachers must also plan the trip schedule with the bus company. They should know when the bus leaves, when it stops, and when it returns to school.

A good bus company will answer every question a teacher asks, even strange or small ones. This shows the company cares about the students on the trip.

In the end, picking the right bus is not just about price. It is about finding a company that keeps every student safe and makes the whole trip a happy memory.

A Handmade Card for Mother's Day

短文
語速：

Every year before Mother's Day, my sister and I make our own card for our mom. We never buy one from a store. This year, we want to make the most special card of all.

First, we open an old drawer full of colored paper and buttons. My sister says we can use the buttons as flower centers, and I think that is a clever plan.

We also like to draw a small candle on the front, because our mom loves lighting a candle after dinner. My sister draws slower than me, but her candle always looks sweeter.

While we work, we tell each other jokes to make the afternoon more fun. My sister tells a joke about a strange singing radio, and it makes me laugh so hard that I push the glue bottle off the table.

"You should be more careful," my sister says, but she smiles at me. We pull some tissue paper from the drawer and clean the table together.

Inside the card, we write that our mom is the most useful and valuable person in our family. We say her love for us is more precious than anything money can buy.

My little brother wants to help too, but he cannot cut paper very well yet. We give him a simple job: he puts every sticker on the card with care.

When the card is finished, it looks more beautiful than the one we made last year. We agree that this memory of working together is the most special part of the whole day.

On Mother's Day morning, our mom opens the card with care and reads every word. She says it is the sweetest gift ever, and she keeps it in her favorite drawer.

A Letter About the Campfire Night

信件
語速：

Dear Mr. Wang,

I write to ask about the campfire night at the end of our summer camp. My cabin group has a few questions before we plan our part of the show.

First, is it OK if we bring a small radio to play music between songs? We think music makes the campfire feel more fun and less quiet.

Second, one of my cabin mates wants to light a candle and tell a strange, funny story about a mountain ghost. Is that too strange for the younger campers?

Third, we want to make a short, silly joke video about camp life. Should we show it on a screen, or should we just do it live by the fire?

We also want to know if our cabin can sit near the fire this year. Last year our group was far from the fire, and it was hard to feel warm on such a cold night.

Actually, our cabin leader said the fire should be the most important part of the night, more important than any radio or video. We agree, but we still hope to add a little fun.

One more thing: if it rains, will the campfire night move inside? A few campers worry a little about wet, dirty shoes.

Please let us know your answers soon so we have enough time to practice. We promise our part of the show will be useful, funny, and full of good memories!

Sincerely,

The Blue Mountain Cabin Group

Backstage Before the Speech Contest

短文
語速：

Before the school speech contest begins, all the speakers wait together in a small room. My hands feel a little cold, and my heart beats fast. Our coach, Mr. Chen, notices that many of us look nervous.

Mr. Chen explains an interesting fact: almost every person feels nervous before speaking in front of a group. He says the body makes a special chemical that makes the heart beat faster and the hands feel cold or wet.

This feeling has a name: stage fright. Even famous singers and actors say they feel stage fright before a big show, so students probably should not feel bad about it.

Mr. Chen gives us a simple trick. He says a few slow, deep breaths can help the body feel calm. Besides slow breaths, thinking about the topic, not the crowd, also helps a speaker feel calm.

He also explains that a little nervous feeling is not always bad. Some studies show that a small amount of nervous feeling can make a speaker sound more excited and full of energy, not weak.

One by one, the speakers walk to the front of the room. When it becomes my turn, I remember to take slow breaths and think about my topic, not the people in front of me.

My speech probably is not perfect, but my voice sounds clear, and my hands feel calm after some time. I notice that this nervous feeling does not stay long once a speaker starts talking.

After the contest, Mr. Chen tells us that every speaker that day felt nervous at some point. Learning this fact makes me feel less alone the next time I feel stage fright.

A Visit to the District Office

短文
語速：

In Taiwan, a district office is a small government building near your home. People go there to get many kinds of papers, such as a new ID card, a copy of a family record, or a paper that shows where they live.

Most district offices open early in the morning and close in the late afternoon. Some offices also open for a few hours on Saturday, so working people do not need to take time off during the week.

The first step at a district office is to take a number from a machine near the door. This number tells you your place in line, so you can sit and wait without standing near the counter.

While you wait, you can look at a list on the wall. It tells you which papers you need for each kind of visit. For example, a new ID card usually needs your old card, a photo, and a family record book.

When your number is called, you walk up to a window and give your papers to a worker. The worker checks your papers with care and tells you if anything is missing or wrong.

If everything is correct, the worker enters your information into a computer. Most simple visits take only ten or fifteen minutes, but a new ID card can take longer because it must be printed at another office.

Many teenagers visit a district office for the first time when they turn fourteen, because that is when they need to get their first ID card. A parent usually helps them fill out the form.

Learning how a district office works is a useful skill. It helps young people understand how their city works, and it makes them feel more responsible when they handle paperwork on their own.

The Lake Charity Ride

短文
語速：

Every October, my school holds a bike ride around the city lake to raise money for the city hospital. This year, my teacher asked me to join the team, and I said yes even though I was a little nervous.

On the morning of the ride, about two hundred people waited near the old bridge. A man with a microphone explained the safety rules before everyone started to ride around the lake together.

For the first few kilometers, I was very happy. The wind was cool, the sun was warm, and I could hear other people laughing and talking as we passed the small boats near the shore.

Halfway around the lake, the chain on my bike came off the wheel. I got off my bike under a tree, and I did not know what to do, so I stayed there and I was very nervous.

A girl from another school came over to help. She said her father taught her how to fix a bike chain, and she showed me how to use a small tool from her bag.

It took us about fifteen minutes to fix the chain together. We talked while we worked, and I learned that she joined the ride to raise money for her cousin, who was sick in the hospital.

When we finally reached the finish line, our friends and family were waiting with water and warm smiles. We were the last two people to arrive, but nobody seemed to mind at all.

That night, I said to my parents that the ride taught me something important: helping a stranger can turn a hard moment into a new friendship, and even if you finish last, you can still finish strong.

A Letter About the Post Office Line

信件
語速：

Dear Grandpa,

I hope you are well. I am sending this letter to tell you about something funny that happened at the post office yesterday.

My mom asked me to mail a birthday card to you, so I walked to the post office after school. When I got there, the line was much longer than I expected. There were at least twenty people in front of me.

I waited for almost thirty minutes. An old man in front of me was talking on the phone the whole time, and a little boy behind me asked his mother again and again when it would be his turn.

When I finally reached the counter, the worker checked the weight of my envelope and asked if I wanted to send it by regular mail or fast mail. I picked fast mail because I wanted the card to arrive before your birthday.

The worker also asked if I wanted to buy a special stamp with a picture of a bird on it. I said yes, because I thought you would like it.

After I finished paying, the worker put the envelope in a big bag with many other letters. I was happy knowing that my card was on its way to you.

I hope the card arrives in time and that you have a wonderful birthday. I can't wait to see you next month.

Love, Mia

A Big Day at the City Pool

短文
語速：

Last Saturday, I went to the city swimming pool with my cousin Ben and our neighbor Lily. It was a hot, sunny morning, and the pool area was already full of families and noisy, happy children.

Ben is younger than me and could not swim well yet, so he stayed near the shallow side. Lily and I can swim well, so we walked to the deep end to join a short swim class.

Our coach, Mr. Chen, told everyone to line up along the edge of the pool. He said, "Before you jump in, listen carefully to the safety rules, please."

He explained that swimmers should never run near the pool because the ground is wet, and wet ground can be slippery. He also said we should never push each other into the water.

Then Mr. Chen said something I will always remember. "Stay calm if something sudden happens, just like your teacher taught you during the earthquake drill at school last month."

After the safety talk, we spent almost an hour to practice simple moves in the water. During practice, Lily was faster than Ben, but Ben did not give up.

During a short break, we stayed by the pool and drank cold water. Ben said that a swim class made him feel both tired and happy at the same time.

Near the end of class, Mr. Chen let us jump from the low board one by one. It felt scary at first, but it was also very fun.

By the time we left the pool, the sun was starting to go down. We all agree that this Saturday was one of our favorite days of the whole summer.

What Can You Do at the Community Center?

短文
語速：

Many towns and cities have a community center. It is a big building where people of all ages can meet, learn new skills, and join fun activities together after school or work.

Most community centers have both a big hall and several small rooms. The big hall is used for large events, and the small rooms are used for clubs like art, music, or cooking.

In the art room, a teacher often helps children draw or paint. Some centers also let visitors use a shelf full of books, so people can read quietly if they want a break.

The cooking club is popular with both children and adults. In this club, students learn to cook simple, healthy dishes, and they often put their food on a big table to share it.

Some centers also offer a homework club for students. In this club, a leading local expert or a friendly volunteer helps children with their homework after school every weekday.

There is often a sports program too. Students can join a basketball team, a table tennis club, or a simple exercise class to stay strong and active with their neighbors.

Community centers are not only for classes. On weekends, many centers hold a special event, such as a small market, a music show, or a game night for the whole neighborhood.

People who join these events often say the center feels like a second home. It gives them a safe, friendly place where they can meet new friends and feel less alone.

Because of all these reasons, community centers are an important part of many neighborhoods. They help both children and adults learn new things and enjoy their free time together.

Why Does Popcorn Pop?

短文
語速：

If you walk into a movie theater, you often smell fresh popcorn before you even buy your ticket. Have you ever thought about why popcorn pops at all?

Popcorn is a special type of corn. Inside each kernel, there is a small amount of water and a little starch, and a hard shell covers both of them.

When the kernel gets hot, the water inside slowly turns into steam. The hard shell does not let the steam get out fast, so pressure builds up inside.

After a while, the pressure gets too strong for the shell. The shell breaks, and the kernel pops open into the white shape we call popcorn.

All of this happens in just a few seconds, though it can look like magic if you watch it inside a clear glass popper.

Movie theaters often use big popcorn machines that can make hundreds of bags in a single hour. That is why the smell fills the whole building so fast.

Not every kernel pops the same way. A kernel without enough water inside will stay hard even after it gets hot for a long time.

The next time you smell popcorn at a movie theater, you will know that water, starch, and heat make that sweet, delicious smell.

A Class Trip to the Science Museum

短文
語速：

Every spring, Ms. Chen's class visits the city science museum for a special trip. All the students feel excited because they visit that big glass building only once a year.

At the entrance, a friendly guide gives each student a small map and shows them where to find the robot exhibit, the most popular part of the museum.

In the first room, students watch a tall robot arm pick up small blocks and put them together, one by one. Many students ask the guide how the robot knows where each block is.

The guide explains that small cameras and a sensor help the robot see the blocks, almost like eyes, and a small computer inside tells the arm right where to move next.

Next, the class walks into a dark room with bright stars on the ceiling. A short movie shows how planets move around the sun, and everyone stays quiet to watch it.

The favorite part for most students is a game room, where visitors can build a small car and race it down a long ramp. Some cars are fast, but others are quite slow.

Before the class leaves, the teacher reminds everyone that science museums are full of ideas people can use in real life, not just facts for a test.

On the bus ride home, the students talk about which exhibit they enjoy the most. Almost everyone agrees that the robot arm is the coolest thing in the whole museum.
