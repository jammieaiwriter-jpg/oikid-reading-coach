Making Banana Bread in Cooking Class

短文
語速：

Every Wednesday afternoon, our class has a cooking lesson. Last week, our teacher taught us how to make a simple snack: sweet banana bread.

Before we start, the teacher checks the recipe card with the class. Every ingredient must be ready: flour, sugar, eggs, and two ripe bananas.

First, we mix the flour and sugar in a big bowl. Then, one student in each group breaks the eggs, and another student helps stir the mix until it looks ready.

After that, we cut the bananas into small pieces and add them to the bowl. The teacher says bananas make the bread rich, sweet, and full of flavor.

Next, we put the mix into a pan and put the pan into the oven. We must wait about thirty minutes, so we clean the tables while we wait.

When the timer rings, the whole room smells wonderful. The teacher takes the bread out of the oven carefully because it is very hot right after baking.

Finally, every group cuts their banana bread into small pieces to share with classmates. Everyone agrees that food made by your own hands always tastes more delicious.

At the end of class, the teacher reminds us to write down the recipe in our notebooks. She says cooking is a useful skill we can use our whole life.

An Afternoon in the Science Lab

短文
語速：

Last Monday afternoon, our class had a special lesson in the school lab. Our teacher, Mr. Lin, said we would do a fun experiment with a magnet.

First, Mr. Lin gave each group a small magnet, a paper clip, and a bowl of water. He said, "Before you touch anything, put on your safety glasses."

My partner Ken and I put a paper clip into the water. Then we used the magnet under the bowl. Slowly, the clip started to move across the water!

"Look!" Ken said. "The magnet can pull the clip without touching it." We tested the same thing three more times, and the result was always the same.

Next, we mixed a strange liquid in a small glass. It made a small bubble and turned a light blue color. It was not magic — it was just science.

Mr. Lin explained why the liquid turned color. He said scientists use tests like this every day to learn about the world. I think this is really interesting.

Near the end of class, we heard a loud sound. One bowl was on the floor by accident. Mr. Lin stayed calm and said, "That is why we wear safety glasses and clean up carefully."

When class ended, nobody wanted to leave the lab. I talked to my mom about the afternoon experiment at dinner. Mom was happy and said I sounded like a real young scientist.

A Letter About Cleaning Day

信件
語速：

Dear Owen,

How are you? Last Saturday, our class had a big cleaning day, and I want to tell you all about it.

Our teacher gave each group a job. My group cleaned the windows, and Gina's group swept the floor. Nobody wanted to clean the trash can, but Sam did it without a word.

At first, the classroom looked terrible. There was dust on every desk, and the corners were full of old paper. It was hard to believe one small room could get so messy.

We worked for almost two hours. My arm was tired from wiping the windows, but I did not stop because I wanted our room to look bright and clean.

Why did we work so hard? Our teacher said a clean room helps us study well, so everyone tried their best that day.

In the end, the classroom looked great. We took a photo of our team in front of the clean windows. Everyone was proud of the change.

My mom picked me up after school. When she saw my dirty shoes, she just laughed and said, Next time, wear your old ones!

I hope your class has a cleaning day soon, too. It is more fun than you think.

Your tired but happy friend,

Zoe

Why Schools Practice Before Graduation

短文
語速：

In Taiwan, most middle schools have a graduation ceremony in June. But before the real day, every grade nine class has a rehearsal. Why do schools do this?

The answer is simple: a rehearsal helps everyone know what to do. Students walk to the stage, take their diploma, and walk back to their seat, again and again.

At the first rehearsal, many students feel nervous. Some walk too fast, and some forget where to stand. A teacher gives directions in a loud voice from the front.

The music teacher also joins the rehearsal. She chooses the songs for the ceremony and checks the time, so the music starts and stops at the right moment.

During a real rehearsal at one school, a teacher called every student by name, just like on the big day. Students walked the same path three times that afternoon.

One student said the rehearsal made her less scared. Before, she was afraid she would drop her diploma or take the wrong path. Now she was ready.

Teachers say a good rehearsal takes about two hours. After it, most students feel calm because they already know each step. Nobody wants a mistake on the real day.

So the next time you see students walking in lines before June, you will know why: they get ready for a very big day of middle school.

A Night on the School Roof

短文
語速：

Last Friday night, our science teacher took our class to the school roof. She said we could see a meteor shower that night. Everyone was excited, and nobody wanted to stay home.

My friend Kate and I brought a small telescope and two blankets. It was cold on the roof, but the sky was clear, so we could see many stars.

At eight o'clock, the teacher gave each student a cup of hot tea. She said, Look up, and stay quiet. The stars will move fast tonight.

Kate saw the first meteor at eight thirty. She was so happy that she gave a little shout. I was curious, so I asked her where to look.

Why did some stars move faster than others? The teacher said it was because they were near Earth. That answer made the night more interesting.

We stayed on the roof for two hours. I counted eleven meteors, but Kate counted thirteen. We were both terrible at counting fast, so we were not sure.

Before we went home, the teacher took a photo of the whole class under the stars. She said this night would become a special memory for us.

On the bus home, I thought about the sky. I want to bring my little brother to the roof next time, so he can see the stars, too.

A Quiet Corner at Lunchtime

短文
語速：

Every day at lunchtime, Ben likes to read in a quiet corner of the school library. Yesterday, while the other students were eating in the cafeteria, Ben was already looking for a new book.

He found a thick book about animals. It had pictures of a fox, a hen, a bat, and even a king snake. Ben sat down beside the window and started to read.

As soon as he opened the book, he saw a page about frogs and toads. The book said some toads live in deep, wet holes in the ground near ponds.

While Ben was reading about toads, his classmate Wei walked over. "What are you reading?" Wei asked. "A book about animals that live in mud and holes," Ben said.

Wei sat down beside Ben. "I think snakes are scary," Wei said. "They are not so bad," Ben said. "Before I read this book, I thought that too, but now I know more."

The two boys read together until the bell rang. They learned that a butterfly can taste with its feet, and a bat can find its way in the dark without seeing anything.

"This is way more interesting than I thought," Wei said with a laugh. "Can we come back tomorrow?" Ben smiled and said, "Of course. There are so many more animals to learn about."

Finally, the lunch break was over, and the boys walked back to class together. Ben put the animal book back on the shelf, but he already knew he wanted to read it again.

How Bats "See" with Sound

短文
語速：

This month, the school magazine team makes a Nature Corner page about bats. Many people think bats do not see very well, but that is not the whole story.

Bats are nocturnal animals. This means they sleep during the day and come out at night. While most animals are resting, bats are flying around and looking for food.

Most bats eat insects like moths and small bugs. Some bats can eat hundreds of insects in just one hour. This helps farmers because there are fewer bugs on their plants.

Bats do not fly in the dark by luck. They use a skill called echolocation. A bat makes a high sound, and the sound hits things around it, like a tree or a butterfly.

The sound comes back to the big ears of the bat. From this sound, the bat knows how far away something is and which way to fly. It can even tell if the thing moves.

Before scientists learned about echolocation, many people were afraid of bats. Some old stories say a bat is like a rat with wings, or even a bird for the king of the night.

Today, scientists know bats are helpful and gentle animals. As soon as the sun sets, bats leave their home in the mountains and start their busy work in the sky.

So the next time the magazine team sees a bat, they remember one thing: it is not lost in the dark. It uses a special sense that helps it find its way home.

Ken's First Big Role

Ken loves English class, so he joins the school drama club this term. The club is getting ready for a short play, and Ken plays a young farmer in the story.

Every Tuesday after school, the students meet in the music room. Their teacher, Mr. Ho, wants everyone to speak slowly and clearly so people watching can understand every word.

At first, Ken feels nervous. He often forgets his words and speaks too fast. His friend Ivy, who plays a friendly worker in the play, helps him practice again and again.

Ivy says, It takes a lot of practice to remember a whole play. You should not give up. Ken now spends thirty minutes every night reading his part out loud at home.

Two weeks later, Ken begins to feel more confident. He can say his words without looking at the paper. Mr. Ho says his progress is amazing, and the whole club feels proud of him.

On the day of the show, many parents and students come to watch. Ken feels a little scared, but Ivy holds his hand and says, We practice enough. Let us have fun today.

The play goes well, and everyone claps for a long time at the end. Ken smiles and thinks about how much he learns. I hope to get a bigger role next term, he says.

A Morning in the School Kitchen

短文
語速：

Every day, our school kitchen makes lunch for over five hundred students. This week, my class gets a chance to visit the kitchen before lunch and talk to the workers there.

The head worker, Mrs. Lin, tells us that a good school lunch needs three things: fresh food, a clear plan, and enough time. Workers begin their day very early to prepare everything.

First, workers wash and cut vegetables from a nearby farm. Mrs. Lin says fresh food helps students stay healthy and do better in class. She wants every student to eat well.

Next, workers cook rice, soup, and a main dish in big pots. It takes almost three hours to cook lunch for the whole school. The kitchen smells amazing during this time!

Mrs. Lin also talks about food waste. She says students should only take the food they can finish. Workers keep records every day to learn how much food is wasted.

Before we leave, Mrs. Lin gives us a small job: we help carry clean plates to the lunchroom. It is hard work, but everyone feels happy to help.

What an interesting morning! Now I understand why school lunch takes so much work. I want to thank the kitchen workers and finish all my food from now on.

The Nature Club's Show: Hermit Crabs

短文
語速：

Every spring, our school's Nature Club holds a show. This year, the members choose hermit crabs as their topic and share what they learn with everyone.

A hermit crab does not have its own hard shell. It finds an empty shell from the beach and lives inside it for protection.

As the crab grows bigger, the old shell becomes too small. The crab must find a new one and move into it.

The club members explain that hermit crabs often line up and wait. When a bigger crab leaves its shell, a smaller crab can move in fast.

Hermit crabs are not really lonely animals. In fact, many of them live together near the same rocks and share the same beach.

They usually come out at night because it is cooler and safer. During the day, most hermit crabs stay under rocks or sand.

One member says hermit crabs eat almost anything, from small plants to bits of dead fish left on the sand.

At the end of the show, the club puts a few hermit crabs in a glass box so visitors can look closely.

Many students say this show teaches them something new and interesting. Do you want to visit the Nature Club's next show, too?

The Quiet Study Room Before the Test

短文
語速：

Every night before a big test, our school opens a quiet study room for students. Vivian and her friend Oscar go there almost every evening this week.

The room is small, but it is very comfortable. There are lamps on every desk, and the chairs are soft. Vivian likes to sit near the window.

Vivian worries about the math test. She spends two hours a night doing practice questions. Oscar helps her check the hard ones.

"It is not easy to remember all these rules," Vivian says. "But it takes time to get better at math. I can't give up now."

Oscar tells her a trick. "Reading the question twice helps a lot," he says. "It only takes a few seconds, but it saves time later."

A teacher walks around the room every hour. She checks if anyone needs help and brings some water for the students.

Near nine o'clock, most students start to pack up their bags. Vivian still has one more page, so she stays a little longer.

"Don't worry," Oscar says. "Finishing this page tonight is better than doing it tomorrow morning before school."

Vivian finishes the last question and smiles. She thinks the quiet room really helps her focus. Next time, she wants to come here even earlier.

How the Lost and Found Box Works

短文
語速：

Every school has a lost and found box, and ours is no different. It sits near the front office, and it is full of many different things.

Students often lose small items like pencil cases, water bottles, and even shoes. When someone finds a lost item on campus, they usually put it in this box.

Every Friday afternoon, a teacher checks the box and writes down what is inside. Then the office puts a short list on the notice board near the stairs.

If your name is not on any label, you can still look through the box yourself. Just ask the office staff first, and they will let you check it.

Some items stay in the box for a long time because nobody claims them. After one month, the school gives these items to a charity in the city.

Teachers often remind students to write their names on their things. That way, if something gets lost, it can go back to the right person quickly.

Last month, a student found a special watch and put it in the box. The owner was so happy when she got it back the next day.

The lost and found box may seem small, but it helps our school stay organized and kind to one another.

The New Running Track

短文
語速：

Last week, workers came to fix our old track. For two weeks, we could not use it at all. Everyone was a little sad, because we love to run there every morning.

During gym class, we watched the workers from the classroom window. They pulled up the old rubber and put down new rubber. It was really interesting to watch.

One day, our gym teacher said the new track would have bright blue lines and a special surface for jumping. We could not wait to try it.

Finally, the workers finished the job. Our teacher let the whole class walk on the new track before anyone ran on it. The surface was soft and bouncy under our shoes.

The next morning, we had our first real practice on the new track. Everyone ran faster than before, and a few students set new personal records.

My good friend Leo said the new track made him feel like a real athlete. I agree with him.

Now, every morning before class starts, more students come early just to run a few times around it. Our school feels more alive than ever.

I am really glad the school fixed the old track. Next year, I hope we can have a fun race on it.
