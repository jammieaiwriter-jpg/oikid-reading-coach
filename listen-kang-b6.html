Why Dodgeball Is a School Favorite

短文
語速：

Every fall, many schools hold a dodgeball tournament during P.E. class. Students often say it is their favorite sport of the whole year, and it is easy to see why.

The rules of dodgeball are simple. Two teams stand on opposite sides of a court, and players try to hit players on the other team with a soft ball.

If a player gets hit, that player must leave the court. However, if a player catches a ball before it touches the ground, the player who threw it must leave instead.

A team wins the round when every player on the other team is out. Because the rules are easy to learn, even new students can join a team on their first day.

Dodgeball also teaches useful skills. Players must move quickly, watch the ball closely, and work together as a team. A player who only thinks about attacking often loses fast.

Teachers like dodgeball because it keeps the whole class active at the same time. Unlike some sports, nobody has to sit and wait a long time for a turn.

Although the game looks simple, players need good teamwork to win. A team with players who share the ball and protect weaker teammates usually beats a team that does not.

For these reasons, dodgeball tournaments remain popular in many schools. The mix of speed, simple rules, and teamwork makes it exciting for players and fun to watch for everyone else.

A Tour of the School Lunch Kitchen

對話（多聲線）
語速：

Coco: Wow, this kitchen is huge! I did not know our school lunch was cooked in such a big room.

Chef Wang: Yes, we cook lunch for over eight hundred students every day, so we need a lot of space and many large pots.

Tom: What time do you start cooking in the morning?

Chef Wang: We start at six o'clock, before most students even arrive at school. By eleven, everything is ready to serve.

Coco: How do you decide what to cook each week?

Chef Wang: A nutritionist helps us plan a menu with vegetables, protein, and rice every day, so students get a balanced meal.

Tom: Do you ever cook something students do not like?

Chef Wang: Sometimes! If many students leave a certain vegetable on their trays, we try a new recipe for it next month.

Coco: That machine over there looks interesting. What does it do?

Chef Wang: That machine washes the vegetables quickly and safely before we cook them. It saves us a lot of time.

Tom: Thank you for showing us around, Chef Wang. I will never look at lunch the same way again!

Chef Wang: You are welcome! Remember to thank the kitchen staff next time you enjoy a warm lunch.

Ben's First Day at Swim Class

短文
語速：

Ben joined a new swim class this summer. Before the first class, he was a little worried because he could only walk a few steps in shallow water.

When he got to the pool, the coach said hello to every student with a big smile. She said, 'Don't worry about small mistakes on the first day.'

First, the coach taught the students how to put their faces in the water the right way. Ben did this many times, and he drank some water before he finally got it right.

Next, the coach showed everyone how to kick their legs while holding the side of the pool. Ben's legs were tired, but he did not stop until his kicks were easy.

After thirty minutes, the coach asked Ben to swim across the pool for the first time. He was afraid, but he did not wait, and he started to swim, one kick at a time.

Halfway across, Ben almost gave up, but he remembered what the coach said: stay calm and keep kicking. As soon as he reached the other side, everyone was happy for him.

Although Ben was very tired after class, he was proud of himself. He could not swim well before, but now he trusted that he could get better every single week.

On the way home, Ben said to his mother that class was harder than he expected, but he was excited to come back next Saturday for his second class.

The Shy Kitten Behind the Gym

短文
語速：

Behind the school gym, there is a quiet corner with a few benches and a small garden. Every afternoon, a group of stray cats gathers there to rest in a cool, quiet spot.

Last spring, a shy new student called Wu Ting joined the animal care club. She was like a stranger at first, but animals always made her happy, so she wanted to help the cats near the gym.

The club members feed the cats every morning before class. They bring bowls of food and clean water, and they keep the feeding area tidy so insects will not come near.

One of the cats, a small gray kitten, was too shy to come close to anyone. The other members said this kitten needed a gentle, patient friend.

Every day, Wu Ting waited near the same spot and stayed silent. She did not try to touch the kitten. She only talked in a low, gentle voice, even when she was tired.

After two weeks, the kitten walked nearer and nearer. One morning, it finally came right up to Wu Ting and let her touch its gray coat for the first time.

The whole club was full of joy that day. They put a story about the kitten on the notice board of the club so other students could read about a stray cat that learned to trust again.

Now, this small, curious kitten follows Wu Ting around the campus after school. Wu Ting says she learned that staying patient, not fast, wins the trust of a stray animal.

A Letter About Our Club Showcase

信件
語速：

Dear Uncle Wei,

I have some exciting news! Last Friday, our school had a club showcase, and my scavenger hunt club finally got to share our hobby with the whole school.

Our club made a small table near the gym with photos of past hunts and a short list of clue ideas from our most popular game. Many classmates came to look and ask questions.

I was shy before the showcase, because I am usually quiet in front of strangers. But once I started to talk about our club, that shy feeling went away soon.

A teacher from another class said our club looked widely open to new members, and neither she nor her students had joined a club like ours before.

After the showcase, five new students signed up to join. Our club leader said this was the largest number of new members we have ever had in one day.

I also talked with a new student who just started at school last month and still felt like a stranger. I gave her some club information, and she was full of joy.

That evening, I helped keep our club room tidy and put everything back in its place. I was proud that our small hobby could bring so many people together.

I hope you can visit our next scavenger hunt sometime. I will send you some photos from the showcase very soon.

Love,

Ray

The First Day of a New Semester

短文
語速：

In Taiwan, the first day of a new semester follows a schedule that most students already know well. Students arrive at school a little earlier than usual, carrying new textbooks and clean uniforms.

At the school gate, teachers stand and greet each classmate with a smile. Some students look excited, while others feel a little nervous about a new term with new subjects.

The morning usually begins with a short gathering outside. The principal gives a welcome speech and reminds every student about basic school rules for the new term.

After the gathering, students walk to their homeroom in a line. Each class picks a new seat chart, and the homeroom teacher hands out a list of textbooks for every subject.

Cleaning time comes next. Each student has a small job: some sweep the floor, some wipe the windows, and some arrange the books on the shelf near the door.

Many students also choose a class job for the semester, such as caring for the plants or checking the board before each lesson. This helps every classmate feel part of a team.

By the end of the first day, the classroom looks clean and ready for study. Teachers often say a tidy classroom helps students start the new semester with a calm mind.

Although the first day can feel long, most students agree it helps them get to know a new schedule, meet new classmates, and prepare for a busy semester ahead.

The Foxes Next Door

短文
語速：

Behind our classroom, there is a big bulletin board. Every month, a different class puts up a new topic there. This month, the topic is about foxes that live in cities.

Many people think that foxes only live in forests or on mountains. But the board says that more and more foxes now live in cities all over the world, next to houses and roads.

Why do foxes move into cities? The board gives one main reason: cities have plenty of food. People throw away food, and foxes are smart enough to find it at night.

It is not easy for a fox to live near so many people and cars, but foxes know how to hide well. During the day, they often sleep in quiet places, like under a small building or behind a fence.

The board also shows some numbers. In some big cities, there can be hundreds of foxes that live close together. Most people never even see one, because foxes usually come out at night.

Some people worry that city foxes are dangerous, but the board explains that this is not true for most foxes. They usually run away from people instead of coming near.

The board asks an interesting question for us: should cities try to help these animals, or should cities try to keep them away? Many students stop to read this question and think about it.

Next month, a new class will change the topic on the board. But this month, walking past the fox topic makes many of us stop, read, and learn something new about the animals near our own city.

Sorting the Lost and Found Box

Mia: Look at this. The Lost and Found box is full again this week.

Ryan: I know. There is a comb, a belt, and even a small piano book inside.

Mia: A piano book? That is strange. Whose piano book is it?

Ryan: I have no idea. Maybe a student left it after music class.

Mia: There is also an envelope with three old stamps on it. It does not have a name.

Ryan: People forget so many small things. I think it is human nature to lose small things sometimes.

Mia: True, but it is sad that a ring or a postcard just sits here for weeks.

Ryan: Ms. Chen said that she wants every student to check the box this Friday.

Mia: That is a good plan. Hopefully, some of these things will find their owners.

Ryan: I will tell my classmates to look through it after school today.

Mia: Good idea. I will put a note on the board so more people know.

Ryan: Do you want to help me put the box into groups, like clothes and school items?

Mia: Sure! I think we can finish grouping it before the bell rings for the next class.

Ryan: Great. Hopefully, this box will be almost empty by next Monday.

The Missing Button

短文
語速：

Ken looks down at his uniform jacket and his face turns pale. A small button near the top is not there anymore. In ten minutes, the whole class will line up for the school photo, and his jacket will not close right.

He looks under his desk and inside his bag, but he can not find the button. To lose a button on picture day makes the morning very hard, and Ken does not know what to do.

Ivy sits next to him. She sees that his hands are not still, and she asks him what is wrong. He explains the problem fast. She says that she always carries a small sewing bag with her, just in case.

It is lucky for Ken that Ivy is always ready for anything. She takes out a needle, some thread, and one small button that almost matches his jacket. This should work, she says with a smile.

To put a button on in ten minutes is not easy, but Ivy has done this many times at home. Her fingers move fast, and she finishes before the teacher even calls the class to line up.

Ken thanks her again and again. He wants to bring his own sewing bag from now on, so this problem will not happen again. Ivy laughs and says that this is a smart plan.

When the man with the camera counts down, Ken stands tall in the front row. His jacket looks perfect, all the way to the top, and nobody can tell that anything went wrong.

Later, Ken looks down at his uniform jacket again, this time with a proud smile instead of worry. The small button that Ivy put on now feels like the strongest button in the whole school.

A Letter About My Uncle's Hardware Store

信件
語速：

Dear Wei-Ting,

How are you? I am writing to tell you about my weekend job at my uncle's hardware store on the corner near our school.

The store is small, but it sells almost everything: nails, tools, ropes, light bulbs, and even garden seeds. My uncle has run this shop for over twenty years.

On Saturdays, I help him arrange the shelves and answer simple questions from customers. Last week, an old man asked me to help him find the right size of screws for his chair.

At first, I felt shy talking to strangers, but now I actually enjoy it. My uncle always tells me that a good shopkeeper listens carefully before giving any advice.

Sometimes, when the shop is quiet, my uncle teaches me how different tools work. I learned how to use a small saw and how to fix a squeaky door.

He also lets me keep a small notebook to write down which items sell fast, like flashlights before typhoon season. It feels like learning real business skills.

Next month, my uncle plans to give me a small prize if I can learn to use the cash register by myself. I am excited and a little nervous.

Please write back and tell me about your summer plans. Maybe you can visit the shop when you come to my town!

Your cousin,

Ming-Hao

Inside Our Gardening Club's Greenhouse

短文
語速：

Behind the school gym, there is a small greenhouse that belongs to the gardening club. Every Tuesday afternoon, about twenty members meet there to plant seeds and take care of young plants.

The greenhouse has a glass roof, so sunlight can reach every plant inside. In winter, the glass keeps the cold wind out, and in summer, big fans keep the air from getting too hot.

Members of the club grow many kinds of plants, from small flowers like roses to useful vegetables like tomatoes and green onions. Each plant has a small name tag with its planting date.

New members first learn how to water plants the right way. Too much water can kill the roots, but too little water makes the leaves turn yellow and dry.

Every October, the club holds a small flower show in the school square. Visitors can see colorful flowers and buy a few small plants to grow at home.

The club also keeps a notebook full of notes about each plant's growth. This information helps new members learn from the mistakes and successes of students from earlier years.

The gardening teacher says the greenhouse teaches more than plant science. Students learn patience, because a seed does not turn into a flower overnight; it takes weeks of careful, steady work.

The Crossing Guard at Our School Gate

短文
語速：

Every morning at seven, Mr. Liu stands at our school gate in a bright yellow vest. He is not a teacher, but he has helped students cross the street in a safe way for almost ten years.

Mr. Liu worked at a small factory near the train station before he retired. He was a little bored at home after that, so he wanted to help at our school gate instead.

Rain or shine, Mr. Liu is always there. On hot days, he wears a wide hat. On rainy days, he holds a big umbrella and tells cars to stop with his orange flag.

One winter morning, a boy ran into the street without looking, right in front of a bus. Mr. Liu quickly took his bag and pulled him back to a safe place.

After that morning, many parents thanked Mr. Liu and gave him warm drinks. He said it was just his job, but everyone was very glad that he helped the boy that day.

Last month, our school gave Mr. Liu a special prize during the morning assembly. Students clapped for almost a minute, and some of them started to cry.

Mr. Liu says he will keep standing at the gate as long as his legs allow him. To him, watching hundreds of students walk into school in a safe way every morning is the part of his day that he likes the most.
