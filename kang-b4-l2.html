全文（點任一字查中文意思）

Last weekend, my family cleaned out my grandmother's old apartment. She is moving to a smaller place, so we had to sort every drawer and box. My mother said, "We should decide what is useful and what we must throw away."

In the first drawer, I found an old radio and a small tape recorder. The radio still made a soft sound, but the tape recorder was broken. My grandmother said these were more valuable to her than any new phone.

"You can't just throw away my memories," she said with a smile. "That old radio is not the most expensive thing here, but it might be the most important one." I could see tears in her eyes.

We also found a box of candles and an old lamp. The lamp was slower to turn on than my phone's light, but its yellow light felt warmer and sweeter somehow. My grandmother said it was her favorite lamp.

My little cousin pulled open the last drawer and pushed a strange button on an old machine. It made a strange, loud sound! We all laughed at the funny joke my grandmother told about that old washing machine.

"This old software will not work on new computers anymore," my father said, holding an old file box. "Should we keep it, or should we finally let it go?" My grandmother thought for a moment before she answered.

"Actually, you can throw that one away," she said. "But the radio and the lamp must stay with me. They are the most precious things in this poor old apartment, more precious than anything new."

By the end of the day, we filled ten boxes, but we kept the radio, the lamp, and one small candle. My grandmother said this afternoon became one of her sweetest memories. We should visit her more often now.

區塊 6 / 6　⑥ GEPT 延伸閱讀：A Garage Sale in the Park講義 原創短文（GEPT 初級字彙範圍）
【挑戰閱讀】約 300 字原創短文，字彙在英檢初級 2000 字內。流程刻意這樣排：①直接把整篇文章從頭讀到尾——會遇到幾個沒學過的字，用上下文猜，不要停（考試就是這樣）→ ②做理解題驗證有沒有讀懂（答案都在文中，用讀的找，不要猜選項）→ ③生字補充，確認剛才猜的意思 → ④最後一段一段朗讀收尾。
先把整篇文章讀完（用眼睛讀，還不用唸）

Every spring, people in my town hold a garage sale in the park. Families bring old things from their apartments and sell them cheap. I always go early with my father to find useful treasures.

This year, an old man had a table full of interesting items: a small radio, a broken lamp, and a drawer full of old photos. He said everything on the table was once valuable to his family.

"This radio still works," he told us. "It sounds a little slow, but you could fix it." My father picked it up and listened. The sound was soft and warm, like music from long ago.

Next to the radio was a strange record with no name on it. The old man laughed and told a joke about how he does not remember the song. "It must be a surprise gift," he said.

My father asked, "How much for the radio and the record?" The old man said, "You can have them both for a sweet price, just one hundred dollars." My father thought it was fair.

A young woman next to us looked at a small candle and a small box. She said the box smells sweet, like flowers. "This could be more valuable than it looks," she said quietly.

Near the end of the table, a poor little boy wanted a toy car, but he did not have enough money. The old man said, "You don't have to pay. Just take it."

The boy's face turned bright with joy. He said "thank you" many times and ran to show his mother. Everyone smiled at the kind old man and his generous heart.

Before we left, my father paid for the radio and the record. He said this garage sale was more interesting than any store. Old, simple things can become the most special treasures of all.

Every spring, people in my town hold a garage sale in the park. Families bring old things from their apartments and sell them cheap. I always go early with my father to find useful treasures.
唸 2
This year, an old man had a table full of interesting items: a small radio, a broken lamp, and a drawer full of old photos. He said everything on the table was once valuable to his family.
唸 3
"This radio still works," he told us. "It sounds a little slow, but you could fix it." My father picked it up and listened. The sound was soft and warm, like music from long ago.
唸 4
Next to the radio was a strange record with no name on it. The old man laughed and told a joke about how he does not remember the song. "It must be a surprise gift," he said.
唸 5
My father asked, "How much for the radio and the record?" The old man said, "You can have them both for a sweet price, just one hundred dollars." My father thought it was fair.
唸 6
A young woman next to us looked at a small candle and a small box. She said the box smells sweet, like flowers. "This could be more valuable than it looks," she said quietly.
唸 7
Near the end of the table, a poor little boy wanted a toy car, but he did not have enough money. The old man said, "You don't have to pay. Just take it."
唸 8
The boy's face turned bright with joy. He said "thank you" many times and ran to show his mother. Everyone smiled at the kind old man and his generous heart.
唸 9
Before we left, my father paid for the radio and the record. He said this garage sale was more interesting than any store. Old, simple things can become the most special treasures of all.
