區塊 1 / 6　① 課文朗讀：Lucky Coins Around the World（原創）講義 原創課次對應文本（呼應本課單字：coin／Greece／luck）
原創課次文本，用本課單字寫成（課本課文請看課本跟著念）。一次念一整段，念完做理解題——題目從這篇文字就能作答。主題是世界各地跟硬幣有關的幸運傳統，包括希臘新年蛋糕藏硬幣的習俗。
全文（點任一字查中文意思）

Many people around the world believe that some objects can bring good luck. A coin is one of the most common lucky objects, and people carry them, give them as gifts, or keep them at home.

In Greece, a coin has a special place in New Year traditions. Families bake a special cake called vasilopita, and they hide one coin inside before baking it.

On New Year's Day, every family member gets a piece of the cake. The person who finds the hidden coin in their piece is said to have good luck for the whole year.

This tradition is very old, and many families in Greece still follow it today. Some families even put a coin under a young plant, hoping it will bring luck to a new business.

Coins are lucky in other places too. In some countries, people throw a coin into a fountain and make a wish. They believe the coin will help their wish come true.

A found coin can also mean luck. Many people say that finding a coin on the ground, especially heads up, means something good will happen that day.

Of course, not everyone believes a coin can really change their luck. Still, these small traditions bring families together and give people something fun to hope for.

Whether the coin is baked in a cake, thrown in a fountain, or found on the street, it reminds people that luck is often more about celebration than magic.

先把整篇文章讀完（用眼睛讀，還不用唸）

Every year on Dragon Boat Festival, Wei-Ting's town holds a fun contest at noon. People bring an egg and try to make it stand straight up on the ground without falling over.

Many people believe an egg can only stand up at noon on this one special day. Wei-Ting is not sure if that is true, but she still enjoys the game every year with her neighbors.

This year, Wei-Ting picks a clean egg from the kitchen. She sits down on the sidewalk and places the egg on a flat stone, using her fingers to hold it a little at a time.

Her little brother tries too, but his egg keeps rolling over. He gets a little angry and wants to give up, so Wei-Ting tells him to slow down and try again.

After a while, Wei-Ting's egg finally stands still. She takes her hands away slowly, and the egg does not fall. Her neighbors clap and cheer for her small win.

An old man nearby explains that the trick is not really about noon. He says a flat stone and calm hands matter more than the exact time of day.

Wei-Ting's brother tries one more time, following her advice. His egg moves side to side, but then it stands too. The two of them smile and take a photo next to their standing eggs.

Before they go home, Wei-Ting's mother gives everyone a rice dumpling. Wei-Ting hopes she can join this same contest again with her family next year.
