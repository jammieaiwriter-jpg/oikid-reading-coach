A Saturday at the Fish Stall

短文
語速：

Every Saturday morning, I help my grandfather at his fish stall in the old market. He wakes up before the sun and drives his small truck full of fresh fish. I ride with him and carry the boxes.

At the market, many sellers set up their stalls before six o'clock. My grandfather washes the fish and puts ice on top of them. The fish stay cold and fresh all morning, and customers like fresh fish the most.

A woman comes and asks, "How much is this big fish?" My grandfather says, "It is two hundred dollars." She looks at a small fish and asks the price too. My grandfather always answers with a big smile.

Sometimes customers ask my grandfather to clean the fish for them. He is very fast with his knife. I watch him work, and I learn a new skill every week. It is not an easy job, but he really loves it.

By nine o'clock, most of the fish are sold. My grandfather gives me a fish to bring home for lunch. My grandmother always cooks it with green onion, and it tastes wonderful every single time.

I used to think this job was boring, but now I understand it better. My grandfather works hard every day to give people good food. Next Saturday, I want to help him again and learn more about his work.

The Soy Milk Shop in the Morning

短文
語速：

In many towns in Taiwan, there is a small soy milk shop near the train station. It opens at five in the morning and closes before noon. Workers and students often stop there before they go to school or work.

The shop makes hot soy milk and cold soy milk every day. Some people like hot soy milk with a little sugar. Other people like cold soy milk because it is quick to drink on a busy morning.

Besides soy milk, the shop also sells other food. You can order some fried bread, a sesame bread, or a warm egg pancake. Many people order two kinds of food and eat them together at a small table.

The shop owner, Mr. Chen, wakes up at three in the morning. He needs a long time to cook the soy milk, so it tastes rich and really good. He says a good soy milk shop needs patience, not just speed.

This kind of shop is a part of local culture. Grandparents, parents, and children all visit these shops. For many families, breakfast at a soy milk shop is a simple but happy way to start a new day.

New Glasses for the Ball Game

短文
語速：

Last week, I did not see the soccer ball well during practice. My coach talked to my mom about it. On Saturday, my mom took me to the glasses shop near our house.

The shop owner was a kind old man. He asked me to sit down and look at a big board on the wall. The letters were big at the top and small at the bottom, in many rows.

I read the big letters at the top with no problem. But when I got to the small letters at the bottom, I did not read them well. The man put some numbers on a piece of paper.

Then I needed to choose new glasses. There were many frames on the shelf. Some were big, some were small, some were round, and some were square. I tried five or six pairs in front of the mirror.

My mom liked a black pair, but I liked a blue pair. The blue pair was light, and it fit my face well. I asked the man, "How much does this pair cost?" He said the price to my mom.

The man said it takes about one hour to make new glasses, so we walked to a small shop nearby and bought some snacks. When we came back, my glasses were ready on the table.

I put on my new glasses right away. I looked out the window, and I saw every leaf on the tree. Even the small words on a sign across the street looked clear.

The next day, I put on my new glasses and went to soccer practice. I saw the ball very well, and I did not miss it. My coach said I looked like a real player.

How a Photo Shop Prints Pictures

短文
語速：

Many years ago, cameras did not show pictures right away. People used film cameras, and they did not see the photo on the camera. They needed to visit a photo shop to find out how the picture looked.

At the photo shop, a worker takes the film out of the camera in a dark room. Light can ruin the film, so the room has no windows. The worker works with care and works fast.

The worker puts the film into a special machine. The machine uses chemicals and water to change the film into real pictures. This part of the work takes some time and needs a dark room.

After that, the worker makes each photo on paper. Different pictures need different sizes, so customers choose big or small photos. Some people want ten copies of one photo for their family.

Long ago, people waited about one week for their photos. Now, some photo shops use new machines, and photos are ready in just one hour. Many students still visit photo shops for school photos.

Every photo shop also has a wall full of sample pictures. Families, students, and pets are all in the pictures. The wall shows the shop's best work to new customers.

Today, most people use their phones to take pictures. They often keep photos only on their phones, not on paper. Still, some special photos, like family photos or birthday photos, go to a photo shop.

A photo on paper can last for many years. When people look at an old photo, it can bring back happy memories of a special day. That is why the photo shop is still important.

Dear Emily,

How are you? I am writing to tell you about my visit to Uncle Ray's shop last weekend.

Uncle Ray fixes water pipes and lights for people in our town. His shop is small, but it has many tools on the wall.

After that, a man came with a broken water pipe. Water came onto the floor, and the man started to worry. Uncle Ray turned off the water and changed the old pipe for a new one.

At lunch, Uncle Ray let me help him clean the tools. It was fun, but my hands were dirty by the end!

Uncle Ray says he wants to teach me more next time I visit. Do you want to come with me and see his shop too?

Write back soon!

Herbal Tea Stalls in Taiwan

短文
語速：

In many old streets in Taiwan, people can find small herbal tea stalls. These stalls sell a cold, dark drink called herbal tea. It is made from several kinds of plants and leaves.

Workers boil the plants in big pots for a long time. Then they let the tea cool down before they put it into large glass bottles. Many stalls keep the bottles near the front so customers can see the tea right away.

Herbal tea is popular in summer because it helps people feel cool. Many workers and students stop by a stall on their way home and buy a cup after a hot afternoon.

Some stalls also sell herbal tea in small bottles or bags so customers can take it home. A few stalls open very early and close late at night, so people can buy tea almost any time.

Most stalls use a large sign with the price on it. A small cup usually costs less than a big cup. Some stalls also offer a free refill if a customer finishes the first cup fast.

Many of these stalls have been in the same family for a long time. Grandparents teach their children how to choose good plants and how long to boil them.

Today, some young people open new herbal tea shops near schools and offices. However, many customers say the old street stalls still make very good herbal tea.

The Rice Delivery on Sunday Morning

短文
語速：

Every Sunday morning, Chen delivers rice bags to small shops near the old market. Last Sunday, he was walking down a narrow street when he saw a long line outside a rice shop.

He parked near the door and took two heavy bags inside. The shop owner, Tsai, was talking with a customer about the price of rice. "How many bags do you need this week?" Chen asked. "I need six bags, please," Tsai answered.

While Chen was counting the bags, he saw something strange. A drawer under the counter was open, and some old photos were inside. "Whose photos are these?" he asked. Tsai said they belonged to her father, from many years ago.

Chen learned that Tsai's father ran this same shop for many years. He worked here every day, and he never left the shop before it got dark. He also had a small pet cat. The cat always sleeps near the rice bags.

After Chen finished his work, Tsai gave him a cup of tea. "Where do you go next?" she asked. "I go to a shop two streets away," he said. "The owner there is waiting for ten more bags."

Before he left, Chen helped Tsai move a few empty boxes to the back room. He also cleaned an old sign above the door. It said the shop opened many years ago.

Tsai said thank you and stayed by the door. Chen got into his truck, and he went past the market toward his next stop.

A Letter About the Locksmith

信件
語速：

Dear Grandma,

How are you? I want to tell you about something funny that happened yesterday after school.

When I got home, I could not find my house key. I looked in my bag three times, but it was not there. I started to worry because nobody was home.

I called Dad, but he was still at his office and could not come right away. So he called a locksmith near our street to help me.

The locksmith came in twenty minutes. He was an ordinary-looking man with a big box of tools. "Do not worry," he said. "I can open this door for you in a few minutes."

He looked at the lock carefully and picked a thin metal tool from his box. While he worked on the lock, he talked about his job. "I am a locksmith because I enjoy fixing small problems," he said.

Why did the door open so fast? Because the locksmith has many years of practice, so a lock like ours is easy for him.

After the door opened, Dad gave the locksmith some money and thanked him again and again. The locksmith laughed and said it was just an ordinary day at work for him.

I learned that being a locksmith is a more interesting job than I thought. Next time, I promise to keep my key safe!

Love always,

Wei-Ting

A Corner in an Old Bookstore

短文
語速：

There is a small, old bookstore near my house. My grandpa's friend, Mr. Lin, owns it. Every weekend, I visit the shop and sit in a quiet corner with old books about interesting careers.

Mr. Lin was a reporter before he opened this bookstore. He worked for a small news channel in another country for ten years. Then he decided to come home and start something new.

"Was it hard to become a reporter?" I ask him one day. He smiles and says, "Yes, but I always believed I could do it. A good reporter needs to catch every important story fast."

On the shelf near the window, there is a book about ordinary people with special jobs: a farmer, a fisherman, a mail carrier, and a truck driver. Mr. Lin says every job has its own value.

I tell him I hope to become a lawyer someday, but sometimes I worry I am not smart enough. Mr. Lin says, "Believing in your dream is the first step. The rest is hard work."

He also tells me about his old boss at the news channel, a woman who once worked as a secretary before she became successful in her career. "She never stopped learning," he says.

Before I leave, Mr. Lin gives me an old book for free. "This is for your future," he says. "Read it, and think about your own plan." I thank him and promise to come back soon.

On my way home, I keep thinking about jobs: lawyer, reporter, farmer, soldier, dentist. I do not have the right answer yet, but I have a new idea: to visit more people and ask about their careers.

The Team Behind a Record Shop

短文
語速：

A record shop sells old and new records, and running one is more than just selling music. Behind every record shop, there is a small team with different jobs to do every day.

The shop boss decides which records to buy and sell. Every week, new goods arrive from different countries, and the boss must catch the newest music trends before other shops do.

Some workers help customers find the right record. Old customers often ask for classic songs, while younger customers want new artists who just started their careers.

Once a month, the shop invites a local singer to perform in the store. Before the show, a worker interviews the singer for the shop's small radio channel, so more people can hear about the event.

Behind the counter, another worker checks orders and makes sure records are ready to deliver to nearby record shops in other cities. This job needs careful hands and a good memory.

Running a successful record shop is not easy. The team must work together, listen to customers, and keep learning about new music every single day.

To become part of this team, a worker does not need a music degree. What matters most is a real love for records and a strong idea about what makes good music.

So the next time someone visits a record shop, they can remember: there is a whole team of ordinary people working hard behind those shelves of records.

The Long Wait at the Dentist

短文
語速：

Last Friday afternoon, Hank took his little sister Gina to the dentist near the small park. Gina had a bad tooth, and she did not want to go inside. Hank took her hand and opened the door for her.

The waiting room was quiet. Six people were on the long chairs by the window. A small boy was playing with a toy car on the ground, and the sound of its wheels filled the whole room.

Gina looked at the door and asked, Does it hurt? Hank did not want to lie to her. A little, he said, but it is over fast. After that, Mom buys us bread on the way home.

While they were waiting, a nurse gave Gina a picture book about teeth. Gina turned the pages one by one. Her hands were still cold, but she was not crying any more, and she asked about every picture.

As soon as the bell on the front desk made a sound, the nurse called Gina's name. Gina got up and looked at her brother. Hank gave her a big smile and put his lucky coin in her hand.

Ten minutes later, Gina came out with a happy face. The dentist fixed the small hole in her tooth, and it did not hurt much. That was easy, she said.

On the bus home, Gina talked about the picture book, not about her tooth. Hank asked for his lucky coin back, but Gina said the coin was hers now.

The Little Bandage on the Pharmacy Shelf

短文
語速：

Every day, people come into our pharmacy for one small thing: a box of bandage strips. It is only a tiny piece of tape and paper, but it does a big job for your body.

A bandage has three parts. The two ends are sticky, so they hold on to your skin. The part in the middle is not sticky, because it sits right on the cut and must come off later without pain.

Why do we cover a cut at all? The air around us is full of germs. A cut is an open door into your body. The part in the middle keeps that door shut and clean.

The size matters, too. A tiny cut on your finger needs a small one. A long cut on your leg needs a wide one. That is why our shelf has boxes in five different sizes.

Some people ask for the kind that can stay on in water. Those strips have a thin cover on the outside. Water runs off them, so they are good for a day at the pool or for washing dishes.

One rule is always the same. Wash the cut with clean water first, and put the bandage on dry skin. If you put one on wet skin, it comes off in a few minutes, and you lose your money.

Next time you get a cut, do you want to take care of it well, too? Ask for the right size, keep the skin dry, and change the bandage every day.

How the Old Grain Shop Sells Rice

短文
語速：

In the old part of town, there is a small grain shop. It sells rice, beans, and other dry food. The shop is more than fifty years old, and many families still shop there today.

Most big stores sell rice in small plastic bags with a fixed price. The old grain shop is different. A customer can buy just the amount he or she needs, even a small cup of rice.

The shop owner, Mr. Lin, uses a big metal scale to weigh the rice. He puts the rice on one side and small metal weights on the other side until the two sides are equal.

This kind of scale is very old, but it is still correct today. Mr. Lin says a good scale never lies about the weight, so customers always trust the number they see.

After he uses the scale to weigh the rice, Mr. Lin tells the customer the price. The price depends on the weight and the kind of rice. Some kinds of rice cost more because they take longer to grow.

Mr. Lin says most young people do not know how to use this kind of scale today. They are used to a modern digital scale that shows the number right away on a small screen.

Even so, some young customers still like to visit the old shop. They say it feels special to watch Mr. Lin work, and the rice always tastes fresh and good.

Mr. Lin hopes his shop can stay open for many more years. He believes that this old way of selling rice is part of the history of this town, not just a way to make money.

A Busy Morning at Uncle Kevin's Stationery Shop

短文
語速：

Every summer, I help my uncle Kevin at his stationery wholesale shop. He does not sell to just one student at a time; he sells boxes of pens and notebooks to small shops around the city.

This morning, a shop owner came in early. She wanted one hundred notebooks and two hundred pens for her small store. My uncle checked his list and told her the total price.

"These pens are cheap when you buy many," my uncle said. "But if you only need ten, the price is higher." The shop owner laughed and said that made sense to her.

My job was to count boxes and carry them to the front door. Some boxes were heavy, so I asked my cousin to help me. We worked fast because more customers were going to arrive soon.

At noon, a school teacher visited. She needed pencils and erasers for a whole classroom. My uncle gave her a special price because she buys from him every year.

I saw something interesting. My uncle never spends time guessing prices. He always checks his book first, then tells the customer the correct total for everything.

In the afternoon, the shop got quiet. My uncle let me try to write prices on a new list. I made a few mistakes, but he helped me fix them and did not get angry.

Before we finished for the day, my uncle gave me a new notebook as a small gift. He said I did a good job today and that I could come back and help again next weekend.

I felt proud on my way home. I learned that a shop is not easy to run, but it can also be fun. What a great day this was!

Waiting in Line for Braised Snacks

短文
語速：

Every Friday after school, Ren and his friend Toby walk to a small braised food stall near their school. Many students already stand in a long line there, so they know the snacks are popular.

The stall owner, Mr. Hsu, cooks noodles, eggs, tofu, and vegetables in a big pot of brown sauce. The smell is strong and delicious, and it makes Ren and Toby very hungry while they wait.

Ren picks two eggs, some noodles, and a piece of tofu. Toby wants more vegetables, so he chooses carrots, mushrooms, and cabbage. They put their food into a basket with a number tag.

While they wait, Ren and Toby talk about their homework. Toby asks Ren why he looks tired. Ren says he stayed up late because he had a big math test the next morning.

After about ten minutes, Mr. Hsu calls their number. He cuts their food into small pieces and puts it into two bowls. The food is still hot, and it smells amazing.

Ren and Toby pay for their food and find a small table outside the stall. They eat quickly because they are so hungry, and they both agree the noodles are the best part.

Before they leave, Toby asks Mr. Hsu a question: why is this stall always so crowded? Mr. Hsu smiles and says it is because he uses fresh food every single day.

On their way home, Ren and Toby talk about coming back next Friday. They both like the stall so much that they want to bring more friends with them next time.

What Is a Taiwanese Ice Fruit Shop?

短文
語速：

In Taiwan, an ice fruit shop is a small shop that sells cold desserts made with shaved ice, fresh fruit, and sweet syrup. These shops are popular in summer, especially on hot afternoons.

Most ice fruit shops offer many toppings. Customers can choose fruit like mango, strawberry, or banana. They can also add red beans, taro balls, or sweet milk on top of the shaved ice.

Most ice fruit shops are small family businesses. The owner often makes the syrup and prepares the fruit every morning before the shop opens. Many shops have been open for more than twenty years.

Students often visit an ice fruit shop after school or on weekends. It is a cheap and good way to cool down on a hot day. A bowl of shaved ice usually costs less than one hundred dollars.

Some ice fruit shops also make fresh fruit juice or milk tea. People who do not want shaved ice can choose a cold drink. The shop stays busy from morning until night in summer.

Ice fruit shops are different from Western ice cream shops. The ice is light and fluffy, not hard like ice cream. Many people say this kind of ice tastes lighter and more refreshing.

Today, some old ice fruit shops become famous on the internet. Visitors travel from other cities just to try a bowl of shaved ice with many colorful toppings. The shops keep their simple style, but more people know about them now.
